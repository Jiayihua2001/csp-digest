Data-efficient machine learning for molecular crystal structure prediction
Simon Wengert, Gábor Cśanyi, Karsten Reuter et al.  (2021, 120 citations)

Why it's foundational: This paper directly addresses the core computational challenge of molecular crystal structure prediction: searching a complex configurational space for local minima that correspond to stable crystal structures, while keeping the energy evaluation accurate enough to rank polymorphs correctly. It frames the central tension in modern CSP, namely that quantum-mechanical accuracy is needed but is computationally prohibitive at the scale required to screen thousands of candidate structures, and it offers data-efficient machine-learning surrogates trained on high-quality QM data as a way to achieve a favorable accuracy-to-cost ratio. For a researcher working on GA/evolutionary methods for molecular crystals, this captures exactly the bottleneck those search algorithms depend on: the quality and speed of the energy function that guides selection.

How it connects: Evolutionary and genetic-algorithm CSP methods generate and evolve populations of candidate packings, and their success hinges on repeatedly evaluating lattice energies to decide which structures survive and reproduce. This work's data-efficient ML energy models plug directly into that loop, dramatically lowering the per-structure cost that otherwise limits how large and deep a GA search can go. It bridges the classical force-field-driven searches (fast but less accurate) and DFT-D ranking (accurate but expensive) that appear throughout the CSP literature, including the DFTB, dispersion-corrected DFT, and fragment-based energy approaches also in this list. It reflects the current trajectory of the field toward ML-accelerated potentials combined with global optimization.

Take away: The single idea to internalize is that CSP is fundamentally a two-part problem, efficient global search coupled with accurate, cheap energy ranking, and that data-efficient machine learning trained on quantum-mechanical reference data is the key to getting both accuracy and speed in the energy evaluations that drive any GA or evolutionary search.
