📚 Classic of the day — build your canon
Modelling temperature-dependent properties of polymorphic organic molecular crystals
Jonas Nyman, Graeme M. Day  (2016, 104 citations)

Why it's foundational: Although the list is dominated by crystal-engineering reviews, polymorphism studies, and energy-method papers, this large-scale study directly addresses a core challenge at the heart of molecular crystal structure prediction: ranking polymorphs correctly. It systematically computes lattice-vibrational Gibbs free energy differences between 475 pairs of polymorphs of organic molecular crystals and examines how temperature changes structures, free-energy orderings, and properties. For a CSP practitioner, understanding free-energy ranking of candidate structures is the essential quantitative foundation on which any search method is judged.

How it connects: Modern molecular CSP has two stages: (1) generating candidate crystal packings via global search (where genetic/evolutionary algorithms, basin hopping, and quasi-random sampling operate) and (2) ranking those candidates by energy to identify the observed polymorph. This paper speaks to stage (2), showing that static lattice energies are insufficient and that temperature-dependent free energies must be included to correctly order polymorphs. That lesson shapes how GA/evolutionary CSP workflows define their fitness/objective function: whatever search strategy you use, the energy landscape you optimize over should reflect free energy, not just enthalpy. It connects to current work on machine-learned potentials and dispersion-corrected DFT (also in this list) that aim to make such free-energy ranking affordable at scale.

Take away: The decisive quantity in CSP is the free-energy ranking of candidate structures, and because lattice-vibrational (thermal) contributions can change the relative ordering of polymorphs with temperature, a reliable CSP protocol must evaluate temperature-dependent Gibbs free energies rather than relying on 0 K lattice energies alone.
