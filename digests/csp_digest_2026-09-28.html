Mining predicted crystal structure landscapes with high throughput crystallisation: old molecules, new insights
Peng Cui, David P. McMahon, Peter R. Spackman et al.  (2019, 105 citations)

Why it's foundational: This paper is a strong cornerstone for a molecular-CSP researcher because it demonstrates the full modern CSP workflow in action: computing a predicted crystal structure energy landscape and then experimentally validating it. It shows CSP not as an isolated calculation but as a predictive discovery engine, identifying new crystal forms (including a hidden porous polymorph of trimesic acid) of well-studied molecules by pairing structure prediction with high-throughput robotic crystallisation. This closes the loop between prediction and reality, which is what makes CSP scientifically credible and useful.

How it connects: The concept of a 'predicted crystal structure landscape' - a set of many low-energy candidate packings generated by global search and ranked by lattice energy - is exactly the output that genetic-algorithm and evolutionary CSP methods aim to produce. Evolutionary methods are one family of search strategies for populating and refining such landscapes, so understanding how a landscape is interpreted, mined, and experimentally targeted gives essential context for why the search matters. It also connects to current work combining CSP with automation, machine-learned energy models, and property-driven materials discovery (e.g., porosity), showing where GA-based generation feeds into downstream screening.

Take away: Internalize that CSP produces an energy landscape of many plausible polymorphs, not a single answer, and its real power emerges when that computed landscape is used to guide and interpret experiment - so the goal of any search method, including genetic algorithms, is to comprehensively and accurately map that low-energy landscape.
