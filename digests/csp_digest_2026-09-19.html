Modeling Polymorphic Molecular Crystals with Electronic Structure Theory
Gregory J. O. Beran  (2016, 379 citations)

Why it's foundational: This 2016 Chemical Reviews article is a defining, broad review of how electronic structure theory is used to model polymorphic molecular crystals. Rather than being a narrow application, it surveys the full methodological landscape (DFT, dispersion corrections, energy ranking, polymorphism) that any molecular CSP practitioner must understand. The abstract explicitly frames the growing relevance of molecular crystals to pharmaceuticals, organic semiconductors, and other applications, and positions electronic structure methods as the increasingly important tool for modeling polymorphism. That scope makes it an ideal cornerstone for building core mental structure.

How it connects: Molecular CSP with genetic-algorithm/evolutionary search has two halves: the global search engine that generates candidate packings, and the energy model that ranks them. This review anchors the second half, the accurate lattice energy evaluation and polymorph ranking that determines whether a GA/evolutionary search actually converges to the right structures. It ties directly to the many related methods in this list (dispersion-corrected DFT for lattice energies, DFT-D, DFTB, machine-learning potentials for structure-energy landscapes) and to modern blind-test-driven CSP where the ranking step is the hard bottleneck. Understanding it lets the researcher judge which energy method to pair with their evolutionary sampling.

Take away: Internalize that reliable molecular CSP hinges on accurately distinguishing tiny energy differences between polymorphs, and that the choice of electronic structure method (especially dispersion treatment) is what makes or breaks the ranking of candidate structures produced by any search algorithm, evolutionary or otherwise.
