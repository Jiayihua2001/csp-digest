A periodic genetic algorithm with real-space representation for crystal structure and polymorph prediction
Nathan Luke Abraham, Matt Probert  (2006, 170 citations)

Why it's foundational: This paper defines a genetic algorithm for crystal structure prediction that operates directly on a periodic real-space representation, requiring no prior assumptions about unit cell size, shape, or symmetry. That is exactly the problem setting for molecular CSP with evolutionary methods: how to encode a crystal, generate a population, and evolve it toward global energy minima. Unlike blind-test reports (which benchmark whole communities) or dispersion-correction and force-field papers (which focus on energy accuracy), this work lays out the core algorithmic machinery of a GA specifically for finding crystal global minima and doubles as a polymorph search technique.

How it connects: It sits at the intersection of the two pillars in the researcher's profile: molecular crystal structure prediction and genetic/evolutionary optimization. It builds conceptually on foundational GA theory (Holland's Adaptation in Natural and Artificial Systems, entry 31) and parallels the ab initio evolutionary approach of USPEX-style methods (entries 3 and 6) and the inorganic-GA precedent (entry 29). Its polymorph-search framing links directly to modern crystal energy landscape work (entries 28, 47) and to today's efforts pairing GA/evolutionary search with fast surrogate energy models and machine-learned potentials (entries 39, 46). Understanding the representation and evolutionary operators here helps a reader parse how newer methods differ, whether via PSO (entry 2) or adaptive DFT-coupled GAs (entry 46).

Take away: The central idea to internalize: CSP can be cast as unbiased global optimization over the crystal energy landscape using a periodic, real-space genetic encoding that lets unit cell parameters and atomic arrangement evolve freely, so that the same search that finds the global minimum also naturally enumerates competitive low-energy polymorphs.
