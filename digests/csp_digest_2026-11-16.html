Auto-Encoding Variational Bayes
Diederik P. Kingma, Max Welling  (2013, 17804 citations, UvA-DARE (University of Amsterdam))

Worth 30 minutes? Kingma and Welling introduced the reparameterization trick and amortized variational inference with an encoder network. Together these made the ELBO trainable by plain stochastic gradients at scale. That template underlies VAEs, latent diffusion and the variational view of diffusion models. MatterGen-style crystal generators inherit the same language of ELBOs, latents and likelihood bounds. It is short, dense and genuinely foundational.

What it could inspire: A learned latent space over molecular packings could let a GA mutate and cross over in a smooth continuous space instead of on raw cell parameters. A decoder conditioned on lattice energy could act as a generative initial-population sampler, like Genarris. The ELBO framing also clarifies how likelihood-based generators relate to Boltzmann generators and to sampling low-energy polymorph basins.

Foundations to learn: Latent-variable models and intractable posteriors. The evidence lower bound (ELBO) and its split into reconstruction plus KL terms. Amortized inference with an encoder network. The reparameterization trick for low-variance gradient estimates. Contrast with score-function (REINFORCE) estimators. Gaussian KL in closed form. These ideas carry directly into diffusion-model derivations and normalizing-flow training.

30-minute reading guide: Read the introduction and Section 2 (problem setup, variational bound) first, about 10 minutes. Then Section 2.3-2.4 on the SGVB estimator and reparameterization, deriving the gradient yourself, about 10 minutes. Then Section 3 (the VAE example) and Appendix B (Gaussian KL), about 7 minutes. Skim the MNIST/Frey face experiments and skip the full variational Bayes appendix.
