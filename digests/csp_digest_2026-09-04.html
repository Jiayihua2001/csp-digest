A test of crystal structure prediction of small organic molecules
Jos P. M. Lommerse, W.D.S. Motherwell, Herman L. Ammon et al.  (2000, 445 citations)

Why it's foundational: This 1999 paper reports the first organized blind test of crystal structure prediction for small organic molecules, hosted at the Cambridge Crystallographic Data Centre. It established the community-wide, controlled benchmark paradigm in which participants are given only atomic connectivity and must predict the crystal structure. For a researcher focused on molecular CSP, this defines the field's central challenge, its rules of engagement, and how success is measured objectively across competing methods.

How it connects: The blind test format launched here became the recurring standard (the later third blind test, CSP2004, appears in this same list) against which every method, including genetic-algorithm and evolutionary approaches, is validated. GA/evolutionary CSP tools are ultimately judged by their ability to locate the experimental structure among many low-energy candidates on a crystal energy landscape, exactly the task this benchmark formalizes. It also frames the two coupled problems that modern CSP still tackles: efficient search of packing space (where GA, PSO, and quasi-random sampling methods compete) and accurate energy ranking (later addressed by dispersion-corrected DFT and machine-learned potentials).

Take away: Molecular CSP is fundamentally a blind, connectivity-to-structure prediction problem, and the way to trust any method, GA/evolutionary or otherwise, is to benchmark it against experimentally known structures under blind conditions rather than by post-hoc fitting.
