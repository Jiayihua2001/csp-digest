GAtor: A First-Principles Genetic Algorithm for Molecular Crystal Structure Prediction
Farren Curtis, Xiayue Li, Timothy Rose et al.  (2018, 133 citations)

Why it's foundational: GAtor is a defining, purpose-built genetic-algorithm method specifically for molecular crystal structure prediction, exactly matching the researcher's focus on GA/evolutionary approaches for molecular (not just inorganic) crystals. Per the abstract, it is a massively parallel, first-principles GA implemented in Python and interfaced with FHI-aims for local optimizations and energy evaluations, making it a concrete, modern cornerstone that operationalizes evolutionary CSP at the level of DFT accuracy.

How it connects: It sits at the intersection of the evolutionary-optimization lineage (Holland's genetic algorithm foundations, and GA-for-crystals work such as inorganic-structure GAs) and the molecular-CSP tradition tracked by the CCDC blind tests and crystal-energy-landscape studies. It contrasts with alternative global optimizers like particle-swarm optimization while sharing the same evolutionary-scheme goal, and its first-principles coupling to a DFT code anticipates current directions that pair GA search with electronic-structure and machine-learning energy models for reliable polymorph ranking.

Take away: The core idea to internalize: a genetic algorithm can drive molecular crystal structure prediction from scratch when tightly coupled to accurate first-principles energy evaluation and made massively parallel, so search strategy and energy-model fidelity must be co-designed to explore the polymorphic energy landscape efficiently.
