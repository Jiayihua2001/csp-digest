📚 Landmark of the day — AI for Science landmark
De novo design of protein structure and function with RFdiffusion
Joseph L. Watson, David Juergens, Nathaniel R. Bennett et al.  (2023, 2350 citations, Nature)

Worth 30 minutes? RFdiffusion showed that fine-tuning a structure predictor (RoseTTAFold) as a denoiser over residue frames turns it into a general generative designer. It handles binders, symmetric oligomers and motif scaffolding, and designs were validated experimentally, including cryo-EM structures. It made diffusion over rigid-body frames the template for structure generation in AI for science, alongside AlphaFold-tier work.

What it could inspire: Molecules in a crystal are rigid or semi-rigid bodies with positions and orientations, which is the same SE(3) frame representation RFdiffusion denoises. That suggests diffusion-generated Z' packings, symmetry-conditioned generation in the spirit of space-group constraints, and motif or synthon scaffolding for conditional CSP. Diffusion-generated candidates could seed or replace GA initial pools such as Genarris, with DFT kept for final ranking.

Foundations to learn: Key ideas: denoising diffusion on Euclidean translations combined with diffusion on rotations (IGSO3), and pretrained-predictor-as-denoiser fine-tuning. Also self-conditioning, conditioning on partial structure (motifs), and symmetric noise for cyclic or dihedral assemblies. Finally, how to validate generated structures by in-silico refolding (AF2 self-consistency), which is analogous to re-relaxing generated crystals.

30-minute reading guide: Read Fig. 1 and the opening sections on the method and the frame-diffusion setup (10 min). Then the symmetric oligomer and motif-scaffolding results (Figs. 2-3), which map best onto crystal symmetry (8 min). Then the Methods on rotation noise, self-conditioning and training loss (10 min). Skim the binder design experiments and wet-lab assay details.
