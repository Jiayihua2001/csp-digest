Combined crystal structure prediction and high-pressure crystallization in rational pharmaceutical polymorph screening
Marcus A. Neumann, Jacco van de Streek, F.P.A. Fabbiani et al.  (2015, 242 citations)

Why it's foundational: This paper is a strong cornerstone for a molecular CSP researcher because it demonstrates the full in silico polymorph screening workflow applied to real pharmaceutical molecules, moving CSP from a trial-and-error experimental game to a predictive, rational endeavor. Among the listed papers it is the clearest landmark that ties together the central goal of molecular CSP (enumerating and ranking polymorphs of organic molecules) with experimental validation via high-pressure crystallization. Unlike the many narrow application or property-specific papers in the list, it defines the practical value proposition of CSP for finding polymorphs that matter to pharmaceuticals, agrochemicals, and pigments.

How it connects: It connects directly to modern CSP practice, where the search-then-rank paradigm underpins essentially every method, including genetic-algorithm and evolutionary approaches that generate and evolve candidate crystal packings before energy ranking. The energy landscape it produces is exactly the object that GA/evolutionary optimizers (as seen in the hard-sphere GA and inorganic framework GA papers here) are designed to explore efficiently. It also links to the broader ecosystem in this list: machine-learned energies and force fields for faster ranking, dispersion-corrected DFT and DFTB for accurate lattice energies, quasi-random sampling for search, and the combined CSP-plus-robotic-crystallization mining studies that grew out of this same predict-then-find philosophy.

Take away: Internalize that molecular CSP is fundamentally a two-stage problem of comprehensively sampling the crystal packing landscape and then accurately ranking candidate structures by lattice energy, and that its ultimate validation and usefulness come from marrying computational prediction with targeted experimental crystallization to discover and control polymorphs.
