Machine learning for the structure–energy–property landscapes of molecular crystals
Félix Musil, Sandip De, Jack Yang et al.  (2017, 229 citations)

Why it's foundational: This paper directly targets the core challenge in molecular crystal structure prediction: building accurate structure-energy-property relationships across the vast space of candidate polymorphs. The abstract states it achieves accuracy using only a few hundred reference configurations and reduces computational effort by a factor of ten for property prediction, while also enabling automatic structural classification of polymorphs. For a researcher focused on GA/evolutionary CSP, the bottleneck is almost always evaluating the energy of enormous numbers of trial structures generated during search, and this work addresses exactly that evaluation step for molecular crystals specifically.

How it connects: Evolutionary and genetic-algorithm CSP methods generate and rank large populations of candidate molecular packings, and each candidate must be scored by energy. The machine-learned surrogate described here slots directly into that loop, replacing or accelerating expensive quantum mechanical lattice-energy evaluations that otherwise dominate cost. It connects to the dispersion-corrected DFT and tight-binding approaches elsewhere in this set (B3LYP-D, DFTB, ML fragment energies) as the learned, cheaper alternative, and to modern ML-driven CSP and materials-discovery pipelines. Its polymorph classification via structural fingerprints also complements the sampling and landscape-mapping ideas central to evolutionary search.

Take away: The decisive internalizable idea is that machine-learned potentials trained on modest quantum reference data can deliver near-quantum accuracy for molecular crystal energies at a fraction of the cost, making it feasible to evaluate the huge candidate populations that genetic/evolutionary CSP relies on while also helping classify the resulting polymorphs.
