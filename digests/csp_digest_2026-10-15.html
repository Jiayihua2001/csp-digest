How to quantify energy landscapes of solids
Artem R. Oganov, Mario Valle  (2009, 210 citations)

Why it's foundational: This paper directly addresses the conceptual core of crystal structure prediction: how to quantify and understand the energy landscapes of solids. It introduces a reduced energy surface, a density-of-states description, and a structure descriptor (fingerprint function) that let one measure similarity and diversity among candidate structures. These are exactly the ingredients an evolutionary/GA-based CSP engine needs to explore, compare, and prune a population of candidate crystals. Among the listed papers this is the one most squarely about the general framework of structure-landscape exploration rather than a narrow synthesis, characterization, or application study.

How it connects: Modern molecular CSP with genetic algorithms works by generating populations of trial structures, relaxing them on an energy surface, and using similarity/distance measures to maintain diversity and avoid duplicate minima during crossover and mutation. The fingerprint and landscape-topology ideas in this paper underpin the duplicate-detection and niching strategies used in evolutionary CSP codes, and the notion that landscapes obey quantifiable rules motivates efficient global optimization. It links naturally to the other evolutionary/GA structure-search papers in the list (grain boundaries, hydrogen superconductors, 2D materials) by providing the shared conceptual language of exploring rugged energy landscapes.

Take away: Internalize that CSP is fundamentally a global exploration of a quantifiable energy landscape, and that a good structural fingerprint plus a notion of landscape topology is what makes population-based search (GA/evolutionary) efficient by distinguishing genuinely distinct minima from duplicates.
