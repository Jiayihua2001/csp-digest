Machine-Learned Fragment-Based Energies for Crystal Structure Prediction
David McDonagh, Chris‐Kriton Skylaris, Graeme M. Day et al.  (2019, 56 citations)

Why it's foundational: For a researcher focused on molecular CSP, this paper directly addresses the defining challenge of the field: searching a complex configurational space for local minima that correspond to stable crystal structures. It frames CSP explicitly as a global optimization problem and tackles the central bottleneck of accurately and efficiently evaluating intermolecular (and here intramolecular/fragment) energies during that search. Among the listed papers it is the one most squarely about the CSP search-and-rank methodology itself rather than a narrow application, a crystal-engineering synthon survey, or an inorganic/materials use case.

How it connects: The abstract situates modern CSP as the combination of (i) a search over configurational space for local minima and (ii) an energy model to rank candidate structures. This is exactly the structure of genetic-algorithm and evolutionary CSP workflows, where an evolving population of candidate packings is repeatedly generated, relaxed, and scored by an energy function. The paper's move from atom-atom force fields toward machine-learned fragment-based energies reflects the current trajectory of the field, where cheap-but-approximate potentials used inside the global optimizer are being replaced by data-driven models that approach quantum-mechanical accuracy without the cost, a theme echoed by GAP-style potentials and ML-driven materials discovery elsewhere in this list.

Take away: Internalize that CSP is fundamentally two coupled problems: efficiently sampling the vast space of packing arrangements (where GA/evolutionary methods supply the search engine) and accurately ranking the resulting local minima by energy (where the fidelity and speed of the energy model, increasingly machine-learned, determine whether the right polymorph is found and correctly prioritized).
