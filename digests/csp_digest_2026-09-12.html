Adaptation in Natural and Artificial Systems (John H. Holland)
Jeffrey R. Sampson  (1976, 320 citations)

Why it's foundational: This is the review of John Holland's Adaptation in Natural and Artificial Systems, the work that originated the genetic algorithm as a general framework for search and optimization based on selection, crossover, and mutation over populations of candidate solutions. For a researcher focused on GA/evolutionary methods in crystal structure prediction, this is the intellectual wellspring: every evolutionary CSP code inherits its core operators and its population-based search philosophy directly from Holland's formulation.

How it connects: Modern molecular CSP relies on stochastic global optimization to explore vast, rugged energy landscapes of packing arrangements. Genetic and evolutionary algorithms (and their relatives, like the particle-swarm scheme in paper 2, which is explicitly framed within an evolutionary scheme) are among the dominant search engines used to generate and refine trial structures before energy ranking with the lattice-energy and dispersion-corrected DFT methods described in the other papers. The GA operators applied to interfaces (paper 35), hard-sphere crystals (paper 37), and protein folding (paper 32) in this list all trace back to the abstractions defined here. Understanding Holland's original formalism lets the researcher reason about why the operators work, how to design representations and fitness functions for crystal packing, and where convergence guarantees do and do not hold.

Take away: Internalize the core evolutionary paradigm: maintain a population of encoded candidate solutions and iteratively improve it via fitness-proportional selection plus recombination and mutation. This general adaptive search principle, not any single crystal-specific trick, is the foundation on which evolutionary CSP methods are built.
