Modelling organic crystal structures using distributed multipole and polarizability-based model intermolecular potentials
Sarah L. Price, Maurice Leslie, Gareth W. A. Welch et al.  (2010, 327 citations)

Why it's foundational: This paper describes DMACRYS, a crystal lattice simulation program built specifically for the two core demands of molecular crystal structure prediction: rapidly assessing thousands to millions of trial packings and ranking their relative lattice energies with high accuracy. It defines the anisotropic, physically motivated model intermolecular potentials (distributed multipole electrostatics and polarizability-based models) that underpin much of modern organic CSP. As such it is a defining methods paper for the energy-evaluation engine that any search algorithm, including a genetic/evolutionary one, must sit on top of.

How it connects: CSP is fundamentally a two-part problem: (1) generate/sample candidate packings and (2) score them by lattice energy. Genetic-algorithm and evolutionary methods address part (1) by evolving populations of candidate structures, but they are only as good as the energy landscape they optimize on. DMACRYS represents the standard for part (2) in molecular crystals via accurate distributed-multipole electrostatics, and it is the accuracy/speed tradeoff at the heart of that step that motivated later machine-learned potentials (see the ML-for-molecular-crystals and data-efficient ML CSP papers) and dispersion-corrected DFT ranking. Understanding this potential-based framework lets the researcher see exactly where a GA plugs in and why the quality of the fitness function matters.

Take away: Molecular CSP hinges on evaluating enormous numbers of candidate crystals with the best possible relative energies; anisotropic distributed-multipole and polarizability-based intermolecular potentials (as in DMACRYS) are the accuracy backbone that a genetic or evolutionary search must optimize against, so the search algorithm and the energy model are inseparable design choices.
