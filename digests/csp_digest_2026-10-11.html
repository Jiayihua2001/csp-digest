The prediction of inorganic crystal framework structures using excluded regions within a genetic algorithm approach
Scott M. Woodley, C. Richard A. Catlow, Peter D. Battle et al.  (2003, 47 citations)

Why it's foundational: This paper introduces a genetic-algorithm approach to crystal structure prediction, directly combining the evolutionary optimization paradigm with the global search problem that defines CSP. Among the papers listed, it is the clearest methodological cornerstone for a researcher focused specifically on GA/evolutionary methods for crystal structures, since it presents a general technique (excluded regions within a GA) for generating feasible framework structures from minimal input, namely unit cell dimensions, constituent elements, and building units. It frames CSP as a constrained global optimization problem solvable by evolutionary search rather than exhaustive enumeration.

How it connects: Modern CSP workflows, whether for molecular or inorganic solids, rest on the same core idea this paper embodies: treat structure generation as an optimization over a vast configurational landscape and use population-based evolutionary operators (selection, crossover, mutation) to converge on low-energy or feasible candidates. The excluded-regions strategy anticipates later constraint-handling and symmetry-aware sampling used in evolutionary CSP codes. It links naturally to the other GA-based entries here (grain boundaries, superconducting hydrides via evolutionary algorithms, deep-learning-augmented GAs) and to energy-landscape thinking needed to rank candidate polymorphs. For a molecular CSP researcher, it provides the methodological template that is then paired with molecular-crystal-specific energy models (dispersion-corrected DFT, lattice-energy methods appearing elsewhere in this list).

Take away: Internalize that crystal structure prediction can be cast as a global optimization problem where a genetic/evolutionary algorithm, guided by physical constraints (such as excluded regions) and known building blocks, efficiently searches the enormous space of candidate structures rather than enumerating them exhaustively.
