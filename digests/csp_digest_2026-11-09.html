📚 Landmark of the day — Molecular-CSP classic
Crystal Engineering: A Holistic View
Gautam Radhakrishna Desiraju  (2007, 1504 citations)

Worth 30 minutes? No AI4Sci landmark is in this list. This is Desiraju's widely cited synthesis of crystal engineering. It frames molecular solids as designed assemblies of directional (hydrogen and coordination bonds) and less directional interactions, and establishes the supramolecular synthon as the transferable unit of packing. It shaped how the field thinks about polymorphism and the limits of design, which is the conceptual backdrop to MCSP.

What it could inspire: Synthons could serve as structural priors in GA search. Examples: synthon-aware crossover or mutation in GAtor, Genarris seeds biased toward common hydrogen-bond motifs, and diversity metrics defined over synthon graphs rather than RDF descriptors. For generative AI4Sci, synthons suggest coarse-grained tokens or conditioning signals for diffusion or flow models of molecular crystals, and a check on whether learned models recover chemically sensible motifs.

Foundations to learn: Key ideas: the hierarchy of intermolecular interactions (strong and weak hydrogen bonds, halogen bonds, van der Waals packing), the synthon concept and its robustness, and Kitaigorodskii close packing versus directional design. Also the interplay of kinetics and thermodynamics in polymorph formation, how Cambridge Structural Database statistics inform design rules, and why crystal structures are emergent and hard to predict.

30-minute reading guide: Spend 10 min on the introduction and the sections on interactions and synthons, and study the synthon figures. Spend 10 min on the polymorphism and crystal structure prediction discussion, noting where the authors say design fails. Skim the coordination polymer and MOF sections. Use the last 10 min to list three synthon-based priors or operators you could test in your GA.
