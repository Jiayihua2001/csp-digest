Conformational Polymorphism in Organic Crystals
Ashwini Kumar Nangia  (2008, 590 citations)

Why it's foundational: This Accounts of Chemical Research article on conformational polymorphism in organic crystals defines a central problem that molecular CSP must solve: the same molecule can adopt different conformers that pack into distinct crystal forms. Among the list, it is the paper that most directly frames the core physical challenge of organic molecular crystal structure prediction rather than a narrow application, a materials-discovery tangent, or an inorganic/force-field method paper.

How it connects: Modern molecular CSP, including genetic-algorithm and evolutionary search methods, must generate and rank candidate structures across both packing arrangements and internal molecular flexibility. The abstract makes clear that conformers can differ between polymorphs and even coexist in one crystal, which is exactly why CSP search spaces couple intramolecular conformational degrees of freedom with lattice packing. Evolutionary algorithms encode these degrees of freedom in their genomes and use energy ranking to find the near-degenerate low-energy forms this paper describes, connecting directly to blind-test-style polymorph prediction and to case studies like ROY referenced elsewhere in the list.

Take away: Internalize that in organic crystals the molecule is not rigid: conformational freedom and crystal packing are coupled, so any CSP search (GA/evolutionary included) must simultaneously explore conformation and packing and distinguish energetically close polymorphs.
