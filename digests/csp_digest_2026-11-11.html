Variational Inference with Normalizing Flows
Danilo Jimenez Rezende, Shakir Mohamed  (2015, 1913 citations, arXiv (Cornell University))

Worth 30 minutes? This paper introduced normalizing flows to machine learning: a simple density is pushed through invertible maps while the exact density is tracked via the change-of-variables formula. That idea runs through Boltzmann generators, continuous normalizing flows and flow matching. It is short, clean theory, the root of the generative-sampling tier you want to understand.

What it could inspire: Flows give exact likelihoods, so samples can be reweighted to a Boltzmann distribution. For MCSP, this suggests generating crystal packings (cell, orientations, positions) with a flow, reweighting against DFT-D energies, and seeding GAtor populations with them. It could also help estimate polymorph free energies, rather than only lattice energies, via targeted free-energy perturbation.

Foundations to learn: Change-of-variables for densities and the log-determinant Jacobian cost. Planar and radial flows as cheap invertible layers. The ELBO, and how a richer posterior tightens it. Infinitesimal flows, the bridge to continuous normalizing flows and later flow matching. Also the trade-off between expressivity and tractable Jacobians that shapes every later flow architecture.

30-minute reading guide: Read sections 2-3 first: the variational setup and the finite and infinitesimal flow derivations, especially the change-of-variables equation. Spend about 10 minutes on the planar flow and its Jacobian determinant. Look at the 2D toy-density figures to build intuition. Skim the VAE experiments and benchmark tables. Finish by noting how this maps onto Boltzmann generators (Noe et al. 2019).
