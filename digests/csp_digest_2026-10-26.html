Predicting Lattice Energy of Organic Crystals by Density Functional Theory with Empirically Corrected Dispersion Energy
Shaoxin Feng, Tonglei Li  (2005, 109 citations)

Why it's foundational: For molecular crystal structure prediction, the central quantity that any search method must optimize is the lattice energy, and this paper directly addresses how to compute it reliably for organic crystals. It tackles the key problem that standard quantum mechanical methods fail to fully capture dispersion (van der Waals) interactions, which dominate the packing of molecular solids, and proposes a density functional theory approach with an empirically corrected dispersion energy. The abstract makes clear that accurate lattice energy is needed to predict structural and physicochemical properties including polymorphism, which is the heart of CSP.

How it connects: Every genetic-algorithm or evolutionary CSP workflow depends on a fitness or ranking function, and that function is almost always the lattice energy. An evolutionary search can generate and mutate candidate packings efficiently, but the candidates are only meaningful if the energy ranking separating true polymorphs from decoys is accurate. The dispersion-corrected DFT philosophy introduced here underlies the energy evaluation step that modern CSP pipelines (and their force-field or machine-learned-potential surrogates) are built to reproduce or accelerate.

Take away: The quality of molecular CSP is limited by the accuracy of the lattice energy evaluation, and for organic crystals you cannot get that right without correctly treating dispersion interactions; the search algorithm (GA or otherwise) is only as good as the energy model that ranks its candidates.
