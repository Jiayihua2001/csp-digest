Inaccurate Conformational Energies Still Hinder Crystal Structure Prediction in Flexible Organic Molecules
Chandler Greenwell, Gregory J. O. Beran  (2020, 81 citations)

Why it's foundational: This paper directly addresses the central bottleneck of molecular crystal structure prediction (CSP) for flexible organic molecules: that inaccurate conformational energies still hinder reliable structure ranking. For a researcher focused on GA/evolutionary CSP of molecular crystals, understanding where DFT-driven CSP succeeds and where it breaks down (intramolecular conformational energetics) is essential groundwork, because any search method, evolutionary or otherwise, is only as good as the energy model used to score candidate structures.

How it connects: Modern CSP workflows, including genetic-algorithm and evolutionary global-search approaches, generate large populations of candidate packings and then rank them by lattice (free) energy. This work highlights that the accuracy of the underlying energy surface, especially the balance between intramolecular conformational energy and intermolecular packing energy, is a limiting factor. It connects to the broader ecosystem in this list: dispersion-corrected DFT and DFT-D methods (B3LYP-D*, DFTB, DFT with empirical dispersion), machine-learned fragment energies for CSP, and the ROY polymorph case studies that serve as recurring CSP benchmarks. It frames why evolutionary searches on flexible molecules remain hard and where method development should focus.

Take away: The quality of CSP outcomes for flexible molecules is governed as much by the accuracy of the conformational energy model as by the efficiency of the structure-search algorithm; improving energy accuracy is a prerequisite for trustworthy polymorph ranking regardless of the global optimization strategy used.
