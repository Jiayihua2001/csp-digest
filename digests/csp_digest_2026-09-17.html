A genetic algorithm for predicting the structures of interfaces in multicomponent systems
Alvin L.-S. Chua, Nicole A. Benedek, Lin Chen et al.  (2010, 183 citations)

Why it's foundational: This paper presents a genetic algorithm for predicting the structures of interfaces in multicomponent systems, which is directly aligned with the researcher's focus on evolutionary/GA-based structure prediction. Among the listed papers, it is one of the clearest expositions of applying a genetic (evolutionary) search strategy to crystal/structure prediction, establishing the core machinery of population-based global optimization for predicting stable atomic arrangements.

How it connects: Modern molecular CSP relies heavily on global optimization to navigate the vast, rugged energy landscape of possible packings, and GA/evolutionary methods are a central family of such search algorithms. This work exemplifies how a genetic algorithm evolves candidate structures through selection, crossover, and mutation toward energetically favorable configurations, a paradigm mirrored in evolutionary CSP codes. It links naturally to complementary landscape and energy-ranking work in the list (for example quasi-random sampling in 10.1021/acs.jctc.5b01112 and lattice-energy assessment in 10.1021/cg0498148), where the search step is paired with accurate energy evaluation.

Take away: Internalize that structure prediction can be framed as a population-based global optimization problem in which a genetic algorithm iteratively evolves and selects candidate structures by their computed energetic stability, providing a systematic, automated route to discover stable configurations without prior structural bias.
