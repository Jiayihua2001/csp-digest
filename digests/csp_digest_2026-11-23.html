📚 Landmark of the day — AI for Science landmark
A fast and elitist multiobjective genetic algorithm: NSGA-II
Kalyanmoy Deb, Amrit Pratap, S. Agarwal et al.  (2002, 49899 citations, IEEE Transactions on Evolutionary Computation)

Worth 30 minutes? NSGA-II is the reference multi-objective evolutionary algorithm. It made Pareto-based GAs practical with fast non-dominated sorting, elitism, and parameter-free crowding distance for diversity. It fixed the cubic sorting cost and non-elitism the abstract criticizes, and became the baseline that later multi-objective GAs are built on or compared against. For anyone running GAs, its selection logic is core vocabulary.

What it could inspire: Polymorph search is implicitly multi-objective: lattice energy versus density, packing, property targets, or structural novelty. NSGA-II style selection could replace scalar energy fitness in GAtor-like codes. Pareto fronts could be built over cheap-ML-energy versus DFT-energy uncertainty, or over energy versus diversity. Crowding distance offers a principled alternative to ad hoc niching or clustering for keeping distinct packing motifs alive.

Foundations to learn: Pareto dominance and fronts. The fast non-dominated sorting algorithm and why it beats the naive cubic version. Elitism through combined parent-offspring populations. Crowding distance as a parameter-free diversity measure. Crowded-comparison tournament selection. Constraint handling by constrained-domination. Together these explain how to keep both convergence pressure and diversity in a population search, the central tension in GA-based CSP.

30-minute reading guide: Start with the introduction's three criticisms of earlier MOEAs (5 min). Read the fast non-dominated sort and crowding-distance sections closely, sketching the algorithms (12 min). Read the main loop with elitist mu+lambda selection (5 min). Skim the constraint-handling section (4 min). Glance at the test-problem figures showing front spread (4 min). Skip the detailed parameter tables and the real-coded versus binary comparisons.
