Convergence Properties of Crystal Structure Prediction by Quasi-Random Sampling
David H. Case, Josh E. Campbell, Peter J. Bygrave et al.  (2015, 125 citations)

Why it's foundational: This paper directly addresses the central methodological problem of molecular crystal structure prediction: how to generate and sample the enormous configurational space of crystal packing arrangements. It analyzes the convergence properties of a defining search strategy (quasi-random, low-discrepancy sampling) for ab initio CSP, framing the search step as the essential bottleneck of the whole prediction pipeline. Understanding what it means for a search to converge on the full set of low-energy packings is the conceptual bedrock on which any specific search algorithm, including genetic/evolutionary methods, must be judged.

How it connects: CSP is fundamentally a global optimization problem over space groups, molecular positions, orientations, and conformations, followed by energy ranking. Genetic and evolutionary algorithms are one family of search engines competing with quasi-random sampling, basin hopping, and simulated annealing to explore exactly the space this paper characterizes. By making explicit what convergence of configurational sampling looks like, the paper gives the researcher a yardstick to evaluate whether a GA has adequately covered packing space and how efficiently it does so relative to the quasi-random baseline. It also connects to modern directions in the list, such as machine-learned energies and data-efficient ML that accelerate the energy evaluation step once candidate structures are generated.

Take away: Internalize that CSP success hinges on adequately and demonstrably sampling the configurational space of crystal packings, and that any search method, GA included, must be assessed by its convergence toward the complete low-energy landscape rather than by any single structure it happens to find.
