From crystal structure prediction to polymorph prediction: interpreting the crystal energy landscape
Sarah L. Price  (2008, 185 citations)

Why it's foundational: This paper defines the conceptual shift at the heart of modern molecular CSP: moving from merely predicting a single most-stable structure to interpreting the full crystal energy landscape as the basis for polymorph prediction. For a researcher focused on molecular crystals, it lays out the defining framing of the field, connecting computational search to real-world polymorphism, solvates, and the practical challenge of controlling crystallisation, rather than being a narrow application to one compound.

How it connects: The idea of generating and ranking a landscape of low-energy structures is exactly what genetic-algorithm and evolutionary CSP methods are built to explore efficiently. GA/evolutionary searches (and alternatives like particle-swarm and quasi-random sampling) are the engines that populate the crystal energy landscape this paper describes, while dispersion-corrected DFT and machine-learned potentials supply the accurate energy ranking it demands. Current work on data-driven CSP, high-throughput crystallisation validation, and questions like how many polymorphs remain undiscovered all descend from this landscape-centric view.

Take away: Internalize that molecular CSP is not about finding one answer but about mapping and interpreting a crystal energy landscape of competing low-energy structures; polymorph prediction, and the quality demanded of any search algorithm and energy model, follows from that landscape perspective.
