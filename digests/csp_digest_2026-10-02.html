📚 Classic of the day — build your canon
Prediction of binary hard-sphere crystal structures
Laura Filion, Marjolein Dijkstra  (2009, 145 citations)

Why it's foundational: This paper directly demonstrates the core methodological pairing at the heart of the researcher's profile: a genetic algorithm combined with Monte Carlo simulations used to predict close-packed crystal structures. As stated in the abstract, it presents a method to predict binary crystal structures in hard-sphere mixtures, which is a clean, canonical illustration of how evolutionary global optimization is applied to the crystal packing problem without the confounding details of specific chemistry.

How it connects: CSP is fundamentally a global optimization problem over a complex packing landscape, and GA/evolutionary methods are a leading way to search that space. This work exemplifies the GA-plus-sampling template that underlies modern molecular CSP workflows, where candidate structures are generated, ranked by energy, and iteratively evolved. It links naturally to the broader CSP literature in the list, such as quasi-random sampling for configurational search (10.1021/acs.jctc.5b01112), lattice-energy ranking via dispersion-corrected methods (10.1021/jz500755u, 10.1021/ct050189a), and the newer machine-learning energy surrogates that aim to accelerate exactly this kind of search (10.1039/d0sc05765g, 10.1021/acs.jctc.9b00038).

Take away: Internalize that crystal structure prediction can be framed as a global search over packing configurations, and that a genetic/evolutionary algorithm coupled to a reliable energy evaluation (here Monte Carlo) is a powerful, general strategy for locating the stable and metastable structures on that landscape.
