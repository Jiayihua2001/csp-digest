Computational prediction of organic crystal structures and polymorphism
Sarah L. Price  (2008, 140 citations)

Why it's foundational: This is a defining review of computational prediction of organic crystal structures and polymorphism. Rather than a single narrow application, it lays out the full conceptual framework of molecular CSP: the challenge of predicting solid forms of organic molecules (especially pharmaceuticals), the necessity of generating diverse trial packings across space groups, and the central problem of computing relative thermodynamic stabilities to rank candidate structures. For a researcher entering molecular CSP with an interest in evolutionary/GA methods, this review establishes the vocabulary, the search-and-rank paradigm, and the polymorphism motivation that all method development sits within.

How it connects: The review frames CSP as a two-part problem of (1) sampling the vast configurational space of possible crystal packings and (2) accurately ranking their energies. This search-plus-ranking structure is exactly what genetic-algorithm and evolutionary methods target on the search side, and what dispersion-corrected DFT and machine-learning potentials target on the ranking side. It connects directly to the modern papers in this list: quasi-random sampling, GA-based structure prediction, ML for structure-energy-property landscapes, and blind-test-style benchmarking. Understanding the polymorphism motivation and the accuracy demands articulated here explains why later evolutionary search methods and energy-ranking improvements were pursued.

Take away: Molecular CSP is fundamentally a coupled problem of thoroughly searching the crystal packing landscape and then ranking candidates by lattice free energy with high enough accuracy to distinguish closely spaced polymorphs; every method choice, including GA/evolutionary search, is judged by how well it serves those two linked goals.
