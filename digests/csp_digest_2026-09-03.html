Towards crystal structure prediction of complex organic compounds – a report on the fifth blind test
David A. Bardwell, Claire S. Adjiman, Yelena A. Arnautova et al.  (2011, 433 citations)

Why it's foundational: This is the report on the fifth CSP blind test (CSP2010), organized by the Cambridge Crystallographic Data Centre as the fifth in an ongoing series of international collaborative benchmarks (CSP1999, CSP2001, CSP2004, CSP2007). Blind tests are the defining community benchmark for molecular crystal structure prediction: participants are given only molecular connectivity and must predict the crystal structure ahead of experimental disclosure, using a range of competing methodologies. As stated in the abstract, this test pushed toward more complex organic compounds, making it the most demanding and representative snapshot of the field's collective methods and their real predictive power.

How it connects: The blind tests are the shared yardstick against which every CSP method, including genetic-algorithm and evolutionary search approaches, is judged. Because participants deploy diverse search algorithms (global optimization, evolutionary/GA, systematic search) paired with energy ranking schemes, this report situates GA/evolutionary methods within the broader competitive landscape and shows what accuracy in searching and ranking is achievable on realistic molecules. It links directly to modern developments seen elsewhere in this list: dispersion-corrected DFT ranking, distributed-multipole potentials, and machine-learning energy models, all of which have been driven by the gaps exposed in these blind tests.

Take away: Internalize that CSP is a two-part problem, thorough conformational and packing search plus accurate relative energy ranking, and that the blind test framework (predicting from connectivity alone, then comparing to experiment on progressively harder molecules) is the standard by which any method, GA-based or otherwise, must prove itself.
