High-Resolution Image Synthesis with Latent Diffusion Models
Robin Rombach, Andreas Blattmann, Lorenz, Dominik et al.  (2021, 736 citations, arXiv (Cornell University))

Worth 30 minutes? Latent Diffusion is the paper behind Stable Diffusion. Its core idea is to train the denoising diffusion model in the compressed latent space of a pretrained autoencoder rather than on raw pixels. This cut compute sharply while keeping quality. Cross-attention conditioning made the model controllable by text or other inputs. It is a landmark of the diffusion era, and its two-stage design now recurs across generative modelling for science.

What it could inspire: Diffusing in a learned latent space suggests a route for molecular crystals. An autoencoder could compress the packing (cell, orientations, positions) of known CSD structures, and a diffusion prior over that latent could propose candidates. These could seed GAtor or Genarris populations in place of random generation. Cross-attention conditioning on the molecular graph or the space group suggests molecule-conditioned generation of polymorphs, ranked afterwards by dispersion-corrected DFT.

Foundations to learn: Diffusion models as a sequence of denoising autoencoders. The score-based, variational-bound view of training. Perceptual compression versus semantic compression, and how the latent downsampling factor trades fidelity for compute. Regularising the latent space (a KL penalty or vector quantisation). Conditioning via cross-attention and classifier-free style guidance. Why separating representation learning from generative modelling helps, a pattern shared with MatterGen-style crystal generators.

30-minute reading guide: Read the abstract and introduction, focusing on the figure comparing perceptual and semantic compression (about 8 minutes). Then read the method section on autoencoder regularisation, the latent diffusion objective and the cross-attention conditioning block diagram (12 minutes). Skim the experiments on downsampling factor versus quality and speed (7 minutes). Skip the inpainting and super-resolution details, the long tables and the appendix.
