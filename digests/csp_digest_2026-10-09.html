Accurate Modeling of Organic Molecular Crystals by Dispersion-Corrected Density Functional Tight Binding (DFTB)
Jan Gerit Brandenburg, Stefan Grimme  (2014, 202 citations)

Why it's foundational: This paper directly addresses the central challenge of organic molecular crystal structure prediction: how to rank candidate structures accurately while remaining computationally tractable. It benchmarks dispersion-corrected density functional tight binding (DFTB) against the gold-standard DFT-D for organic crystals, establishing a method that bridges the accuracy-versus-cost gap that dominates every CSP workflow. Because any search method, including genetic algorithms, ultimately depends on reliably scoring and relaxing the many generated candidate structures, the energy evaluation layer studied here is a cornerstone of practical CSP.

How it connects: Modern CSP pipelines separate structure generation (sampling the crystal packing landscape via genetic/evolutionary algorithms, random search, or quasi-random methods) from structure ranking (lattice energy evaluation). GA/evolutionary methods generate and evolve large populations of trial crystals, and each must be locally optimized and energetically ranked; the dispersion-corrected DFTB approach provides the efficient, accurate energetics needed to make such high-throughput evaluation feasible. This connects to current work on machine-learned potentials and fragment-based energies (e.g. GAP-style and ML fragment energies) that pursue the same efficiency-accuracy tradeoff for scoring CSP candidates.

Take away: The real bottleneck in molecular CSP is accurate yet affordable lattice-energy evaluation; dispersion corrections are essential, and intermediate-cost quantum methods like DFTB-D can approach DFT-D accuracy to enable screening of the many structures that generation methods such as genetic algorithms produce.
