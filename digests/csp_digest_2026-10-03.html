Prediction of inorganic crystal framework structures
Scott M. Woodley, Peter D. Battle, Julian D. Gale et al.  (2004, 42 citations)

Why it's foundational: This paper demonstrates a genetic algorithm applied directly to crystal structure prediction, generating framework structures from minimal input (unit cell dimensions, constituent atoms) using an exclusion-zone strategy to bias the GA search toward physically meaningful packings. For a researcher focused on GA/evolutionary CSP, it is a clear methodological cornerstone: it shows how the core evolutionary machinery (population, selection, recombination, mutation) is adapted to the specific problem of placing atoms/building units within a periodic cell to find low-energy structures. The companion 2003 excluded-regions paper (b312526b) develops the same idea; this one extends and validates it.

How it connects: Modern CSP still rests on the same two-part logic this work embodies: a global search engine for exploring configurational space plus an energy ranking of candidate structures. The GA/evolutionary branch of CSP (and related evolutionary approaches to materials structure search) descends directly from the kind of population-based optimization shown here, where constraints and problem-specific encodings guide sampling. It pairs naturally with other items in this map: quasi-random sampling (acs.jctc.5b01112) as an alternative search strategy, dispersion-corrected DFT and tight-binding methods (ct050189a, jz500755u) as the energy-ranking stage, and machine-learned potentials (physrevlett.104.136403, c7sc04665k, d0sc05765g, acs.jctc.9b00038) as modern accelerators for the costly evaluation step that any GA-based search must perform many times.

Take away: CSP is fundamentally a constrained global optimization problem: a genetic/evolutionary algorithm can efficiently search the vast space of atomic arrangements within a unit cell when guided by sensible problem-specific constraints (such as excluded regions), and the quality of the final structures depends jointly on the search strategy and the accuracy of the energy ranking.
