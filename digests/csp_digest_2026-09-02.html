Crystal structure prediction using ab initio evolutionary techniques: Principles and applications
Artem R. Oganov, Colin W. Glass  (2006, 2546 citations)

Why it's foundational: This paper introduces the merger of ab initio total-energy calculations with a purpose-built evolutionary algorithm for crystal structure prediction, establishing one of the defining evolutionary methodologies in the field (the approach later embodied in USPEX). It states that the method reliably predicts the most stable structure along with a set of low-energy metastable ones, which is precisely the capability a researcher focused on GA/evolutionary CSP needs to understand at its origin.

How it connects: It sits at the heart of the evolutionary CSP lineage that this researcher works in, connecting directly to related evolutionary and swarm methods (e.g. the PSO approach in paper 2 and the explanatory review in paper 6, 'How Evolutionary Crystal Structure Prediction Works'). It underpins modern work where evolutionary global optimization is coupled to increasingly accurate energy models, including the dispersion-corrected DFT and machine-learned potentials that later CSP papers build upon. Understanding the algorithmic principles here frames how genetic operators, population evolution, and energy ranking are combined in current molecular and inorganic CSP.

Take away: CSP can be posed as a global optimization problem over the energy landscape, and a specifically designed evolutionary algorithm coupled to accurate energy evaluation can efficiently locate not just the global-minimum structure but a family of low-energy metastable structures.
