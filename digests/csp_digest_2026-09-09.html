Global Optimization of Clusters, Crystals, and Biomolecules
David J. Wales, Harold A. Scheraga  (1999, 1127 citations)

Why it's foundational: This is a defining review of global optimization applied across clusters, crystals, and biomolecules, framing crystal structure prediction as the search for the global minimum of a potential energy function. It sits at the intellectual root of the entire CSP field by articulating the central computational challenge that all subsequent methods, including genetic and evolutionary algorithms, are designed to solve.

How it connects: Modern molecular CSP, whether using genetic algorithms, evolutionary schemes, or particle-swarm optimization (as in later works listed here), inherits the global-optimization framing this paper describes. The GA and evolutionary methods central to the researcher's profile are precisely the stochastic search strategies developed to attack the rugged, high-dimensional energy landscapes this paper surveys, connecting directly to Holland's adaptation ideas and to later benchmark and dispersion-corrected energy-ranking work in the list.

Take away: CSP is fundamentally a global optimization problem: predicting a crystal structure means locating the global (and competitive local) minima of a potential energy surface, and the quality of predictions hinges on both the search algorithm and the accuracy of the energy function used to rank candidates.
