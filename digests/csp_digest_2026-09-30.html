Improved real-space genetic algorithm for crystal structure and polymorph prediction
Nathan Luke Abraham, Matt Probert  (2008, 43 citations)

Why it's foundational: This paper directly targets the researcher's core interest: genetic-algorithm crystal structure and polymorph prediction. Among the listed papers it is the one that develops the GA methodology itself in real space, rather than applying CSP to a single system or reviewing crystal engineering. It confronts a defining practical problem of evolutionary CSP, namely stagnation during evolution when the population collapses toward similar structures, and introduces a mechanism to penalize similar structures to maintain diversity and thereby improve both efficiency and accuracy of the search.

How it connects: Modern molecular CSP relies on global optimization over an enormous configurational landscape of lattice parameters, molecular positions, orientations, and conformations. GA/evolutionary methods encode candidate packings as chromosomes and evolve them via selection, crossover, and mutation. The diversity-preservation idea in this paper, penalizing near-duplicate structures, is a recurring theme that reappears in fingerprint-based niching and structure-similarity filters used across evolutionary CSP and inorganic evolutionary codes. It also connects to complementary sampling strategies in the list (quasi-random sampling, doi 10.1021/acs.jctc.5b01112) and to the energy-ranking bottleneck addressed by dispersion-corrected DFT and machine-learned potentials (e.g. dois 10.1021/jz500755u, 10.1039/c7sc04665k, 10.1039/d0sc05765g, 10.1021/acs.jctc.9b00038), which supply the energies the GA optimizes.

Take away: The central lesson is that a good evolutionary CSP search is not just about the energy function but about preserving population diversity: penalizing similar structures prevents premature convergence (stagnation) and is essential to efficiently and accurately locate low-energy polymorphs across a rugged landscape.
