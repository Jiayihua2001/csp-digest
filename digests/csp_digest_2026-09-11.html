An adaptive genetic algorithm for crystal structure prediction
Shunqing Wu, Min Ji, Chong Wang et al.  (2013, 194 citations)

Why it's foundational: This paper presents a genetic algorithm specifically designed for crystal structure prediction, which is exactly the intersection of the researcher's stated focus: evolutionary methods applied to CSP. It defines a defining methodological strategy, combining fast classical potentials for broad structural exploration with the accuracy of density functional theory in an adaptive, iterative loop. This adaptive GA framework addresses the central tension in CSP: the need to search an enormous configurational space cheaply while ranking candidates accurately, making it a cornerstone method rather than a narrow application.

How it connects: The abstract situates the work within the broad GA/evolutionary paradigm for structure search, sharing conceptual DNA with Holland's foundational work on adaptation (paper 20) and standing alongside alternative global optimizers such as particle-swarm optimization (paper 2). The adaptive coupling of cheap potentials with DFT prefigures modern machine-learning-accelerated CSP workflows (papers 28, 33, 44), where surrogate models replace or augment classical potentials to reduce expensive quantum calculations. For a researcher focused on molecular CSP with evolutionary methods, this bridges the algorithmic core (crossover, mutation, selection over structural populations) to the accuracy-versus-cost problem that dominates current CSP practice.

Take away: The key idea to internalize is the adaptive, iterative marriage of fast approximate energy evaluation for exploration and accurate quantum-mechanical evaluation for ranking, orchestrated by a genetic algorithm so that the search efficiently converges on low-energy structures without wasting expensive calculations on poor candidates.
