Metastable high-pressure single-bonded phases of nitrogen predicted via genetic algorithm
Yansun Yao, John S. Tse, К. Tanaka et al.  (2008, 78 citations)

Why it's foundational: This paper is one of the defining demonstrations of the genetic-algorithm approach to crystal structure prediction combined with first-principles structural optimization. The abstract states that a genetic algorithm for CSP is coupled with ab initio relaxation, starting from a population of randomly generated structures that are then evolved. This is exactly the evolutionary/GA methodology at the heart of the researcher's profile, presented as a general predictive engine rather than a narrow one-off application. It illustrates the core machinery: population initialization, energy-based ranking via quantum mechanical optimization, and iterative evolution toward stable and metastable structures.

How it connects: The GA-plus-first-principles-relaxation paradigm shown here is the direct ancestor of modern evolutionary CSP workflows (e.g. the population-based search strategies now standard in the field). It links tightly to the broader CSP landscape represented elsewhere in this list, where the search step (sampling packing arrangements) is paired with an accurate energy ranking step (dispersion-corrected DFT, DFTB, and increasingly machine-learned potentials). For a researcher focused on GA/evolutionary molecular CSP, this is the conceptual bridge from random or quasi-random sampling to guided evolutionary optimization, and it motivates why later work emphasizes both better search heuristics and better energy models.

Take away: Internalize the two-part logic of evolutionary CSP: a genetic algorithm evolves a population of candidate structures while first-principles (or otherwise accurate) energy minimization scores and relaxes each candidate, so that fitness-driven selection steers the search toward the most stable and interesting metastable packings from random starting points.
