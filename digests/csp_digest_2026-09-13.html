Crystal structure prediction via particle-swarm optimization
Yanchao Wang, Jian Lv, Li Zhu et al.  (2010, 2741 citations)

Why it's foundational: This paper introduces a general-purpose crystal structure prediction method built on population-based global optimization within the evolutionary scheme, using particle-swarm optimization. As the abstract explicitly contrasts PSO with the genetic algorithm and its evolution operators, it defines and situates the entire family of evolutionary CSP methods that a GA/evolutionary CSP researcher must understand. It is a landmark method paper (over 2700 citations) that establishes prediction of structures from scratch as a tractable global optimization problem.

How it connects: Evolutionary and swarm-based CSP methods share the same core machinery: encode candidate structures, evaluate them via energy minimization, and iteratively evolve a population toward the global minimum. Understanding PSO in direct comparison to genetic algorithms clarifies what evolution operators (crossover, mutation, selection) actually contribute and where population-based searches converge or fail. This connects directly to modern GA-based CSP engines and to newer work that couples such global searches with machine-learned potentials and DFT ranking, which appear elsewhere in this list.

Take away: Crystal structure prediction can be cast as a population-based global optimization over the energy landscape, and genetic algorithms are one instance of a broader evolutionary strategy for efficiently navigating that landscape from scratch, without prior structural assumptions.
