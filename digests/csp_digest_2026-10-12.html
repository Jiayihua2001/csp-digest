Crystal structure prediction for cyclotrimethylene trinitramine (RDX) from first principles
Rafał Podeszwa, Betsy M. Rice, Krzysztof Szalewicz et al.  (2009, 60 citations)

Why it's foundational: Among the listed papers, this is the clearest example of an end-to-end molecular crystal structure prediction study: it applies CSP together with a first-principles-derived potential energy function to a real molecule (RDX) and ranks the stability of competing polymorphs. It demonstrates the core CSP workflow of generating candidate packings and scoring them by lattice energy from a transferable potential, which is exactly the problem that GA and evolutionary CSP methods are built to solve.

How it connects: Modern molecular CSP revolves around two coupled challenges that this paper exercises directly: (1) exhaustively searching the space of crystal packings and conformations, and (2) ranking candidate structures by relative energy accurately enough to identify observed polymorphs. Genetic and evolutionary algorithms are a leading strategy for the search half, treating unit cell parameters, molecular position/orientation, and conformation as a genome to evolve toward low-energy packings. The energy-ranking half shown here (nonempirical potential energy functions) connects to the dispersion-corrected DFT and force-field work also in this list (B3LYP-D, DFT+dispersion lattice energies), and to the polymorph-enumeration spirit of ROY studies. Together these define the landscape that current ML-potential and blind-test CSP work continues to push on.

Take away: CSP is a two-part problem: thoroughly sample the many possible crystal packings and conformations, then rank them by accurate relative lattice energies; getting a wrong polymorph ordering usually traces back to one of these two steps, and GA/evolutionary methods are the engine for the sampling step.
