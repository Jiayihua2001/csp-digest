Reliable and practical computational description of molecular crystal polymorphs
Johannes Hoja, Hsin-Yu Ko, Marcus A. Neumann et al.  (2019, 205 citations)

Why it's foundational: This paper directly addresses the central goal of molecular CSP: reliably predicting the polymorphic energy landscape of an organic molecular crystal. Unlike the many narrow applications, benchmark-style single-molecule studies, or crystal-engineering reviews in this list, it explicitly frames the challenge of computationally describing molecular crystal polymorphs and proposes a reliable and practical protocol for doing so, which is exactly the core problem a CSP researcher must understand.

How it connects: It sits at the heart of modern CSP practice, where a global search (often driven by genetic/evolutionary or quasi-random sampling methods) generates candidate structures that must then be ranked by accurate lattice energies. The abstract emphasizes reliable and practical computational description of polymorphs, the ranking/energetics step that any evolutionary search feeds into. It connects to the search-plus-ranking pipeline shared with GA approaches (e.g. hard-sphere and grain-boundary GA papers here), to dispersion-corrected DFT ranking methods (B3LYP-D*, DFTB-D), to machine-learning energy models for crystals, and to blind-test and drug-development motivations that drive current work.

Take away: The decisive bottleneck in molecular CSP is not just generating candidate structures but reliably and affordably ranking their relative energies, since the true polymorph must emerge from a dense, near-degenerate energy landscape; a trustworthy energy model is what makes any search method, including evolutionary/GA searches, actually predictive.
