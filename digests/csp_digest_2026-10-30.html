📚 Landmark of the day — Molecular-CSP classic
Efficient Reconstruction of Complex Free Energy Landscapes by Multiple Walkers Metadynamics
Paolo Raiteri, Alessandro Laio, Francesco Luigi Gervasio  (2005, 616 citations)

Worth 30 minutes? Of this list, it is the only paper offering transferable sampling theory rather than reviews or case studies. Multiple-walker metadynamics showed that many replicas can share and build one history-dependent bias potential, so free-energy reconstruction parallelizes almost linearly. It made metadynamics practical on clusters and became standard in PLUMED-style enhanced sampling. That includes polymorph-transition and nucleation studies that go beyond static lattice energies.

What it could inspire: For MCSP: use free-energy rather than 0 K lattice energies for final ranking, and study polymorph interconversion. Shared-bias walkers resemble a GA population sharing a fitness memory, which suggests tabu or bias-sharing niching across GAtor replicas. In AI4Sci it is the classical baseline that Boltzmann generators and learned collective variables aim to beat, so you should know it.

Foundations to learn: History-dependent biasing: repeatedly deposit Gaussians on collective variables (CVs), and the accumulated bias converges toward the negative free-energy surface. Key ideas are error control via Gaussian height and deposition rate, convergence and the CV-choice problem, and the parallel-sampling argument for sharing a bias. This is background for the later well-tempered metadynamics, OPES, and machine-learned CVs.

30-minute reading guide: Spend 10 min on the introduction and the method recap: how the bias is built and why the walkers share it. Spend 10 min on the error and efficiency analysis, especially how the error scales with the number of walkers, plus the main free-energy-convergence figures. Spend 5 min on the test system results. Skim the technical implementation details. Use the last 5 min to sketch how a shared bias could map onto a crystal-polymorph CV such as lattice parameters.
