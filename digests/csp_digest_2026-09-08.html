Crystal structure prediction from first principles
Scott M. Woodley, C. Richard A. Catlow  (2008, 741 citations)

Why it's foundational: This 2008 Nature Materials paper, 'Crystal structure prediction from first principles,' is a defining statement of the modern evolutionary-algorithm approach to predicting crystal structures purely from chemical composition. It established the paradigm that global optimization over the energy landscape, coupled with first-principles energetics, can reliably locate stable and metastable structures without prior experimental input. Its high citation count and its role in launching evolutionary CSP methodology mark it as a genuine cornerstone rather than a narrow application.

How it connects: The evolutionary/genetic-algorithm philosophy it articulates underpins essentially all modern GA-based CSP work, including the adaptive GA (10.1088/0953-8984/26/3/035402), particle-swarm variants (10.1103/physrevb.82.094116) that are framed explicitly as alternatives within the same evolutionary scheme, GA methods for interfaces and hard-sphere systems (10.1038/nmat2712, 10.1103/physreve.79.046714), and the broader global-optimization tradition (10.1126/science.285.5432.1368, Holland's foundational GA text 10.1137/1018105). It also connects forward to the energy-ranking and dispersion-corrected electronic-structure methods (e.g. 10.1021/acs.chemrev.5b00648) and machine-learned potentials (10.1103/physrevlett.104.136403, 10.1039/d0sc05765g) that now supply the fast, accurate energies these searches require.

Take away: CSP can be posed as a global optimization problem over the potential energy surface where evolutionary operators intelligently sample crystal packing space, so that stable structures emerge from composition alone; internalize the coupling of a smart search algorithm with first-principles energetics as the core engine of structure prediction.
