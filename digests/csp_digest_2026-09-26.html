Energy Ranking of Molecular Crystals Using Density Functional Theory Calculations and an Empirical van der Waals Correction
Marcus A. Neumann, Marc‐Antoine Perrin  (2005, 295 citations)

Why it's foundational: This 2005 paper addresses the central bottleneck of molecular CSP: reliably ranking the energies of candidate crystal structures so that the experimentally observed polymorph is identified as low in energy. It combines high-level DFT with an empirical van der Waals correction to achieve accurate structure optimization and energy ranking of molecular crystals, defining the hybrid dispersion-corrected DFT approach that underpins essentially all modern first-principles CSP workflows.

How it connects: In any evolutionary or genetic-algorithm CSP pipeline, the search (GA generation, crossover, mutation of trial packings) must be paired with an accurate fitness function that ranks candidates by lattice energy. This paper supplies exactly that energetic backbone. Its dispersion-corrected DFT philosophy connects directly to later methods in the list such as DFT-D lattice energy work (ct050189a, jz500755u DFTB), machine-learned potentials and fragment energies (c7sc04665k, d0sc05765g, acs.jctc.9b00038) that aim to reproduce this accuracy at lower cost, and to blind-test-style landscape mining (c9sc02832c, ncomms8793). Understanding why accurate dispersion-corrected ranking matters also frames the persistent challenge of conformational energy errors in flexible molecules (acs.cgd.0c00676).

Take away: CSP success hinges as much on an accurate, dispersion-aware energy ranking function as on the search algorithm itself; a genetic algorithm can only find the right polymorph if the underlying energy model correctly orders the closely spaced polymorph energies.
