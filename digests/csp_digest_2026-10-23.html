Materials informatics based on evolutionary algorithms: Application to search for superconducting hydrogen compounds
Takahiro Ishikawa, Takashi Miyake, Katsuya Shimizu et al.  (2019, 60 citations)

Why it's foundational: Among the listed papers, this is the clearest demonstration of an evolutionary/genetic-algorithm approach to crystal structure prediction, which is precisely the researcher's focus. It lays out a structured, multi-stage GA framework (data collection, genetic algorithm plus genetic programming, property modeling) applied to discovering stable crystalline compounds. Unlike the many application-specific polymorph, cocrystal, and force-field papers in the list, it directly addresses the search-and-optimization machinery that defines GA-based CSP.

How it connects: Modern molecular CSP relies on global optimization over the energy landscape of candidate packings, and genetic/evolutionary algorithms are a central strategy for exploring that vast configurational space. This paper exemplifies how GA and genetic programming are combined with materials informatics to navigate chemical and structural space toward stable targets, a workflow that parallels evolutionary molecular crystal packing searches. It also bridges to current trends by integrating data-driven property prediction with evolutionary search, foreshadowing the hybrid ML-plus-GA approaches now common in CSP (see also the deep-learning-augmented GA work in the list).

Take away: Internalize the core idea that structure/compound discovery can be framed as an evolutionary optimization problem: iteratively generate, evaluate, and recombine candidate structures guided by an energy or property objective, which is the backbone of genetic-algorithm-based crystal structure prediction.
