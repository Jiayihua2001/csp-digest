Revisiting the Blind Tests in Crystal Structure Prediction: Accurate Energy Ranking of Molecular Crystals
Aldi Asmadi, Marcus A. Neumann, John Kendrick et al.  (2009, 79 citations)

Why it's foundational: This paper revisits the CCDC blind tests, the community-wide benchmark exercises that define how success in molecular CSP is measured. It reports a hybrid DFT/MM approach that correctly ranked each of the four 2007 blind-test experimental structures as lowest in lattice energy, tying a concrete methodological advance to the field's canonical evaluation framework. For a researcher building core knowledge, understanding the blind test paradigm and the central challenge of accurate energy ranking is essential.

How it connects: The blind test framework is the shared yardstick against which all modern CSP methods, including genetic-algorithm and evolutionary approaches, are judged. Evolutionary/GA search generates candidate crystal structures, but the decisive step is ranking them by lattice energy to identify the observed polymorph; this paper foregrounds exactly that ranking problem. It links directly to later dispersion-corrected DFT work (papers 18, 30, 31, 42), machine-learning energy models (25, 39), and quasi-random/sampling search strategies (38) that all aim to improve either the search or the energy discrimination that blind tests probe.

Take away: Generating candidate structures is only half of CSP; the harder, decisive problem is ranking them by lattice energy accurately enough to single out the real polymorph, and blind tests are the benchmark that validates whether a method (search plus energetics) actually works.
