The Supramolecular Synthon Approach to Crystal Structure Prediction
J.A.R.P. Sarma, Gautam R. Desiraju  (2002, 117 citations)

Why it's foundational: This paper directly addresses ab initio crystal structure prediction of small organic molecules and was contributed as part of a Cambridge Crystallographic Data Centre blind test on CSP. The CSP blind tests are the community-defining benchmarking exercises for the entire field, and a method paper submitted to one of them sits at the methodological core of molecular CSP rather than being a narrow application. It defines a concrete strategy (the supramolecular synthon approach) for generating and ranking candidate packings.

How it connects: The core CSP workflow it embodies (generating candidate structures then ranking by energetics) is exactly the skeleton that modern genetic-algorithm and evolutionary CSP methods automate: an evolutionary search replaces the manual synthon-guided generation of trial structures, while lattice-energy ranking (later refined by dispersion-corrected DFT, see the DFT-D energy-ranking work in this list) provides the fitness function. Understanding the blind-test framing also lets the researcher situate later GA/evolutionary tools, quasi-random sampling searches, and machine-learning-accelerated CSP within the same benchmark-driven lineage.

Take away: CSP is fundamentally a two-part problem, generation of plausible candidate packings and reliable energetic ranking of them, and progress is measured against community blind tests; a genetic/evolutionary method is essentially a smarter engine for the generation-and-selection loop that this approach frames by hand.
