## Why it matters
Synthesizes and taxonomizes over seventy studies (2020–2026) into a unified framework of uncertainty-driven active-learning strategies—on-the-fly, fine-tuning, kernel-based, and reaction-aware—for data-efficient interatomic potential development

## Abstract
> Developing a machine learning interatomic potential (MLIP) that is accurate for the specific conditions under which it will be applied, rather than simply for the predefined configurations chosen by the model developer, is a significant challenge in atomistic simulation. Active learning (AL) addresses this challenge by closing the loop between candidate generation, data selection, reference calculation, and model updating. Here, we survey more than seventy studies, with an emphasis on work from 2020 to 2026, to understand how active learning has evolved from a niche, on-the-fly training technique for query-by-committee and Gaussian process force fields into a diverse family of data-efficient strategies. he reviewed examples include on-the-fly molecular dynamics, active data selection for fine-tuning pretrained models, kernel- and representation-based acquisition using frozen pretrained models, and reaction-aware sampling for chemical systems far from equilibrium.
