# Pharos: Learning to Control Rare Events at Scale

## Why it matters
Stochastic optimal control framework jointly learning committor (transition probability) and steering bias, yielding rate predictions, transfer across ion-assembly systems, millisecond host-guest binding, and MLIP reaction studies.

**Judge:** Strong group, concrete multi-system validation of committor-plus-control learning; a foundational-leaning rare-event method relevant to polymorph transitions and nucleation kinetics.

## Abstract
> Rare events drive transformations across physics, chemistry and biology, yet predicting their mechanisms and rates remains a fundamental challenge. Here we introduce Pharos, a framework that learns to make rare transitions accessible while predicting how frequently they occur under physical dynamics. Through stochastic optimal control, Pharos jointly learns the probability of completing a transition and how to steer simulations toward it, allowing sampling and learning to improve one another. Across biomolecular benchmarks, Pharos learns accurate transition probabilities with order-of-magnitude gains in convergence speed over previous algorithms. It transfers across ion assembly systems in explicit solvent while maintaining accurate reaction rate predictions, scales to host-guest binding systems that span millisecond timescales, and enables chemical reaction studies with a computationally demanding machine learning interatomic potential. By combining efficient sampling, quantitative kinetics, and transferability, Pharos opens a route toward foundation models of rare event mechanisms and rates across molecular systems.
