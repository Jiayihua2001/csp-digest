## Abstract
> Despite major advances in structure prediction, modelling the motions and interactions that drive biomolecular function at scale remains a central challenge. We introduce BioEmu2, a generative model for atomically resolved biomolecular structures combining static structure prediction, equilibrium sampling and time evolution, that samples orders of magnitude faster than molecular dynamics (MD). Trained on experimental and synthetic structures and extensive MD data, it supports multi-component systems containing proteins, nucleic acids, modified residues, ligands and ions. Structure-prediction accuracy is comparable to top open-weight biomolecular folding models, and a context parallel mode enables prediction of megadalton complexes. Equilibrium distributions approximate MD references with free-energy errors near 1 kcal/mol across tested proteins and complexes. Time propagation predicts long-timescale dynamical trajectories and kinetics of binding and conformational changes. Generated structures can be controlled by conditioning on residue contacts or steering with user-defined bias potentials. Enhanced sampling enables free energy calculation involving rare states.
