📚 Landmark of the day — AI for Science landmark
Highly accurate protein structure prediction with AlphaFold
John Jumper, Richard Evans, Alexander Pritzel et al.  (2021, 43588 citations, Nature)

Worth 30 minutes? AlphaFold 2 turned protein structure prediction, a 50-year grand challenge, into a largely solved problem. It reached near-experimental accuracy and set the template for AI-for-science: physics-aware architecture, end-to-end differentiable structure output, and learned confidence. Later work such as AF3, RFdiffusion and generative crystal models builds on its ideas. It is the reference point for any claim that ML can predict 3D structure.

What it could inspire: For MCSP: learned confidence scores (pLDDT-like) for ranking or pruning GA candidates before DFT, and equivariant structure modules that output coordinates directly. Iterative recycling is analogous to GA refinement. Co-evolution-like priors could be replaced by CSD packing statistics. It also raises the question of whether an end-to-end learned generator plus a learned confidence score could replace random structure generation in Genarris.

Foundations to learn: Attention over pair representations and triangle multiplicative updates, which enforce geometric consistency. Invariant point attention as an SE(3)-equivariant way to update rigid frames. FAPE loss, which compares local frames. Recycling and self-distillation. Calibrated per-residue confidence prediction. Using evolutionary covariation (MSA) as an inductive bias.

30-minute reading guide: Read the abstract and Fig. 1 for the accuracy context (5 min). Then Fig. 3 and the main-text description of the Evoformer and structure module (10 min). Next, the confidence and ablation figures (8 min). Finish with the Supplementary sections on IPA and FAPE (7 min). Skip the CASP-specific benchmarking details and the MSA database construction.
