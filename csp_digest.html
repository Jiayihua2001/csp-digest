Finding the low-energy structures of Si[001] symmetric tilted grain boundaries with a genetic algorithm
Jian Zhang, Cai‐Zhuang Wang, Kai‐Ming Ho et al.  (2009, 64 citations)

Why it's foundational: This paper presents a global structure optimization scheme built on a genetic algorithm for predicting low-energy crystalline atomic arrangements, which is exactly the methodological heart of evolutionary CSP. Although the test system is Si grain boundaries rather than molecular crystals, the paper explicitly frames the genetic algorithm as a fast and efficient method for structure prediction and demonstrates that it recovers not only the most stable structure but also a family of low-energy metastable structures. That ability to map out a set of competitive low-energy minima from random starts is the defining task of CSP, and the GA machinery (populations, selection, crossover, mutation driving a search over configuration space) is the transferable cornerstone concept.

How it connects: Modern molecular CSP and evolutionary search codes all rest on the same principle demonstrated here: treat crystal structure determination as a global optimization problem over lattice and atomic/molecular degrees of freedom, and use an evolutionary/genetic algorithm to escape local minima and sample many competitive polymorphs. The emphasis on retrieving low-energy metastable structures connects directly to polymorphism prediction (relevant to the ROY studies and blind-test style CSP in this list, e.g. the ROY CSP work at 10.1039/c8fd00039e) and to broader evolutionary materials informatics (e.g. the superconducting-hydride GA search at 10.1103/physrevb.100.174506 and the deep-learning-augmented GA at 10.1063/5.0057162). For a researcher focused on GA/evolutionary molecular CSP, this is the clearest crystalline-structure demonstration of the core search engine, which can then be specialized by adding rigid-body molecular descriptors, space-group sampling, and accurate lattice energies (as in the dispersion-corrected DFT and force-field papers listed).

Take away: Internalize that crystal structure prediction is a global optimization problem and that a genetic/evolutionary algorithm is a practical, efficient way to search the energy landscape and return not just the single most stable structure but the whole set of low-energy (metastable/polymorphic) candidates.
