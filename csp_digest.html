Prediction of the crystal structures of axitinib, a polymorphic pharmaceutical molecule
Manolis Vasileiadis, Constantinos C. Pantelides, Claire S. Adjiman et al.  (2014, 80 citations)

Why it's foundational: This paper directly addresses the core task of the researcher's field: predicting the polymorphs of a flexible organic pharmaceutical molecule (axitinib) from first principles. Unlike narrow applications that merely characterize an observed form, it demonstrates the full CSP workflow of generating and ranking candidate crystal structures across multiple possible polymorphs, which is the defining challenge of molecular CSP. It is explicitly framed around the general problem statement that organic molecules can crystallize in multiple structures with very different physical and mechanical properties, and that predicting them is a challenge with great potential benefits.

How it connects: Molecular CSP for flexible, pharmaceutically relevant molecules is precisely where global search methods such as genetic and evolutionary algorithms earn their value, because the configurational space (conformation, packing, space group, Z') is enormous and must be sampled efficiently. The axitinib case is a canonical hard example involving conformational flexibility, which is exactly the regime that stochastic/evolutionary search and later machine-learned ranking methods target. It connects to the blind-test benchmark culture (see POLY59 and the ROY studies in this set) and to modern lattice-energy ranking via dispersion-corrected methods, situating GA-style structure generation within the broader predict-then-rank pipeline.

Take away: Internalize the two-stage logic of molecular CSP: exhaustively generate plausible packing arrangements (including multiple molecular conformations) via an efficient global search, then rank them by accurate lattice energy to identify which polymorphs are likely to appear. The difficulty and payoff both scale with molecular flexibility, which is what motivates evolutionary/genetic search strategies.
