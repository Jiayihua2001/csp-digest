A method for the prediction of the crystal structure of ionic organic compounds—the crystal structures of o-toluidinium chloride and bromide and polymorphism of bicifadine hydrochloride
Patrick McArdle, Karen Gilligan, Desmond Cunningham et al.  (2004, 141 citations)

Why it's foundational: This paper presents a concrete method for predicting crystal structures of ionic organic compounds, demonstrated on real systems (o-toluidinium halides and polymorphic bicifadine hydrochloride) that differ in both molecular conformation and packing mode. Among the listed papers, it is the one most directly centered on molecular/organic crystal structure prediction as a methodology, combining crystallisation studies, quantum mechanical calculations, and prediction of competing packings - the core workflow of CSP.

How it connects: Modern molecular CSP rests on exactly the challenges this paper engages: searching over molecular conformations and packing arrangements and ranking candidate structures by lattice energy. Evolutionary and genetic-algorithm CSP methods automate this search over the conformational and packing degrees of freedom highlighted here. The handling of ionic/salt systems and conformational polymorphism also links to the broader CSP literature in this list (ROY blind-test style work, conformational polymorphism reviews, dispersion-corrected lattice-energy methods) that defines what a GA-based search must reproduce.

Take away: CSP of molecular (and ionic organic) solids is fundamentally a global search over molecular conformation plus crystal packing, scored by lattice energy - internalize that the problem couples flexible-molecule conformational variation with packing optimization, which is precisely why global optimizers like genetic/evolutionary algorithms are suited to it.
