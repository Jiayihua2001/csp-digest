Knowledge-based model of hydrogen-bonding propensity in organic crystals
Peter T. A. Galek, L. Fábián, W. D. Samuel Motherwell et al.  (2007, 170 citations)

Why it's foundational: For a researcher focused on molecular (organic) crystal structure prediction, this paper presents a knowledge-based, statistically grounded method (the logit hydrogen-bonding propensity model) derived from the Cambridge Structural Database to predict which donors and acceptors actually form hydrogen bonds in a crystal. Hydrogen bonding is the dominant structure-directing interaction in organic crystals, and a defensible, CSD-trained way to rank likely packing motifs is exactly the kind of core, generalizable tool that underpins molecular CSP rather than a narrow one-off application. Among the listed papers it is the one most directly about predicting organic crystal structure determinants in a transferable, database-driven way.

How it connects: Modern molecular CSP pipelines generate large pools of candidate structures and must rank or filter them; knowledge-based propensity models like this one provide a complementary, data-derived prior to the lattice-energy minimization and dispersion-corrected DFT ranking seen in related entries. For GA and evolutionary CSP specifically, hydrogen-bonding propensity gives domain knowledge that can seed populations, bias crossover and mutation toward physically sensible packings, and prune implausible candidates, improving search efficiency. It also sits in the same CSD-statistics tradition that now feeds machine-learning descriptors and is validated against hard cases such as the many polymorphs of ROY discussed elsewhere in this set.

Take away: Statistical analysis of the experimentally observed structures in the CSD can be turned into a predictive, probabilistic model of which intermolecular interactions (here hydrogen bonds) will form, giving CSP a data-grounded prior to guide and rank candidate crystal structures.
