📚 Landmark of the day — Molecular-CSP classic
Scaling deep learning for materials discovery
Amil Merchant, Simon Batzner, Samuel S. Schoenholz  (2023, 1511 citations)

Worth 30 minutes? GNoME is one of the landmark AI-for-science papers the reader explicitly asked for. It showed that scaling graph networks with active learning against DFT turns crystal discovery into a data flywheel, and the reported number of new stable inorganic crystals is far beyond earlier efforts. It reset expectations for ML-guided structure generation and stability screening, and it set off the debate on novelty and synthesizability that followed.

What it could inspire: For MCSP it suggests a GA or Genarris loop where an ML energy model is retrained on every DFT-ranked batch, with candidate generation scaled up aggressively. The paper's two pipelines (substitution and random structure search) map onto GA mutation and crossover. It also prompts the question of whether neural scaling laws on energy accuracy could carry over to molecular crystals with dispersion-corrected DFT labels.

Foundations to learn: Key ideas: the convex hull and decomposition energy as the measure of stability; active learning and data flywheels; neural scaling laws in GNNs (error falls with data); message-passing interatomic networks; out-of-distribution generalization; and the difference between predicted stability and experimental realizability (the later critiques are worth knowing).

30-minute reading guide: Start with the abstract and Fig. 1 (the active-learning pipeline and the growth in discoveries). Next read the scaling-law figure on prediction error versus data, then the generation strategies (SAPS substitutions and AIRSS random search). Skim the materials-property and MLIP sections. Skip the Methods hyperparameters. Spend the last 5 minutes on how to evaluate novelty and stability claims.
