B3LYP augmented with an empirical dispersion term (B3LYP-D*) as applied to molecular crystals
Bartolomeo Civalleri, Claudio Marcelo Zicovich-Wilson, Loredana Valenzano et al.  (2007, 920 citations)

Why it's foundational: For molecular crystal structure prediction, the central bottleneck is ranking candidate crystal packings by their relative lattice (cohesive) energies with enough accuracy to distinguish polymorphs that often differ by only a few kJ/mol. This paper addresses exactly that: it shows that a standard density functional (B3LYP) augmented with a damped empirical dispersion correction reproduces structures and cohesive energies of a representative set of molecular crystals in excellent agreement with experiment, and also captures lattice vibrational modes. Because van der Waals dispersion is the dominant cohesive force in most molecular solids and is missing from plain DFT, this dispersion-corrected approach is a cornerstone of the energy-ranking machinery that any CSP workflow depends on.

How it connects: Modern molecular CSP is a two-stage problem: generate many candidate structures, then rank them by energy. Genetic-algorithm and evolutionary search methods drive the generation and sampling stage, exploring the vast space of space groups, molecular orientations, and conformations, but they are only as good as the energy model used to score each candidate. Dispersion-corrected DFT of the kind introduced here (and its descendants) is the accuracy standard against which cheaper force fields and, more recently, machine-learned potentials are benchmarked. Understanding why dispersion corrections matter clarifies why blind-test successes and current ML-potential CSP work invest so heavily in getting lattice energies right, and why evolutionary searches must be coupled to a trustworthy objective function.

Take away: In molecular crystals the energy differences between competing polymorphs are tiny and dominated by dispersion, so reliable CSP ranking requires an energy method that explicitly includes dispersion; the search algorithm (e.g. a GA) only finds what the energy model can correctly rank.
