Organic crystal polymorphism: a benchmark for dispersion-corrected mean-field electronic structure methods
Jan Gerit Brandenburg, Stefan Grimme  (2016, 70 citations)

Why it's foundational: This paper centers on the crystal structure prediction (CSP) blind test, which is the defining community benchmark for the entire field of molecular CSP. It analyzes the energy landscapes of the sixth CSP blind test targets and constructs a dedicated benchmark set (POLY59) of 59 crystal structures for evaluating the electronic structure methods used to rank predicted polymorphs. For a researcher building a mental map of molecular CSP, the blind test framing plus a curated benchmark set is exactly the kind of cornerstone that defines how methods are assessed, rather than a narrow one-off application.

How it connects: Modern molecular CSP workflows typically combine a structure-generation/search step (where genetic and evolutionary algorithms explore the configurational space of packings and conformations) with an energy-ranking step. This paper speaks directly to that second step by benchmarking dispersion-corrected mean-field electronic structure methods on real blind-test polymorphs. Because GA/evolutionary searches generate many candidate structures that must then be reliably ranked, the accuracy standards and benchmark data established here are what any evolutionary CSP pipeline ultimately has to meet. It ties the search methods the researcher cares about to the community-standard evaluation (the blind test) and to the energy methods that determine which generated structures are physically meaningful.

Take away: Molecular CSP is judged by blind tests and reproducible benchmarks, and the hard part is not only generating candidate structures but accurately ranking their lattice energies; dispersion-corrected first-principles methods are the yardstick, so any evolutionary/GA search is only as good as the energy model used to evaluate its candidates.
