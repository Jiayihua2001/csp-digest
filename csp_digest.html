📚 Landmark of the day — AI for Science landmark
Accurate structure prediction of biomolecular interactions with AlphaFold 3
Josh Abramson, Jonas Adler, Jack Dunger et al.  (2024, 15713 citations, Nature)

Worth 30 minutes? AlphaFold 3 replaced AF2's structure module with a diffusion model that generates raw atom coordinates directly. One network now jointly predicts complexes of proteins, nucleic acids, ligands, ions and modified residues, and it outperformed specialised docking tools. It moved structure prediction from protein-only folding toward general molecular assembly, the closest landmark analogue to packing arbitrary molecules into crystals.

What it could inspire: The key idea is a diffusion generator over atom coordinates, conditioned on learned pair representations, with confidence heads for ranking. Applied to MCSP, a generator could replace random or symmetry-based seeding in Genarris or GA mutations, proposing plausible packings that DFT then ranks. Also worth studying: how AF3 handles generative hallucination and ensemble ranking, which mirrors the problem of many low-energy polymorphs.

Foundations to learn: Learn how a conditional denoising diffusion model generates 3D coordinates without explicit equivariance, using data augmentation instead. Then the Pairformer trunk with its triangle updates and pair representations. Then the confidence metrics: pLDDT, PAE and PDE. Finally, the cross-distillation used against hallucination. For the underlying theory, read DDPM and Sohl-Dickstein 2015 alongside.

30-minute reading guide: Spend 5 minutes on the abstract and Fig. 1 (architecture plus the benchmark comparison). Spend 10 minutes on the diffusion module and Pairformer description, including the training-setup figure. Use 8 minutes for the ligand and complex accuracy results and the confidence calibration. Spend 7 minutes on the limitations: stereochemistry violations, hallucination and conformational ensembles. Skip the per-category benchmark breakdowns and the supplementary hyperparameters.
