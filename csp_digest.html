Significant progress in predicting the crystal structures of small organic molecules – a report on the fourth blind test
Graeme M. Day, Timothy G. Cooper, Aurora J. Cruz‐Cabeza et al.  (2009, 448 citations)

Why it's foundational: This is the report on the fourth blind test of crystal structure prediction, one in the internationally recognized series of collaborative CSP blind tests organized through the Cambridge Crystallographic Data Centre. The blind tests are the defining benchmark exercises of the molecular CSP field: multiple research groups are given only the atomic connectivity of small organic molecules and must predict their crystal structures without knowing the experimental answer. The abstract explicitly frames this as an evaluation of the present state of computational methods for predicting the crystal structures of small organic molecules, marking measurable progress over the earlier tests (CSP1999, CSP2001, CSP2004).

How it connects: The blind test framework is the shared yardstick against which every molecular CSP methodology, including genetic-algorithm and evolutionary search strategies, is measured and compared. The two central subproblems it exposes, exhaustive/stochastic sampling of the crystal packing landscape and accurate energy ranking of candidate structures, are exactly the two ingredients that GA/evolutionary methods target: the search operators explore packing space while the fitness function (lattice energy) discriminates between polymorphs. Understanding what the community counts as success, which molecule classes remain hard, and how sampling and ranking failures are diagnosed lets a researcher position any new evolutionary sampler within the established evaluation culture that connects the older empirical-potential work (papers 21, 23) to modern DFT-D and machine-learned energy landscape approaches.

Take away: Molecular CSP is fundamentally a two-part challenge, comprehensive generation of candidate packings plus reliable relative energy ranking, and progress is measured through community blind tests using only molecular connectivity as input; any GA/evolutionary method must be judged by how well it addresses both halves against that benchmark.
