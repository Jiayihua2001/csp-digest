📚 Classic of the day — build your canon
Predicting crystal structures of organic compounds
Sarah L. Price  (2013, 528 citations)

Why it's foundational: This is a defining review of organic crystal structure prediction that lays out the conceptual core of the field: CSP methods search for the most thermodynamically stable crystal structure while making various approximations in evaluating the crystal energy, with the global-minimum structure taken as the prediction of an experimental form. For a researcher focused on molecular CSP, this review frames the central problem (search plus energy ranking) that every method, including genetic/evolutionary approaches, must solve.

How it connects: It ties directly to modern CSP by articulating the two coupled challenges that GA/evolutionary methods address: efficiently sampling an enormous configurational space of candidate packings and accurately ranking their energies. The evolutionary/GA methods elsewhere in this list (particle-swarm optimization, adaptive GAs, GA plus energy minimization for inorganics and interfaces) are concrete instantiations of the search step this review formalizes, while the crystal energy landscape and dispersion-corrected DFT work map onto its ranking step. It thus provides the umbrella context that connects search heuristics, energy models, polymorphism, and the ongoing move toward machine-learned potentials.

Take away: Internalize that CSP is fundamentally a global optimization over crystal packing space combined with sufficiently accurate relative energy evaluation, and that the reliability of any method (GA/evolutionary or otherwise) depends on jointly getting both the search completeness and the energy ranking right.
