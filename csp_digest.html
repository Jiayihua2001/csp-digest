Conformational Polymorphism
Aurora J. Cruz‐Cabeza, Joel Bernstein  (2013, 776 citations)

Why it's foundational: This is a defining review of conformational polymorphism, which sits at the heart of molecular crystal structure prediction. For a researcher using GA/evolutionary methods on molecular crystals, the central challenge is that flexible molecules can adopt different conformations in different crystal packings, dramatically expanding the search space the global optimizer must explore. The paper provides a rigorous, quantitative definition of conformational change versus conformational polymorphism and reviews the likelihood of its occurrence, giving the conceptual grounding needed to understand why molecular CSP is harder than rigid-body or inorganic structure prediction.

How it connects: Modern molecular CSP workflows must sample both intramolecular degrees of freedom (conformation) and intermolecular packing simultaneously. Evolutionary and genetic-algorithm approaches encode torsional/conformational variables alongside cell parameters and molecular positions, and the distinction drawn here between conformational adjustment and genuine conformational change directly informs how one designs genotype representations and crossover/mutation operators. Understanding conformational polymorphism also connects to blind-test benchmarking and to systems like ROY (referenced in other papers on this list) where many polymorphs arise precisely from conformational flexibility, which is where GA-based search is stress-tested.

Take away: Internalize that molecular conformational flexibility is a primary driver of polymorphism and therefore a core dimension of the CSP search problem, so any evolutionary/GA method for molecular crystals must explicitly and correctly sample conformational space, not just crystal packing.
