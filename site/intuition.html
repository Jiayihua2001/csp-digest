FastCSP: Accelerated Molecular Crystal Structure Prediction with Universal Model for Atoms (2025) · doi:10.48550/arxiv.2508.02641

Core idea: FastCSP is an end-to-end molecular CSP workflow driven entirely by a single pretrained universal MLIP (UMA), covering conformer generation, random structure generation (Genarris 3), relaxation, ranking, and optional free-energy and conformer-energy corrections. It matches dispersion-inclusive DFT (PBE-D3) fidelity for both semi-rigid and flexible molecules without any system-specific fine-tuning or DFT re-ranking, recovering all 74 experimental polymorphs across 38 compounds within 9 kJ/mol of the global minimum.

Innovation: Prior MLIP-based CSP either used system-specific models trained per-target (e.g., AIMNet2 in blind test 7) or retained a final DFT re-ranking step. FastCSP uses a single pretrained multi-task model, exploiting complementarity between the OMC task (PBE-D3, periodic, accurate for intermolecular/packing) and the OMol task (wB97M-V, finite molecules, accurate for intramolecular conformer energies) to build an OMC->OMol conformer correction that fixes GGA delocalization error without any post-hoc high-level DFT.

A pool of ~20 conformers (fewer than 10 usually suffices) gives comfortable coverage.

Assistant synthesis (interpretation): For GAtor 2.0, the most transferable lesson is the two-tier energy strategy: use a cheap universal MLIP (OMC-level, PBE-D3-like) for the GA fitness/relaxation loop where you need thousands of evaluations and packing accuracy, but recognize it inherits GGA delocalization error verbatim - so for conformationally flexible or Z'>1 targets you must layer a higher-theory intramolecular correction (OMol/wB97M-V single-points on extracted monomers) to rebalance conformer vs packing energy before final ranking. This replaces the expensive PBE0+MBD re-ranking that currently gates GAtor throughput, and the ROY result (rank 336 -> 1) shows the correction can flip the global minimum, which matters directly for GA niching: your fitness landscape's basins will be systematically mislocated for conjugated flexible molecules unless corrected, so niches built on raw MLIP energy could converge to the wrong 'stable' conformational family. Practically, run the GA with a generous but modest conformer/torsion seed pool (Genarris already samples this), lean on aggressive deduplication to prune the population, treat finite-T free energies as a diagnostic rather than an automatic ranking improvement, and always sanity-check MLIP-vs-DFT agreement using a common reference minimum plus a check that your target's functional groups are actually represented in OMC25/OMol25 - underrepresented scaffolds (the kind of exotic chemistry that shows up in blind tests) are exactly where the universal model quietly fails and where per-system fine-tuning or DFT is still warranted.

☆

Principles

☆
CSP success has two distinct requirements: (1) the experimental structure must correspond to a local minimum of the fitness function so it is GENERATED, and (2) it must be RANKED highly. Structures can be generated by the search but discarded during intermediate ranking before reaching accurate final ranking, causing failures unrelated to search coverage.
principle · scope: all CSP workflows with hierarchical generate-then-rank pipelines · established
'Experimental structures were initially generated by some search algorithms but not ranked highly by the intermediate optimisation and ranking methods, and therefore not brought forward to the final stages where these missing structures could have ranked highly.'
source: Report on the sixth blind test of organic crystal structure prediction (2016) — doi.org/10.1107/s2052520616007447
☆
A CSP landscape densely populated with low-energy putative structures near the global minimum signals likely polymorphism and unreliable single-structure prediction; large energy gaps above the minimum signal a robust prediction. Multiple submissions independently finding the same low-energy putative structures strengthens confidence that they are real candidate forms.
principle · scope: interpreting energy landscapes to guide experiment rather than predict 'the' structure · suggested
'For XXV, some of the submitted lists show large gaps in terms of energy... For other systems, the results show a range of structures close to the global minimum, which is more indicative of potential polymorphism'; 'many of the other structures predicted as being low in energy were found in multiple submissions'
source: Report on the sixth blind test of organic crystal structure prediction (2016) — doi.org/10.1107/s2052520616007447

☆
The Harris approximation (superposition of self-consistent single-molecule densities, energy evaluated without a self-consistency cycle) is accurate for weakly interacting molecules but fails when densities overlap; because it is non-variational, its binding-energy error is always negative and grows asymptotically as molecules get closer.
principle · scope: Rigid molecular crystals/dimers of small organics in the van der Waals regime; PBE+TS@Harris · suggested
HA 'becomes exact when the molecules are far apart'; near equilibrium |dBE| small (0.0740, 0.0049, 0.0288 eV for targets II, XIII, XXII); 'Because of the non-variational nature of the HA, dBE(x) is always negative and its magnitude increases asymptotically with decreasing distance.'
source: Genarris: Random generation of molecular crystal structures and fast s (2018) — doi.org/10.1063/1.5014038
☆
A descriptor based on relative positions and orientations of neighboring molecules (RCD) captures crystal packing motifs (correlating with space-group symmetry) better than atom-distance descriptors like the RDF, which instead track unit-cell volume; RCD also better identifies pre-relaxation near-duplicates.
principle · scope: Rigid molecule crystals; comparison on 5000-structure raw pool of Target II · single-study
RCD shows 'stronger correlation with space group symmetry' and 'weaker correlation with the unit cell volume' than RDF; RCD-AP 'grouped almost all the duplicate pairs together up to 7 clusters'; RCD consistently higher silhouette scores, increasing with cluster number while RDF's decrease.
source: Genarris: Random generation of molecular crystal structures and fast s (2018) — doi.org/10.1063/1.5014038

This corrects sampling bias against rarely-generated packing motifs.
principle · scope: Clustering of crystal-structure pools / synthetic 2D-3D test data · suggested
'While k-means groups three of the satellite regions together into one cluster, AP successfully identifies them as distinct clusters'; AP identified a distinct low-a cluster containing structures within 0.2 eV of the global minimum; AP 'does not depend on a randomized initialization'.

☆

principle · scope: Molecular crystal structure prediction generally (CSP blind tests) · established
'weak van der Waals interactions... lead to many local minima that are extremely close in energy, requiring energy resolution of a few meV'; 'Generic force fields have consistently been demonstrated to produce poor results'; MBD with hybrid functionals 'shown to be highly accurate'.

☆
Standard vdW cutoffs (0.85 x sum-of-radii) wrongly reject structures with strong X-H...Y hydrogen bonds because real H-bond contact distances are much shorter than typical vdW contacts; N/O donor-acceptor pairs need dedicated shorter cutoffs (effective sr ~0.55-0.62), while C-donor and halogen H-bonds are fine at 0.85.
principle · scope: Homomolecular crystals with O-H/N-H donors to O/N acceptors; cutoffs from CSD IsoStar distributions · established
'the sum of the vdW radii multiplied by the default sr value of 0.85... exceeds a large number of non-bonded interaction distances... Using the default sr value, structures with strong hydrogen bonds, such as glycine, would be deemed unphysical'; Table 2 gives sr 0.55-0.62 for OH/NH...O/N.
source: Genarris 2.0: A random structure generator for molecular crystals (2020) — doi.org/10.1016/j.cpc.2020.107170
☆
Space groups with glide planes and screw axes are easier to populate than those with mirror planes, because mirror-related molecules can overlap with their own mirror image; this biases which space groups are readily sampled even when a uniform distribution is targeted.
principle · scope: Random generation of rigid molecular crystals; illustrated for benzene Z=4 and glycine Z=4 · suggested
'space groups like P2/m (10), Pmm2 (25), and Pmmm (47) which have mirror planes are harder to generate as molecules that touch the planes overlap with their own mirror image. In contrast, space groups with glide planes and screws axes are easier to generate because symmetry-equivalent molecules are translated in space.'
source: Genarris 2.0: A random structure generator for molecular crystals (2020) — doi.org/10.1016/j.cpc.2020.107170
☆
A regularized hard-sphere potential can drive close packing purely geometrically: freezing internal molecular geometry and optimizing lattice vectors, molecular positions and orientations to minimize cell volume subject to intermolecular distance penalties reproduces near-experimental unit cell volumes without any energy evaluation.
principle · scope: Rigid, single-conformer molecules, Z'=1, all space groups; demonstrated on 6 targets including H-bonded, van der Waals, and energetic crystals · single-study
Rigid Press minimizes F(s)=V(s)+P_contact(s) with piecewise differentiable penalty; after Rigid Press, cell volumes are close to experimental (slightly underestimated for aspirin/Target XXII/Target I; very close for dense delta-HMX and DNI). Matches to experiment appear for all targets only after Rigid Press.
source: Genarris 3.0: Generating Close-Packed Molecular Crystal Structures wit (2025) — doi.org/10.1021/acs.jctc.5c01080

Three physical effects are each individually necessary for quantitative rankings: hybrid exchange (Pauli repulsion / reduced self-interaction error), beyond-pairwise many-body dispersion, and vibrational (entropic) free energy; which one dominates is system-dependent.
principle · scope: Diverse molecular crystals (rigid, flexible, salt, cocrystal), PBE0+MBD+Fvib framework · suggested
'all of the three previously mentioned theoretical effects are required'; Pauli exchange 'plays a crucial role for system XXII, while MBD effects are the most important factor for system XXVI'; harmonic Fvib has 'by far the largest impact' for XXIII and XXV.
source: Reliable and practical computational description of molecular crystal (2019) — doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aau3338
☆
Starting global optimization from already-symmetric trial structures finds the global energy minimum in fewer attempts than purely random generation, because symmetry biases sampling toward realistic, higher-symmetry configurations that nature prefers.
principle · scope: Global optimization / CSP for atomic clusters and crystals · established
'It is generally believed that by beginning with already-symmetric structures, fewer attempts are needed to find the global energy minimum'; benchmarks show symmetric seeding found ground states while random did not.
source: PyXtal: A Python library for crystal structure generation and symmetry (2020) — doi.org/10.1016/j.cpc.2020.107810
☆
Pre-symmetrization spans structure space more broadly (higher average energy, more diverse) whereas purely random structures cluster tightly around a narrow 'glassy' energy range, especially for larger systems.
principle · scope: LJ clusters and elemental C/Si crystals (2-16 atoms/cell) · suggested
'pre-symmetrization spans the possible structure space more effectively, while purely random structures are more clustered around a specific energy range' and 'prevent the clustering of glassy structures found in pure random generations for large systems'
source: PyXtal: A Python library for crystal structure generation and symmetry (2020) — doi.org/10.1016/j.cpc.2020.107810
☆
A molecule can only occupy a Wyckoff position if its point group contains the site symmetry as a subgroup AND it is oriented so its symmetry axes align with the site symmetry axes; orientation and shape (not just position) determine WP compatibility for molecules.
principle · scope: Molecular crystals, molecules on special Wyckoff positions · established
'if the object is instead a molecule, then the WP compatibility is also determined by orientation and shape' and the requirement to be 'sufficiently symmetric' and 'oriented such that its symmetry operations are aligned with the Wyckoff site symmetry'
source: PyXtal: A Python library for crystal structure generation and symmetry (2020) — doi.org/10.1016/j.cpc.2020.107810
☆
GGA/semi-local functionals (PBE) suffer delocalization error that overstabilizes extended pi-conjugation, artificially lowering the energy of flatter (small-dihedral) molecular conformations and biasing the ranking of conformational polymorphs; this bias propagates doubly because planar conformers also pack more favorably. A pretrained MLIP faithfully reproduces this same error since it is trained on GGA data.
principle · scope: Conformationally flexible molecules with rotatable bonds connecting conjugated systems (ROY S-C-N-C torsion, Target X amide-ring conjugation) · established
'Semi-local functionals... overstabilize extended pi-conjugation, artificially lowering the energy of molecular conformations with smaller (more planar)... dihedral angles'; UMA-OMC torsion scan 'closely tracks B86bPBE-XDM', overstabilizing planar minimum by ~4 kJ/mol vs CCSD(T)
source: FastCSP: Accelerated Molecular Crystal Structure Prediction with Unive (2025) — doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2508.02641
☆
Seeding CSP with a conformer that matches the crystal geometry neither guarantees nor is strictly required for recovering the experimental structure, because intermolecular packing forces ultimately reshape the molecular conformation during relaxation; a somewhat-different initial conformer can still relax into the correct crystal. A pool of ~20 conformers (fewer than 10 usually suffices) gives comfortable coverage.
principle · scope: Flexible molecules up to 4 rotatable bonds; conformer pools from RDKit + UMA-OMol · suggested
'seeding the workflow with conformers that match the crystal geometry does not, in and of itself, guarantee successful CSP because intermolecular interactions ultimately determine the final relaxed molecular structure'; highest-ranked conformer needed was rank #17; 'fewer than 10 conformers would have sufficed for most systems'
source: FastCSP: Accelerated Molecular Crystal Structure Prediction with Unive (2025) — doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2508.02641

Decision rules (when to use what)

☆
Finite-temperature free energies (harmonic Helmholtz Fvib) change polymorph rank order and repeatedly IMPROVED the ranking of experimental structures; use them when energy gaps between competitors are small. Free-energy/MD calculations cost >=1 order of magnitude more than the geometry optimization.
decision_rule · scope: polymorph ranking where lattice-energy gaps are small (e.g. XXII, XXIII) · suggested
'The second lists of Day et al., Price et al. and Tkatchenko et al. all employed Helmholtz free energies, which changed the rank order... and, in all three cases, improved the ranking of the experimentally known structure'; 'the cost of all of these calculations is substantial, often more than an order of magnitude more than the initial geometry optimisation'
source: Report on the sixth blind test of organic crystal structure prediction (2016) — doi.org/10.1107/s2052520616007447
☆
Computational cost and success are weakly correlated: for XXII some cheap empirical-potential + rigid-multipole submissions (<200 CPU hours) succeeded while several full DFT/ab initio submissions (orders of magnitude more cost) failed. Match method complexity to the physics that actually limits the target.
decision_rule · scope: CSP method selection across target difficulty levels · suggested
'a few submissions predicted the known experimental structure with less than 200 CPU hours, using comparatively simple empirical potentials... Conversely, a number of full DFT/ab initio submissions for XXII failed to predict the experimental structure, despite using orders of magnitude more computational resources'
source: Report on the sixth blind test of organic crystal structure prediction (2016) — doi.org/10.1107/s2052520616007447
☆
Validate a CSP method on chemically diverse targets spanning different intermolecular interaction types; GAtor was benchmarked on four past blind test targets characterized by different interaction motifs and found the experimental plus other low-energy structures for all four.
decision_rule · scope: Benchmarking molecular CSP methods against CSP blind test targets · single-study
Paper: 'applied to a chemically diverse set of four past blind test targets, characterized by different types of intermolecular interactions. The experimentally observed structures and other low-energy structures are found for all four targets.'
source: GAtor: A First-Principles Genetic Algorithm for Molecular Crystal Stru (2018) — doi.org/10.1021/acs.jctc.7b01152
☆
Set the close-contact cutoff (sr, fraction of summed vdW radii) in an intermediate window: too small admits unphysically close contacts that produce large negative HA errors, too large makes structures energetically too similar for the HA to resolve. An sr around 0.8-0.9 (or ~0.625 tuned) balances physicality against HA reliability and motif diversity.
decision_rule · scope: HA-screened random pools of rigid small organics · single-study
sr 'should be large enough to avoid unphysical structures... and small enough to allow for a diversity of crystal packing motifs'; default half-normal sr with bounds 0.9/0.05/0.8; sr=0.500 outliers had N...N contacts of 1.64, 1.80, 1.56 A; sr=0.750 pool too close in energy for HA to resolve.
source: Genarris: Random generation of molecular crystal structures and fast s (2018) — doi.org/10.1063/1.5014038
☆
To seed a genetic algorithm, prefer a maximally diverse initial population over a low-energy one: the Diverse pool gave the best GA convergence to representative low-energy motifs and consistently found the experimental structures, while Energy-seeded runs were inconsistent and Random-seeded runs performed worst.
decision_rule · scope: GAtor genetic-algorithm runs on Targets II, XIII, XXII from Genarris pools · single-study
'starting the GA from the Diverse pool results in the best performance... consistently find the experimental structures'; Energy pool 'leads to inconsistent performance'; Random pools 'consistently exhibit the worse performance'. 'We recommend using the Diverse workflow of Genarris to produce initial pools for GAtor.'
source: Genarris: Random generation of molecular crystal structures and fast s (2018) — doi.org/10.1063/1.5014038
☆
Use the Robust down-selection workflow (RCD or RDF descriptor -> affinity-propagation cluster to ~10% -> single-point PBE+TS energies -> AP cluster again to ~10% keeping lowest-energy per cluster -> full relaxation) to produce a small, diverse, low-energy initial population rather than to guarantee finding the experimental structure.
decision_rule · scope: Genarris 2.0 curated-pool generation feeding downstream search (e.g., genetic algorithms) · single-study
'the goal of the Robust workflow is not necessarily to find the experimental structure but to produce an initial population for a structure search algorithm, such as a genetic algorithm.' Default cluster count is 10% of the pool; workflow uses AP clustering then energy-based selection then relaxation.
source: Genarris 2.0: A random structure generator for molecular crystals (2020) — doi.org/10.1016/j.cpc.2020.107170
☆
Pre-optimize randomly generated structures with Rigid Press before MLIP/DFT relaxation: it costs up to 2 orders of magnitude less than MLIP relaxation and sharply reduces the number of downstream relaxation steps, and for some targets a match to the experimental structure is only found when Rigid Press is used.
decision_rule · scope: CSP workflows relaxing thousands to millions of putative structures · single-study
'The computational cost of Rigid Press optimization is lower by up to 2 orders of magnitude than relaxation using MLIPs.' For epsilon-CL-20, a match to experiment can only be found with Rigid Press (SI). SI Fig S1 shows Rigid Press pre-optimization reduces MACE-OFF relaxation steps.
source: Genarris 3.0: Generating Close-Packed Molecular Crystal Structures wit (2025) — doi.org/10.1021/acs.jctc.5c01080
☆
When selecting survivors from clusters in a hierarchical CSP workflow, keep multiple low-energy structures per cluster (e.g., up to 5 within a 10 kJ/mol window) rather than only the single lowest-energy structure, because an inaccurate MLIP can rank the true structure poorly and a strict energy cutoff would discard it.
decision_rule · scope: Hierarchical CSP with general-purpose MLIP early-stage ranking, especially chemistries far from training data (energetic materials) · single-study
For DNI the experimental structure is ranked #79 (19.3 kJ/mol above min) by MACE-OFF but becomes the global minimum at PBE+MBD SPE; it survives because it is 2nd in its cluster and up-to-5-within-10-kJ/mol selection is used. Procedure especially benefits delta-HMX and epsilon-CL-20.
source: Genarris 3.0: Generating Close-Packed Molecular Crystal Structures wit (2025) — doi.org/10.1021/acs.jctc.5c01080
☆
Dispersion-inclusive DFT remains indispensable for final ranking; validate general-purpose MLIP performance per system, and if inadequate, train a system-specific potential (e.g., AIMNet2) rather than trusting out-of-the-box MLIP energies.
decision_rule · scope: CSP energy ranking; especially chemistries far from MLIP training data · single-study
A system-specific AIMNet2 for CL-20 gives higher match rate, lower RMSD30, lower energy MAE and higher Kendall vs PBE+MBD than MACE-OFF, ranking the experimental structure #4 at 2.50 kJ/mol above minimum. Authors 'suggest exercising caution' and recommend case-by-case validation.
source: Genarris 3.0: Generating Close-Packed Molecular Crystal Structures wit (2025) — doi.org/10.1021/acs.jctc.5c01080
☆
Relative stability of near-degenerate polymorphs can invert between 0 K lattice energy and finite-temperature free energy, so finite-temperature (e.g., QHA) corrections are needed when two forms are within ~1 kJ/mol.
decision_rule · scope: Near-degenerate polymorphs (aspirin Forms I and II), dispersion-inclusive DFT · single-study
PBE+MBD at 0 K ranks Form II below Form I by 0.69 kJ/mol, but PBE+MBD QHA free energy at 300 K predicts Form I more stable than Form II by 1.10 kJ/mol, consistent with experiment; prior studies note relative stability depends on method and free-energy corrections.
source: Genarris 3.0: Generating Close-Packed Molecular Crystal Structures wit (2025) — doi.org/10.1021/acs.jctc.5c01080
☆
Compute expensive PBE0+MBD and vibrational free energies only for the low-energy window, not all structures: hybrid energies for structures within ~4.5 kJ/mol of the PBE+MBD ranking and Fvib for structures within ~3 kJ/mol of the PBE0+MBD ranking.
decision_rule · scope: Cost-constrained final ranking of molecular crystals · single-study
'PBE0 + MBD calculations are available for (at least) the structures located within the top 4.5 kJ/mol'; 'Vibrational free energies were calculated for (at least) all structures that are located within the top 3 kJ/mol according to the PBE0 + MBD ranking'.
source: Reliable and practical computational description of molecular crystal (2019) — doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aau3338
☆
For Z'>1 handling, use the sampling list that explicitly includes Z'=2 structures; missing an experimental form (XXIII form E) can happen simply because it fell just outside the energetic window used for Z'=2 candidates.
decision_rule · scope: Multi-molecule asymmetric unit (Z'=2) molecular crystals in CSP · single-study
'we used the list that also included Z' = 2 structures'; form E 'was located just outside the energetic window considered for the Z' = 2 structures'.
source: Reliable and practical computational description of molecular crystal (2019) — doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aau3338
☆
By default do not generate crystals containing mirror (inverted) copies of chiral molecules, since a chiral molecule's mirror image can have different chemical/biological properties; enable inversion only when appropriate.
decision_rule · scope: Molecular crystals of chiral molecules, especially pharmaceutical applications · established
'a chiral molecule's mirror image will possess different chemical or biological properties... By default, PyXtal does not generate crystals with mirror copies of chiral molecules. The user can choose to allow inversion if desired.'
source: PyXtal: A Python library for crystal structure generation and symmetry (2020) — doi.org/10.1016/j.cpc.2020.107810
☆
To generate new candidate structures while preserving symmetry, perturb only the asymmetric unit (free WP coordinates, molecular orientation along allowed axes, flexible dihedrals, and symmetry-constrained cell); to model phase transitions or diversify, use group-subgroup (translationengleiche/klassengleiche) transitions with Wyckoff splitting and cell transformation.
decision_rule · scope: Symmetry-constrained structure modulation for CSP and phase-transition studies · suggested
'perturb only the atoms in the asymmetric unit... the whole molecule can be reoriented along the allowed rotational axes. If the molecule has flexible rotors, additional perturbation on these dihedral angles' and group-subgroup transition 'randomly pick one possible transformation path between G and H'
source: PyXtal: A Python library for crystal structure generation and symmetry (2020) — doi.org/10.1016/j.cpc.2020.107810
☆
Apply an OMC->OMol (intramolecular) conformer energy correction when the experimental structure differs from the GGA global minimum primarily by molecular conformation - i.e., for conformational polymorphs where GGA delocalization error overstabilizes planar/conjugated conformers; it is not beneficial (and can hurt) for rigid molecules or when intermolecular interactions dominate.
decision_rule · scope: Flexible molecules with conformational polymorphism (ROY, Target X, Target XXII); ineffective for semi-rigid molecules and worsened Target XVIII · suggested
Conformer correction lifted ROY's Y form from rank #336 to #1 and recovered Y > YT04 > R ordering; improved Target X from #5 to #1 and Target XXII to #1; 'no appreciable benefit for the semi-rigid compounds'; worsened Target XVIII from #1 to #22
source: FastCSP: Accelerated Molecular Crystal Structure Prediction with Unive (2025) — doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2508.02641
☆
Quasi-harmonic free-energy corrections at finite temperature are NOT universally beneficial: they tend to improve recall for flexible molecules but slightly worsen it for semi-rigid molecules, and do not reliably fix polymorph ordering. Do not assume adding QHA free energies improves rankings.
decision_rule · scope: Molecular crystal polymorph ranking at 300 K via QHA; benchmark of 38 organic molecules · established
'for the semi-rigid compounds the free energy corrections make the recall slightly worse'; 'for flexible compounds... overall improvement in recall'; 'free energy corrections did not necessarily improve the stability ranking among polymorphs' - mirroring blind test 7 findings
source: FastCSP: Accelerated Molecular Crystal Structure Prediction with Unive (2025) — doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2508.02641

Standard pipeline practices

☆
Hierarchical ranking is standard: cheap potentials or coarse DFT (e.g. Harris-approximation charge densities) triage a large pool, then progressively expensive methods (bespoke multipole/DFT-fitted potentials, then full periodic vdW-DFT like PBE+MBD, TPSS-D3, PBE+XDM) rank the survivors near the global minimum.
pipeline_practice · scope: modern CSP ranking workflows (2016 blind test participants) · established
'a hierarchical approach has been adopted, in which a less intensive computational method or algorithm is used initially... More computationally demanding methods and algorithms were then employed for the final set of structures closest to the global minimum'; 'Solid-state DFT calculations have been used by 12 submissions... where only two submissions employed DFT' in the fifth test
source: Report on the sixth blind test of organic crystal structure prediction (2016) — doi.org/10.1107/s2052520616007447
☆
GAtor performs the GA search with dispersion-inclusive DFT for local optimizations and energies, then re-relaxes and re-ranks the best generated structures with a hierarchy of increasingly accurate DFT functionals and dispersion methods.
pipeline_practice · scope: First-principles molecular CSP pipeline (GAtor + FHI-aims) · single-study
Paper: 'best structures generated are re-relaxed and re-ranked using a hierarchy of increasingly accurate DFT functionals and dispersion methods'; local optimizations/energies via FHI-aims with dispersion-inclusive DFT.
source: GAtor: A First-Principles Genetic Algorithm for Molecular Crystal Stru (2018) — doi.org/10.1021/acs.jctc.7b01152
☆
Genarris workflows apply hierarchical screening: generate a large raw pool (5,000-10,000), evaluate energies (HA for Diverse/Energy, self-consistent DFT for Rigorous), then alternate RCD-AP clustering with energy-based selection, reducing to ~1% final pool and fully relaxing only at the end. Diverse selects cluster exemplars for maximal spread; Energy over-clusters to concentrate on low-energy basins; Rigorous iterates cluster/select/partial-relax down to 5% then energy-selects.
pipeline_practice · scope: Genarris Diverse/Energy/Rigorous workflows for rigid molecules, Z=4/Z'=1 · single-study
Raw pools of 5,000 (II, XIII) and 10,000 (XXII); 'each step of the Diverse and Energy workflows reduces the pool to 10%'; final pool 1% of raw; Rigorous repeats until pool <5%, then purely energy-based selection to 1%; full relaxation only for final pools.
source: Genarris: Random generation of molecular crystal structures and fast s (2018) — doi.org/10.1063/1.5014038
☆
The generation loop is: estimate solid-form volume (relax single molecule + ML model) -> find compatible space groups within a symmetry tolerance -> per space group draw a cell volume from a Gaussian (mean = predicted volume, sigma = 3x prediction error) -> build random cell, place/orient molecule (random for general positions, axis-aligned for special positions) -> apply symmetry ops -> hierarchical distance checks -> add passing structures to raw pool.
pipeline_practice · scope: Genarris 2.0 raw-pool generation for semi-rigid molecules with no rotatable bonds · single-study
'A random volume is drawn from a Gaussian distribution whose mean and standard deviation are the predicted volume and three times the prediction error... The space group symmetry operations are then applied... The attempted structures that pass the intermolecular distance checks... are added to the raw pool.'
source: Genarris 2.0: A random structure generator for molecular crystals (2020) — doi.org/10.1016/j.cpc.2020.107170
☆
Structure checking is done in three escalating stages to control the O(N^2) cost: Stage I ignores periodicity and uses center-of-mass/molecule-length screening (kills most bad structures cheaply), Stage II does approximate minimum-image distances in fractional coordinates after lattice reduction, and Stage III applies a rigorous sphere-decoder closest-vector solution only for the few non-orthogonal structures that survive.
pipeline_practice · scope: Genarris 2.0 close-contact detection, including triclinic/oblique cells · single-study
'Genarris 2.0 performs a series of three hierarchical structure checks. Failed structures are discarded at each stage... most of the unphysical structures generated are rejected at this stage [I]... only few non-orthogonal structures that pass Stage I and Stage II reach Stage III. Hence, the overall efficiency is not compromised.'
source: Genarris 2.0: A random structure generator for molecular crystals (2020) — doi.org/10.1016/j.cpc.2020.107170
☆
The Genarris 3.0 CSP down-selection pipeline runs: generate ~4000 structures per compatible space group at 1.5x inflated volume -> Rigid Press compression -> loose-tolerance duplicate removal per space group -> MACE-OFF SPE + affinity-propagation clustering to 10% -> MACE-OFF full relaxation + duplicate removal -> AP cluster to 100, keep up to 5 within 10 kJ/mol per cluster -> PBE+MBD SPE + AP cluster (keep all within 10 kJ/mol) -> final PBE+MBD relaxation + duplicate removal.
pipeline_practice · scope: Genarris 3.0 CSP workflow, Z'=1; thresholds are user-tunable · single-study
Detailed workflow in Methods: s_r=0.95 proximity check for generation, Rigid Press default s_r=0.85, StructureMatcher tolerances 0.5/0.5/10deg; AP clustering to 10% then 100 clusters; 10 kJ/mol windows; PBE+MBD final relaxation Tier 2 tight, 3x3x3 k-grid.
source: Genarris 3.0: Generating Close-Packed Molecular Crystal Structures wit (2025) — doi.org/10.1021/acs.jctc.5c01080
☆
The recommended final-refinement pipeline: (1) take top ~100 sampled structures per system, (2) full PBE+TS geometry/lattice relaxation without symmetry constraints + duplicate removal, (3) PBE+MBD tight-settings relative energies, (4) PBE0+MBD static lattice energies (approximated via light-level hybrid correction added to tight PBE+MBD), (5) add PBE+TS harmonic vibrational free energies at the experimental temperature.
pipeline_practice · scope: Final-stage ranking of a limited structure set (~100-500 structures) after external sampling; not a from-scratch search method · single-study
Materials and Methods describe exactly this order; 'this computational procedure is intended for the final stability ranking of a molecular CSP using a limited structure set'; total 501 structures, 15-992 atoms.
source: Reliable and practical computational description of molecular crystal (2019) — doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aau3338
☆
PyXtal's generation pipeline runs in order: check Wyckoff compatibility with stoichiometry, generate lattice, insert Wyckoff positions from largest (general) to smallest multiplicity, merge close atoms into special positions, then distance-check; each step retries up to a max before failing.
pipeline_practice · scope: Random symmetric structure generation, atomic and molecular · single-study
Flowchart: 'Wyckoff Compatibility Check, Lattice Generation, Wyckoff Position Insertion, Wyckoff Merging, Distance Check' and 'PyXtal starts with the largest available WP... in order of descending multiplicity'
source: PyXtal: A Python library for crystal structure generation and symmetry (2020) — doi.org/10.1016/j.cpc.2020.107810
☆
The FastCSP pipeline order is: (1) RDKit multi-sampler conformer generation + UMA-OMol relaxation + Butina clustering with energy gate; (2) Genarris 3 random crystal generation across compatible space groups/Z with Rigid Press close-packing; (3) StructureMatcher deduplication (removes ~67% semi-rigid, ~43% flexible); (4) full UMA-OMC relaxation + second deduplication; (5) rank by lattice energy; optional QHA free energy and OMC->OMol conformer corrections.
pipeline_practice · scope: End-to-end MLIP-driven CSP workflow, Z'<=1 · single-study
Figure 1 and Methods: conformer generation -> Genarris random generation + Rigid Press + dedup -> UMA-OMC relaxation + dedup -> ranking, with optional free energy and conformer corrections; 'an average of 67% for the Semi-Rigid and 43% for the Flexible molecules' removed at first dedup
source: FastCSP: Accelerated Molecular Crystal Structure Prediction with Unive (2025) — doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2508.02641

Pitfalls

☆
Conformers that appear unstable or are saddle points for the isolated molecule in vacuo can be the ones adopted in the stable crystal; some may not even correspond to a gas-phase minimum. Rigid searches using gas-phase-favored conformers systematically miss experimental structures (seen acutely for XXVI, the largest/flexible target).
pitfall · scope: flexible molecules with multiple accessible conformations in CSP · established
'conformers that appear unstable for the molecule in vacuo can be found in the stable crystal structure... in some cases the solid-state conformation may not even correspond to a conformer on the isolated molecule's potential-energy surface'; XXVI failures 'attributed to difficulties in generating the experimental conformation due to its distorted nature'
source: Report on the sixth blind test of organic crystal structure prediction (2016) — doi.org/10.1107/s2052520616007447
☆
Force fields and even some vdW-DFT approximations can mispredict molecular conformation: for target XXII many methods flattened the hinged dithiino ring (a saddle point), traced to poor treatment of S atoms with some dispersion approaches, seeding wrong crystal structures even in fully ab initio submissions.
pitfall · scope: molecules with soft internal degrees of freedom / heavy-atom vdW (S) treatment · single-study
'Some force fields fail to adequately represent the hinge of this molecule... Even some DFT methods have difficulty with the conformation... traced back to issues treating the S atoms with some vdW approaches... a number of submissions, even fully ab initio ones, featured crystal structures with flat or nearly flat molecules'
source: Report on the sixth blind test of organic crystal structure prediction (2016) — doi.org/10.1107/s2052520616007447
☆
0 K equilibrium geometries inflate RMSD vs experiment because they omit thermal expansion and zero-point motion. For XXII an MD simulation at the true 150 K experimental temperature gave RMSD20 = 0.140 A, smaller than any submitted static structure; a 300 K MD gave 0.187 A.
pitfall · scope: comparing predicted 0 K structures to finite-temperature diffraction structures · single-study
'For XXII, MD simulations at 300 K gave an RMSD20 of 0.187 A... but a post-test MD simulation at the experimental temperature of 150 K, gives a value of 0.140 A, which is smaller than the RMSD values for the submitted structures. This demonstrates the significant contribution of thermal and zero-point motion to RMSDs.'
source: Report on the sixth blind test of organic crystal structure prediction (2016) — doi.org/10.1107/s2052520616007447
☆
Salt vs co-crystal is a fine and sometimes unresolvable line: target XXV, announced as a co-crystal, was later found proton-disordered (O occupancy 0.58, N 0.42) - a mixture of salt and co-crystal. Methods with fixed protonation states cannot explore this; only DFT-based rankings allowed barrierless proton migration to reveal salt forms.
pitfall · scope: multi-component acid-base systems where proton position is ambiguous · single-study
'the hydrogen-bonding proton in XXV is disordered, making the structure a mixture of a molecular salt and a co-crystal'; 'proton occupancies of 0.58 on the carboxylic acid oxygen and 0.42 on the nitrogen'; salt prediction 'possible due to their use of DFT in the final ranking stage, which allows for proton migration... only if there is no barrier'
source: Report on the sixth blind test of organic crystal structure prediction (2016) — doi.org/10.1107/s2052520616007447
☆
Predicted crystal energy landscapes can produce a top-ranked putative structure that is not the experimentally reported form and differs in Z' and packing motif, so the lowest-energy prediction should not be assumed to be the known experimental polymorph.
pitfall · scope: Molecular CSP ranking; e.g. Target II 5-cyano-3-hydroxythiophene · single-study
Paper: for Target II the top-ranked putative structure is a Z'=2, P-1, scaffold-packing-motif structure 'which has not been reported previously.'
source: GAtor: A First-Principles Genetic Algorithm for Molecular Crystal Stru (2018) — doi.org/10.1021/acs.jctc.7b01152
☆
Energy-selective screening over-samples low-energy basins, increasing the chance that similar structures relax into the same minimum (more post-relaxation duplicates) and leaving high-energy but distinct packing motifs under-sampled; the HA's largest ranking errors also occur precisely in the low-energy region that energy selection emphasizes, degrading r^2 step-to-step.
pitfall · scope: HA-ranked energy-selective workflows for rigid small organics · single-study
Energy workflow 'tends to yield a higher number of duplicates' (e.g., 26 and 28 dup pairs for II and XXII); 'Energy and Rigorous workflows under-sample or completely miss certain clusters... tend to be higher in energy'; 'In the Energy workflow r^2 deteriorates significantly... where the errors of the HA are most severe.'
source: Genarris: Random generation of molecular crystal structures and fast s (2018) — doi.org/10.1063/1.5014038
☆
Volume models trained on ambient-pressure CSD structures systematically over-predict cell volumes for high-pressure polymorphs; expect the predicted volume to be far from experiment when the target forms under pressure.
pitfall · scope: Benzene high-pressure Z=2 (P2_1/c) polymorph at 25 kbar vs ambient-trained model · single-study
'Our prediction for Z = 4 is much closer to the experimental volume than for Z = 2 because the latter forms under pressure of 25 kbar whereas the volume estimation model was trained on structures obtained under ambient pressure.'
source: Genarris 2.0: A random structure generator for molecular crystals (2020) — doi.org/10.1016/j.cpc.2020.107170
☆
Unconstrained relaxation of pool structures can break or create symmetry, changing the apparent space group, Z, or Z', so the space group of a raw structure is not a reliable label for the relaxed structure.
pitfall · scope: DFT geometry optimization (PBE+TS, no lattice constraints) of glycine/benzene pools · single-study
'Relaxation of the final pool may break existing symmetries or create new ones as there are no constraints imposed. This resulted in additional space groups with a different Z or Z'. For example, space group Cmc2_1 (36) and space group P1 were created after geometry optimization for glycine with Z = 2.'
source: Genarris 2.0: A random structure generator for molecular crystals (2020) — doi.org/10.1016/j.cpc.2020.107170
☆
General-purpose MLIPs (MACE-OFF23(L)) give strongly chemistry-dependent accuracy: excellent for drug-like molecules (aspirin, Target I) but markedly worse for chemistries underrepresented in training (Target XXII and especially energetic materials with nitrogen-rich groups), degrading both geometry relaxation and energy ranking.
pitfall · scope: MACE-OFF23(L) trained on SPICE 1.0 (drug-like molecules); molecular crystal relaxation and ranking vs PBE+MBD · single-study
Relaxation match rates (RMSD30<1A): aspirin 86.6%, Target I 91.0%, Target XXII 80.2%, epsilon-CL-20 73.7%, delta-HMX 61.8%, DNI 56.9%. Ranking MAE/RMSE: <5 kJ/mol and Kendall >0.7 for aspirin/Target I; 9-13 kJ/mol and Kendall 0.5-0.6 for Target XXII/CL-20/DNI; >20 kJ/mol and Kendall <0.4 for delta-HMX. Experimental structure misranked as #12 (HMX), #8 (CL-20), #19 (DNI) by MACE-OFF.
source: Genarris 3.0: Generating Close-Packed Molecular Crystal Structures wit (2025) — doi.org/10.1021/acs.jctc.5c01080
☆
Thermodynamic ranking can predict an unobserved structure more stable than all known forms; the true experimental form may be kinetically favored, so a low predicted energy does not guarantee experimental realizability without considering nucleation/growth kinetics and solvent.
pitfall · scope: Complex polymorphic landscapes with many structures in a few kJ/mol (e.g. flexible drug candidate XXIII) · single-study
Str. N70 is ~1.5-3 kJ/mol more stable than any experimental form yet unobserved; 'we hypothesize that form A might be kinetically favored'; 'thermodynamics alone is insufficient for understanding polymorph crystallization'.

☆
Modeling molecules as spheres for intermolecular distance checks (center-center only) fails for anisotropic molecules like benzene, which can overlap under some rotations even when centers are far apart; direct atom-atom checking is needed and is the dominant cost for molecular crystal generation.
pitfall · scope: Molecular crystal generation with non-spherical molecules · established
'a spherical model works poorly for irregularly shaped molecules like benzene... short separations along the perpendicular axis, but must be further apart along the planar axes' and 'this is typically the largest time cost for generation of molecular crystals'
source: PyXtal: A Python library for crystal structure generation and symmetry (2020) — doi.org/10.1016/j.cpc.2020.107810
☆
Relative-energy error metrics are highly sensitive to the reference choice when the MLIP and DFT predict DIFFERENT global minima. Referencing each method to its own minimum can hide large disagreements; using a common (DFT) reference exposes them.
pitfall · scope: Systems where UMA and PBE-D3 global minima differ (ROY, Target I, Target XVI, imidazole, nicotinamide, etc.) · suggested
For ROY, MAE is 0.94 kJ/mol when each method uses its own global minimum but jumps to 7.26 kJ/mol when the PBE-D3 minimum is the common reference
source: FastCSP: Accelerated Molecular Crystal Structure Prediction with Unive (2025) — doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2508.02641
☆
Universal MLIPs degrade for chemistry underrepresented in training data: molecules with rare functional groups or scaffolds (diazo/diazide-carbonyl in Target XVI, aryl-CF2-SO2 linker in Target XXXI) show the worst energy MAE (2.32 and 1.96 kJ/mol) and lowest rank correlations, because the underrepresented group forms the structural core determining packing.
pitfall · scope: OMC25-trained UMA on molecular crystals with functional groups <0.1% of training set · single-study
'molecules with diazo functional groups comprise <0.1% of the training set of the OMC25 dataset'; Target XVI worst-case MAE 2.32 kJ/mol, Spearman 0.51; the 'underrepresented chemistry constitutes the structural core'
source: FastCSP: Accelerated Molecular Crystal Structure Prediction with Unive (2025) — doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2508.02641
