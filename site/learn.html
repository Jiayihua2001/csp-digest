Learn MCSP

A backbone-first map and curriculum of molecular crystal structure prediction — built from 44 digested papers · updated 2026-08-27

How the field fits together

Spine runs history (blind tests as benchmark) -> generation (search) -> ranking (energy accuracy) -> systems (hard cases) -> finite-t (free energy) -> sota (ML era). GAtor and Genarris form the Marom-group backbone linking generation to ranking; the dispersion lineage (TS -> MBD -> PBE0+MBD, Hoja 2019) anchors ranking and feeds finite-t; MLIPs (MACE-OFF, AIMNet2, UMA/FastCSP) are the current frontier accelerating the whole pipeline.

Where GAtor 2.0 sits: GAtor sits at the generation/ranking junction: it is the genetic-algorithm search engine (generation) that couples molecular-crystal breeding operators and ML niching to a hierarchical dispersion-inclusive DFT re-ranking ladder (ranking). GAtor 2.0 development draws on Genarris for seed pools, on the PBE0+MBD gold standard for accuracy targets, and on MLIPs (MACE-OFF, AIMNet2, UMA) to accelerate relaxation and ranking, while the blind tests provide the benchmark and the hard-systems and finite-T branches define its open frontiers (Z'>1, multi-component, free energy).

The knowledge tree

History: the CSP blind tests

The CCDC blind tests are the field's recurring objective benchmark for CSP, running from the first workshop in 1999 through the seventh cycle reported in 2024. The early tests (1-3) documented that structure generation could often capture the experimental structure while lattice-energy ranking was the bottleneck, and that flexible molecules and Z'=2 targets were unsolved. Test 4 marked a turning point with dramatically higher success rates on small rigid molecules and the first cocrystal target; test 5 recognized dispersion-corrected DFT as a reliable ranking method and added a 1:1 salt and polymorphic hydrate; test 6 was the largest to date with salt-hydrate and large flexible targets, documenting the rise of vdW-inclusive DFT, hierarchical workflows, and free-energy corrections. The seventh test split into structure-generation and structure-ranking reports, adding coordination complexes, PXRD-assisted CSP, cocrystal stoichiometry, and blind disorder prediction, and benchmarked MLIPs against periodic DFT-D.

Structure generation & search

Structure generation is the search stage: producing physically reasonable candidate crystals to feed downstream ranking. Two philosophies appear here: random generation with constraints (AIRSS; Genarris) and evolutionary/genetic algorithms (USPEX; GAtor). The Genarris line is the Marom-group generator: Genarris 1.0 introduced the Harris-approximation fast screening and the relative coordinate descriptor with affinity-propagation clustering; Genarris 2.0 added MPI parallelization, ML volume estimation, special Wyckoff positions, and hydrogen-bond-aware cutoffs; Genarris 3.0 added the Rigid Press close-packing algorithm and a MACE-OFF/DFT clustering down-selection workflow. GAtor is the genetic algorithm with molecular-crystal-specific breeding operators and ML-based evolutionary niching to sample the PES uniformly. PyXtal provides symmetric trial-structure generation with arbitrary Z' support.

Energy ranking & accuracy

Energy ranking is the accuracy-limited stage of CSP: polymorphs are typically separated by less than 1 kcal/mol, so ranking must discriminate near-degenerate structures. The dispersion-inclusive DFT lineage runs from empirical vdW corrections (Neumann-Perrin 2005) through the density-derived pairwise TS scheme (2009) to many-body dispersion (MBD, 2012), culminating in the PBE0+MBD+F_vib hierarchical protocol (Hoja et al. 2019) that recovers experimental structures as rank 1 for the 6th blind test single-polymorph systems. Beran's reviews survey the broader electronic-structure toolkit (periodic DFT/MP2, fragment methods, DMC). Adiabatic connection methods (HFAC24) offer a parameter-free post-HF alternative to empirical dispersion corrections for periodic lattice-energy ranking.

Hard systems: salts, cocrystals, flexibility, Z'>1

Hard systems extend CSP beyond single-component, rigid, Z'=1 crystals to salts, cocrystals, hydrates, conformationally flexible molecules, and Z'>1 structures. Blind tests documented these as the persistent frontier: torsional flexibility defeated all participants in early tests, and Z'=2 and multi-component targets were repeatedly the hardest. CrystalPredictor demonstrated early connectivity-only search reaching Z'>1 and less-common space groups; DMACRYS targeted anisotropic potentials for pharmaceutical polymorphs like carbamazepine. The Steed review organizes high-Z' crystals in relation to cocrystals and inclusion compounds. EnvXGen addresses partial-information CSP where part of the cell is fixed (host molecule of a cocrystal, heavy atoms). Facts-and-fictions provides data-driven polymorphism statistics on which molecular properties correlate with polymorphism.

Finite temperature & free energy

Finite-temperature CSP moves from static lattice energy to free energy, since vibrational entropy and thermal expansion can re-rank polymorphs at experimental temperatures. Nyman and Day's large-scale studies quantify static versus vibrational energy differences across 500+ polymorph pairs and report enantiotropic re-ranking between 0 K and melting in about 21% of systems, with thermal expansion generally a small effect. The aspirin PRL shows many-body dispersion coupling to phonons breaks the lattice-energy degeneracy of forms I and II via entropic stabilization. Firaha et al. push toward real-world conditions, computing solid-solid free-energy differences with error bars under temperature and humidity and unifying anhydrate and hydrate stoichiometries on one landscape. Price's CSP_0/CSP_thd/CSP_aim framework articulates the conceptual gap between lattice-energy minimization and thermodynamically actionable prediction.

The ML era & state of the art

The ML era replaces or accelerates expensive DFT stages with machine-learned interatomic potentials. Genarris 3.0 uses MACE-OFF23(L) in early down-selection while flagging its unreliability on chemistries underrepresented in its training set. MACE-OFF is a transferable short-range MLIP for organic molecules; AIMNet2 targets neutral, charged, and element-organic chemistry with a hybrid short-range-ML plus long-range-physics model; the AIMNet2 blind-test work trains target-specific potentials on gas-phase n-mers that transfer to crystalline environments. FastCSP drives an entire end-to-end workflow with a single universal MLIP (UMA), eliminating force-field pre-screening and DFT re-ranking, with an OMC->OMol conformer correction. Wengert's Delta-ML corrects a DFTB baseline with a short-range ML model trained on molecular clusters. Reviews by Bowskill and Beran frame the state of the art around the blind-test history.
