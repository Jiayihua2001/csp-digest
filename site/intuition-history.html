Reliable and practical computational description of molecular crystal polymorphs (2019) · doi:10.1126/sciadv.aau3338

Core idea: A two-stage CSP protocol combining GRACE Monte Carlo parallel-tempering sampling with a hierarchical first-principles energy ranking (PBE0+MBD static lattice energy plus PBE+TS harmonic vibrational free energies) correctly ranks the experimental polymorph as most stable across a diverse set of five sixth-blind-test systems. For the hardest flexible system (XXIII, 5 polymorphs), all experimental forms fall within the top ~4.3 kJ/mol, and finite-temperature/anharmonic corrections further compress them. The work demonstrates that exchange (hybrid), many-body dispersion, and vibrational entropy are all individually necessary to achieve ~1 kJ/mol ranking accuracy.

Innovation: Merges the best-performing sampling engine (Neumann/GRACE) with the best-performing ranking physics (Tkatchenko DFT+MBD) from the same blind test into a single validated, computationally feasible final-refinement protocol, and systematically layers in hybrid exchange, MBD, harmonic Fvib, quasi-harmonic thermal expansion, and Morse anharmonicity to quantify each contribution's effect on relative stability.

☆
Reliable polymorph ranking requires relative (free) energies converged to roughly 1 kJ/mol per molecule, and coexisting/experimentally observed polymorphs typically span only a few kJ/mol.

Assistant synthesis (interpretation): For GAtor 2.0, this paper is the canonical template for the ranking half of MCSP and a cautionary tale about the search half. The GA plays the role GRACE plays here: its job is to guarantee the true low-energy basins survive into a small final pool, because the expensive PBE0+MBD+Fvib refinement can only reorder what it is given and cannot rescue a form the GA never generated (form E fell out of the window). The tiered energy protocol maps directly onto a GAtor descriptor/MLIP funnel: cheap surrogate (MLIP or PBE+TS) for the GA fitness and niching, then PBE+MBD to fix pairwise-dispersion errors, then hybrid exchange for charge-transfer/salt cases, then vibrational entropy last and only on the top few kJ/mol. The message that all three physics ingredients are individually decisive, and system-dependent, argues that MACE-OFF/AIMNet2/UMA surrogates used inside the GA should be validated against PBE0+MBD+Fvib on the low-energy window before trusting rankings, and that finite-T (QHA + anharmonicity) matters most for flexible, entropy-driven systems like XXIII where forms sit within ~1 kJ/mol. The N70 result is a direct motivation for niching that preserves diverse packing motifs (e.g. distinct sheet-stacking) even at similar energy, and a reminder that GA-predicted 'most stable' structures need kinetic/solvent caveats before claiming they are experimentally accessible.

Genarris 3.0: Generating Close-Packed Molecular Crystal Structures with Rigid Press (2025) · doi:10.1021/acs.jctc.5c01080

Core idea: Genarris 3.0 generates random molecular crystal structures in all compatible space groups (Z'=1) and introduces the 'Rigid Press' algorithm, which uses a regularized, differentiable hard-sphere potential to compress the unit cell into a close-packed arrangement based purely on geometry, with no energy evaluations. It is coupled to a clustering-and-down-selection workflow using MLIPs (MACE-OFF23(L)) in early stages and dispersion-inclusive DFT (PBE+MBD) for final ranking, and was demonstrated on six chemically diverse targets.

Innovation: The Rigid Press geometric close-packing algorithm (regularized hard-sphere potential, symmetry-preserving, ~2 orders of magnitude cheaper than MLIP relaxation), combined with a diversity-preserving affinity-propagation clustering and multi-structure-per-cluster selection workflow that mitigates inaccurate MLIP energy rankings; also a systematic assessment of general-purpose MACE-OFF transferability across chemistries including energetic materials.

☆
Energy differences between molecular crystal polymorphs are usually only a few kJ/mol, which is what demands high-accuracy ranking methods.

'the energy differences between polymorphs are usually only a few kJ/mol, requiring high accuracy.' In results, aspirin Form II vs Form I differ by 0.69 kJ/mol (PBE+MBD, 0 K); putative EM structures lie 2.69 kJ/mol (CL-20) and 1.69 kJ/mol (DNI) above experimental forms.
source: Genarris 3.0: Generating Close-Packed Molecular Crystal Structures wit (2025) — doi.org/10.1021/acs.jctc.5c01080
☆
A regularized hard-sphere potential can drive close packing purely geometrically: freezing internal molecular geometry and optimizing lattice vectors, molecular positions and orientations to minimize cell volume subject to intermolecular distance penalties reproduces near-experimental unit cell volumes without any energy evaluation.
principle · scope: Rigid, single-conformer molecules, Z'=1, all space groups; demonstrated on 6 targets including H-bonded, van der Waals, and energetic crystals · single-study
Rigid Press minimizes F(s)=V(s)+P_contact(s) with piecewise differentiable penalty; after Rigid Press, cell volumes are close to experimental (slightly underestimated for aspirin/Target XXII/Target I; very close for dense delta-HMX and DNI). Matches to experiment appear for all targets only after Rigid Press.

'The computational cost of Rigid Press optimization is lower by up to 2 orders of magnitude than relaxation using MLIPs.' For epsilon-CL-20, a match to experiment can only be found with Rigid Press (SI). SI Fig S1 shows Rigid Press pre-optimization reduces MACE-OFF relaxation steps.

Assistant synthesis (interpretation): For GAtor 2.0, Genarris 3.0's Rigid Press is the natural upstream partner: seeding the GA's initial population with geometrically close-packed, symmetry-consistent structures should improve the quality and diversity of the parent pool while cutting expensive relaxation steps, and its pure-geometry nature makes it a cheap 'repair' operator that GA offspring could be passed through before energy evaluation. Finally, the aspirin 0 K/300 K inversion is a reminder that GA rankings and reported landscapes near degeneracy should be treated as provisional until finite-temperature (QHA) corrections are applied.

Genarris 2.0: A random structure generator for molecular crystals (2020) · doi:10.1016/j.cpc.2020.107170

Core idea: Genarris 2.0 is an MPI-parallelized random molecular crystal structure generator that builds physically reasonable structures across all 230 space groups (general and special Wyckoff positions), using a machine-learned solid-form volume predictor, hierarchical intermolecular distance checks, and hydrogen-bond-aware cutoffs, followed by clustering/energy-based down-selection to produce a curated pool for CSP.

Innovation: Versus the original Genarris: MPI/mpi4py parallelization with FHI-aims run inside sub-communicators, a topological-fragment ML model for solid-form volume estimation trained on the CSD, systematic generation on special Wyckoff positions via molecular-axis/viewing-direction alignment, a three-stage hierarchical structure-check scheme (including rigorous non-orthogonal minimum-image via sphere decoding), and element-specific hydrogen-bond cutoff distances.

☆
Solid-form volume (density) of a molecule varies across polymorphs with a standard deviation of ~2.95% - this sets a floor on how accurately any volume-estimation model can predict a specific polymorph's cell.
statistic · scope: Homomolecular organic crystals at room temperature/standard pressure; 2,173 unique polymorph pairs from the 2017 CSD · established
'The distribution has a standard deviation of 2.95% with respect to the solid form volume of the molecule... places a lower bound on the expected accuracy of estimated solid form molecular volumes.'
source: Genarris 2.0: A random structure generator for molecular crystals (2020) — doi.org/10.1016/j.cpc.2020.107170
☆

statistic · scope: Chemically diverse CSD dataset, molecules 5-260 atoms spanning H,C,N,O, halogens, B,P,S,Si,Te,Se · single-study
'the ratio... was found to be 1.47... standard deviation of 4.72% error... the fragment based model achieves an error of similar magnitude to the volume differences between polymorphs... sigma = 2.93%'

'the sum of the vdW radii multiplied by the default sr value of 0.85... exceeds a large number of non-bonded interaction distances... Using the default sr value, structures with strong hydrogen bonds, such as glycine, would be deemed unphysical'; Table 2 gives sr 0.55-0.62 for OH/NH...O/N.

The space group symmetry operations are then applied... The attempted structures that pass the intermolecular distance checks... are added to the raw pool.'

'the goal of the Robust workflow is not necessarily to find the experimental structure but to produce an initial population for a structure search algorithm, such as a genetic algorithm.' source: Genarris 2.0: A random structure generator for molecular crystals (2020) — doi.org/10.1016/j.cpc.2020.107170
Assistant synthesis (interpretation): For GAtor 2.0, Genarris is the front end that seeds the initial GA population, so its biases become the GA's starting biases. Three lessons transfer directly: (1) The ML volume predictor's ~3% floor and its ambient-pressure training bias mean the initial cell volumes for high-pressure or unusually dense targets will be off - worth widening the volume-sampling sigma or seeding hand-built cells when chasing such polymorphs. (2) The special-Wyckoff generation and glide/screw-vs-mirror sampling bias matter for niching: if you want diversity across space groups (and for Z'>1 or high-symmetry molecules like benzene), you must actively ensure special positions and mirror-plane groups are represented, because random generation under-samples them and energy selection can further prune them. (3) Symmetry can change under relaxation, so any GA fingerprint/niching scheme keyed on space group should re-detect symmetry post-relaxation rather than trust the generated label. Finally, the H-bond cutoff insight is a practical reminder that for strongly H-bonded systems (amino acids, pharmaceuticals) generic vdW filters throw away the very dense, physically correct structures the GA needs in its gene pool.

Genarris: Random generation of molecular crystal structures and fast screening with a Harris approximation (2018) · doi:10.1063/1.5014038

Core idea: Genarris generates random molecular crystal structures of rigid molecules under physical constraints (symmetry, cell parameters, close-contact checks), then screens them cheaply using a Harris approximation (superposition of single-molecule densities evaluated with dispersion-inclusive DFT without self-consistency). Machine-learning clustering with a purpose-built relative coordinate descriptor (RCD) plus affinity propagation drives selection into three workflows (Diverse, Energy, Rigorous) tailored to different goals, including seeding genetic algorithms.

Innovation: First application of the Harris approximation to molecular-crystal configuration-space screening (fragments = whole molecules), implemented in FHI-aims with Wigner-matrix basis rotations. Introduces the relative coordinate descriptor (RCD) capturing relative positions/orientations of neighbor molecules rather than interatomic distances, and pairs it with affinity propagation clustering to resolve packing motifs and under-sampled regions.

☆
The Harris approximation (superposition of self-consistent single-molecule densities, energy evaluated without a self-consistency cycle) is accurate for weakly interacting molecules but fails when densities overlap; because it is non-variational, its binding-energy error is always negative and grows asymptotically as molecules get closer.
principle · scope: Rigid molecular crystals/dimers of small organics in the van der Waals regime; PBE+TS@Harris · suggested
HA 'becomes exact when the molecules are far apart'; near equilibrium |dBE| small (0.0740, 0.0049, 0.0288 eV for targets II, XIII, XXII); 'Because of the non-variational nature of the HA, dBE(x) is always negative and its magnitude increases asymptotically with decreasing distance.'
source: Genarris: Random generation of molecular crystal structures and fast s (2018) — doi.org/10.1063/1.5014038
☆
