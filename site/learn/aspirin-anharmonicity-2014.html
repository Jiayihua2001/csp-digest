Role of Dispersion Interactions in the Polymorphism and Entropic Stabilization of the Aspirin Crystal

Anthony M. Reilly, Alexandre Tkatchenko · Physical Review Letters (2014) · 202 citations · doi:10.1103/physrevlett.113.055701 confidence: low ⚠ verification flagged claims
> This PRL uses first-principles calculations to explain the polymorphism of aspirin, which has two known crystal forms (I and II) that are essentially degenerate in lattice energy. The authors argue that room-temperature free-energy differences, rather than purely kinetic effects, explain why form I is more abundant. The key finding is that explicitly including many-body van der Waals (dispersion) interactions in the free energy reveals a subtle coupling between collective electronic fluctuations and quantized lattice vibrations (phonons) that stabilizes form I. They also analyze elastic properties and rule out mechanical instability as the source of form II metastability. Contribution

Provides a free-energy (entropic/vibrational) explanation for aspirin polymorphism as an alternative to kinetic arguments, demonstrating that many-body van der Waals interactions couple to lattice vibrations to break the lattice-energy degeneracy between forms I and II at room temperature.

Method

First-principles calculations of lattice energies, free energies including vibrational (phonon) contributions, and elastic properties for the two aspirin polymorphs. Many-body van der Waals interactions are explicitly incorporated into the free energy (consistent with Tkatchenko-group MBD methodology, though specifics are not confirmed from the abstract). Elastic property analysis is used to assess mechanical stability.

Key results

- The two observed aspirin forms are reported to be degenerate in lattice energy (no numeric value given in abstract) - Explicit inclusion of many-body van der Waals interactions in the free energy produces a coupling between collective electronic fluctuations and quantized lattice vibrations that stabilizes the most abundant form (form I) - Elastic property analysis rules out mechanical instability of form II as the cause of its metastability - Specific numerical values (energy differences, free-energy differences, temperatures beyond 'room temperature') are not available from the abstract

Limitations

- Digest based on abstract/metadata only; quantitative results, functional/method details, and error bars are unavailable - Abstract does not state which DFT functional or exact dispersion scheme (e.g. MBD vs TS) was used - Focused on a single-component, well-studied molecular crystal (aspirin); generalization to broader CSP not addressed in abstract - Phonon/free-energy calculations at finite temperature typically rely on harmonic or quasi-harmonic approximations, but the specific approximation is not stated in the abstract

Takeaways

- Lattice-energy degeneracy does not settle polymorph ranking; finite-temperature free energy (vibrational entropy) can be decisive - Many-body dispersion matters not just for static lattice energies but also through its coupling to lattice vibrations, affecting phonon free energies - For GAtor 2.0 ranking of near-degenerate polymorphs, static PBE0+MBD lattice energies may be insufficient; free-energy/phonon corrections may be needed to distinguish forms - Elastic/mechanical stability analysis can independently rule out dynamical instability as a cause of metastability

Relevance to GAtor 2.0

Highly relevant to finite-temperature stability and lattice-energy ranking in GAtor 2.0. Aspirin is a canonical CSP benchmark, and this work shows that near-degenerate polymorphs may require free-energy (vibrational, entropic) corrections beyond static dispersion-inclusive DFT to rank correctly. It directly motivates coupling MBD dispersion to phonon calculations for ranking, relevant to any pipeline that stops at static PBE0+MBD energies.
