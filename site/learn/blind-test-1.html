A test of crystal structure prediction of small organic molecules

Jos P. M. Lommerse, W.D.S. Motherwell, Herman L. Ammon, Jack D. Dunitz, A. Gavezzotti, Detlef W. M. Hofmann · Acta Crystallographica Section B Structural Science (2000) · 445 citations · doi:10.1107/s0108768100004584 confidence: low ⚠ verification flagged claims
> This is the report on the first CCDC blind test of crystal structure prediction, held as a collaborative workshop in May 1999 at the Cambridge Crystallographic Data Centre. Given only atomic connectivity for four small organic compounds, 11 participants using a range of available CSP programs each submitted up to three predicted structures per compound. Only seven submitted structures were judged 'correct' (close to an experimental structure), and no single program was consistently reliable. The paper diagnoses that lattice-energy-based CSP is fundamentally limited by the presence of many structures within a few kJ/mol of the global minimum, so force-field details and parametrization strongly affect ranking. Contribution

Established the first organized, community-wide blind test of CSP for small organic molecules, providing a benchmark and honest assessment of the state of the field circa 1999. Framed the central difficulty that would dominate CSP for decades: dense energy landscapes with many near-degenerate structures near the global minimum.

Method

Blind test format: four target compounds selected, atomic connectivity provided to participants, no experimental structures revealed. Each of 11 participants allowed to submit at most three candidate structures per compound using their own CSP methodologies (principal computer programs of the time). Submissions compared against known experimental crystal structures and classified as correct or not.

Key results

- 11 participants, 4 target compounds, at most 3 proposed structures allowed per compound per participant - Seven proposed structures were classified as 'correct' (close to an experimental structure) across the whole test - No program gave consistently reliable results - One of the compounds had two polymorphs, but only one polymorph was predicted correctly among the calculated structures - Many candidate structures were found within a few kJ/mol of the global minimum, making energy ranking highly sensitive to force-field methodology and parametrization

Limitations

- Digest based on abstract/metadata only; specific compounds, methods, and per-compound success breakdown not available here - Lattice-energy-based methods cannot reliably discriminate the true structure when many structures lie within a few kJ/mol of the minimum - Force-field detail and parametrization dominate ranking, indicating energy models of the era were not accurate enough - Small sample (four compounds, small organic molecules only) limits generality - Reflects circa-1999 methods; predates modern dispersion-inclusive DFT and MLIP approaches

Takeaways

- This is the origin point (CSP2000) of the CCDC blind test series that GAtor and modern CSP methods are ultimately benchmarked against - The core diagnosis - many structures within a few kJ/mol of the global minimum - motivates the need for high-accuracy ranking energetics such as PBE0+MBD in GAtor 2.0 - Even when the correct structure is generated, ranking is the bottleneck; polymorph discrimination was already recognized as hard here - Provides historical baseline: seven correct out of a limited pool shows how immature reliable CSP was, useful for framing progress narratives

Relevance to GAtor 2.0

Directly relevant as the founding blind test in the CSP blind-test series that GAtor targets. The paper's central lesson - dense low-energy landscapes and ranking sensitivity to force fields - is exactly the problem GAtor 2.0 addresses via dispersion-inclusive DFT (PBE0+MBD) ranking and, increasingly, MLIPs. Useful historical citation for motivating accurate lattice-energy ranking and for contextualizing later blind tests.
