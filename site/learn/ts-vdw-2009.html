Accurate Molecular Van Der Waals Interactions from Ground-State Electron Density and Free-Atom Reference Data

Alexandre Tkatchenko, Matthias Scheffler · Physical Review Letters (2009) · 6356 citations · doi:10.1103/physrevlett.102.073005 confidence: low ⚠ verification flagged claims
> This PRL introduces the Tkatchenko-Scheffler (TS) method, a parameter-free scheme for computing long-range van der Waals (vdW) interactions from mean-field electronic structure calculations. Effective atomic C6 dispersion coefficients are derived by combining the ground-state electron density of a molecule or solid (via Hirshfeld partitioning, implied) with accurate free-atom reference data. Because the effective atomic volume and hence C6 coefficient adapts to the local bonding environment, the method captures chemical-environment dependence of dispersion without empirical fitting. The abstract reports a mean absolute error of 5.5% in C6 coefficients versus experimental values for 1225 intermolecular pairs, largely independent of the exchange-correlation functional used. The paper also analyzes vdW radii and the damping function in the C6*R^(-6) DFT correction. Corpus: tier D - `ts-vdw-2009` - source mode: metadata - confidence: low - FLAGGED - see below

Contribution

A parameter-free (per the abstract), density-derived pairwise C6/R^6 dispersion correction for DFT in which effective atomic C6 coefficients respond to the local bonding environment, using free-atom reference data plus the self-consistent electron density.

Method

Effective atomic C6 coefficients derived from the ground-state electron density combined with accurate free-atom reference data; summation of interatomic C6 coefficients for the long-range dispersion energy; analysis of vdW radii and a damping function for the C6*R^(-6) correction to DFT. Specific partitioning scheme details are not stated in the abstract.

Key results

- Mean absolute error in C6 coefficients of 5.5% versus accurate experimental values across 1225 intermolecular pairs - Accuracy reported as largely irrespective of the employed exchange-correlation functional - Effective atomic C6 coefficients depend strongly on the bonding environment of an atom in a molecule

Limitations

- Digest based on abstract/metadata only; full-text details unavailable - Pairwise C6/R^6 formulation does not include higher-order (C8, C10) or many-body/beyond-pairwise dispersion terms (the later MBD method addresses this) - Requires a damping function whose parametrization couples to the vdW radii and the chosen functional - Validation reported for C6 coefficients against experiment, not directly for lattice or binding energies in the abstract

Takeaways

- This is the foundational TS dispersion method underlying later PBE0+MBD workflows relevant to molecular crystal lattice-energy ranking - Environment-dependent atomic polarizabilities/C6 (via density) are the key idea distinguishing TS from fixed-coefficient DFT-D schemes - Provides the density-based C6 machinery that MBD (Tkatchenko et al.) later extends to include many-body screening

Relevance to GAtor 2.0

Directly relevant: TS dispersion is the ancestor of the PBE0+MBD dispersion-inclusive DFT commonly used to rank molecular crystal candidate structures in GAtor workflows. Understanding the origin of the effective atomic C6 coefficients and damping function contextualizes accuracy and failure modes of dispersion-inclusive lattice-energy rankings.
