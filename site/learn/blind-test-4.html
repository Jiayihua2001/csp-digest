Significant progress in predicting the crystal structures of small organic molecules – a report on the fourth blind test

Graeme M. Day, Timothy G. Cooper, Aurora J. Cruz‐Cabeza, K.E. Hejczyk, Herman L. Ammon, S. X. M. Boerrigter · Acta Crystallographica Section B Structural Science (2009) · 448 citations · doi:10.1107/s0108768109004066 confidence: low ⚠ verification flagged claims
> This paper reports the fourth blind test of organic crystal structure prediction (CSP), organized to benchmark the state of computational CSP methods. Fourteen research groups predicted crystal structures for four targets (three single-component molecular crystals and one 1:1 cocrystal) given only molecular diagrams, with the experimental structures withheld by an independent referee. Each group could submit three predictions per target. The abstract reports a marked improvement over prior blind tests, with 13 successful predictions overall and at least two groups correct for each target; one group correctly predicted all four as their top-ranked choice at high computational cost. Contribution

Provides a community-wide benchmark demonstrating a dramatic improvement in CSP success rates for small, fairly rigid organic molecules, including the first inclusion of a cocrystal target, and establishes that CSP can be constructively applied to study crystallization and polymorphism.

Method

Collaborative blind test: an independent referee withheld recently determined, unpublished crystal structures; 14 groups generated and ranked candidate structures using a variety of (unspecified in the abstract) methods from molecular diagrams alone; three predictions allowed per target across four systems.

Key results

- 14 research groups participated - 4 target systems: 3 single-component crystals and 1 1:1 cocrystal - 3 predictions allowed per target - 13 successful predictions in total - At least 2 groups correctly predicted the observed structure for each of the 4 targets - One group correctly predicted all 4 structures as their first-ranked choice, at considerable computational expense

Limitations

- Digest based on abstract/metadata only; full-text details unavailable - Abstract does not name the specific methods, energy models, or ranking procedures used by each group - Success limited to small and fairly rigid molecules; larger or flexible molecules not addressed - No quantitative energy-ranking accuracy or RMSD figures reported in the abstract - The one fully successful group's approach was computationally very expensive

Takeaways

- The 2007/2009 fourth blind test marks a turning point where CSP became reliable for small rigid organic molecules, useful historical context for GAtor benchmarking - First blind test to include a cocrystal (multi-component) target, relevant to multi-component CSP work - Demonstrates that top-ranked (rank-1) success is achievable but was tied to high computational cost, motivating cheaper ranking approaches like MLIPs - Establishes the blind-test framework and referee-withheld-structure methodology that later CSP blind tests build on

Relevance to GAtor 2.0

Directly relevant as a foundational CSP blind-test benchmark predating GAtor; the cocrystal target connects to multi-component/Z'>1 crystal prediction, and the noted trade-off between rank-1 accuracy and computational cost motivates modern lattice-energy ranking (dispersion-inclusive DFT, MLIPs) that GAtor 2.0 targets. Useful as historical baseline when framing progress in GA-based CSP.
