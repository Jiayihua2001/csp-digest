The seventh blind test of crystal structure prediction: structure generation methods

Lily M. Hunnisett, Jonas Nyman, Nicholas Francia, Nathan S. Abraham, Claire S. Adjiman, Srinivasulu Aitipamula · Acta Crystallographica Section B Structural Science Crystal Engineering and Materials (2024) · 83 citations · doi:10.1107/s2052520624007492 confidence: low ⚠ verification flagged claims
> This is the first of two reports on the seventh CCDC blind test of crystal structure prediction (CSP), focusing on structure generation methods. Seven target systems of varying complexity were used: a Si- and I-containing molecule, a copper coordination complex, a near-rigid molecule, a cocrystal, a polymorphic small agrochemical, a highly flexible polymorphic drug candidate, and a polymorphic morpholine salt. According to the abstract, many CSP methods reproduced the experimental structures for the small flexible agrochemical, while few groups succeeded on the higher-complexity systems. A PXRD-assisted exercise showed CSP can solve a structure from a low-quality powder pattern, and cocrystal stoichiometry prediction was explored via multiple approaches. Crystallographic disorder was a recurring theme, and two groups blindly predicted disorder for the first time. Large-scale comparisons showed some methods generate largely overlapping structure sets. Contribution

Reports the community-wide seventh blind test results specifically for structure generation, expanding target complexity (coordination complexes, salts, cocrystals, flexible drug candidates), and documents new capabilities: PXRD-assisted CSP from low-quality data, cocrystal stoichiometry prediction, and first-time blind prediction of crystallographic disorder.

Method

Organized blind test by the Cambridge Crystallographic Data Centre with multiple participating groups submitting predicted crystal structures for seven targets. Includes a PXRD-assisted structure-determination exercise, cocrystal stoichiometry prediction tasks, and large-scale comparison of predicted structure sets across methods. Specific methods and metrics not available from abstract only.

Key results

- No quantitative numbers reported in the abstract - Many CSP methods successfully reproduced experimental structures for the small flexible agrochemical - Few groups were successful for the higher-complexity systems - A PXRD-assisted exercise successfully determined a crystal structure from a low-quality PXRD pattern - Two groups blindly predicted the existence of disorder for the first time - Comparisons showed some methods yield largely overlapping sets of predicted structures

Limitations

- Digest based on abstract/metadata only; full methods, numerical success rates, and per-target results are unavailable - Abstract does not name which groups or methods succeeded or failed - No quantitative ranking or energy-based results discussed in the abstract (this part focuses on structure generation; ranking presumably covered in the companion part 2)

Takeaways

- The seventh blind test broadened target scope to coordination complexes, salts, cocrystals, and highly flexible drug candidates - relevant stress tests for GA-based generators - Disorder and Z'>1/multi-component crystals remain frontier challenges; blind disorder prediction is a notable new achievement - Overlap in predicted structure sets across methods raises questions about sampling diversity - relevant to GA niching and Genarris generation strategy - PXRD-assisted CSP and cocrystal stoichiometry prediction are demonstrated practical use cases - Consult the companion part 2 (ranking methods) for lattice-energy/DFT results not in this structure-generation part

Relevance to GAtor 2.0

Directly relevant as the current benchmark for GA molecular CSP. The flexible agrochemical and drug candidate targets test conformational sampling; the cocrystal, salt, and coordination complex targets test multi-component and Z'>1 handling that GAtor 2.0 and Genarris must address. The finding that some methods produce overlapping structure sets speaks to the value of GA niching for diversity. Ranking/dispersion-DFT details are in the separate companion paper.
