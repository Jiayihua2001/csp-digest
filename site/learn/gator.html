Takeaways

- GAtor is a Python, massively parallel first-principles genetic algorithm for molecular [[concepts/crystal-structure-prediction|CSP]] — local optimizations and energy evaluations done with dispersion-inclusive DFT via [[entities/fhi-aims|FHI-aims]]. - Offers configurable fitness, selection, crossover, and mutation operators; breeding operators are designed specifically for molecular crystals to balance exploration vs. exploitation. - Implements evolutionary niching: ML clusters the dynamically-updated population by structural similarity, then applies a cluster-based fitness function. Aim is to evolve subpopulations and overcome initial-pool bias / genetic drift, sampling the [[concepts/crystal-structure-prediction|PES]] more uniformly. - Best structures are re-relaxed and re-ranked using a hierarchy of increasingly accurate DFT functionals and dispersion methods. - Applied to four past [[concepts/csp-blind-tests|CCDC blind-test]] targets covering different intermolecular-interaction types. Experimentally observed structures and other low-energy structures were located for all four. - For Target II (5-cyano-3-hydroxythiophene), the top-ranked putative structure is a *Z′* = 2, *P*1̅ scaffold-packing motif not previously reported in the experimental literature. - Highly cited in the molecular-CSP / CCDC blind-test community: 114 citing publications at clip time, with citation #1 being the [[sources/2026-05-02_genarris-3|Genarris 3.0 (2025)]] paper from the same group — a direct lineage to Jade's other ingest.

Summary

The paper introduces [[entities/gator|GAtor]] ("Genetic Algorithm for crystals") — a Python framework for predicting the crystal structures of molecules from first principles using a genetic algorithm. The motivation is the standard CSP problem: organic molecules can crystallize in many [[concepts/polymorphism|polymorphs]], the energy differences between polymorphs are typically only a few kJ/mol, and exhaustive DFT-quality sampling of the configuration space is computationally prohibitive. A genetic algorithm reduces the number of energy evaluations needed by evolving a population of candidate structures toward low-energy regions. GAtor's design pivots on three things the abstract emphasizes: 1. Custom breeding operators for molecular crystals. Rather than reusing genetic operators developed for atomic crystals or general optimization problems, the authors define crossover and mutation operations that respect molecular integrity and crystal-packing physics. This is what gives the algorithm its exploration/exploitation balance for this domain. 2. Evolutionary niching via ML clustering. A perennial failure mode of genetic algorithms is that the population converges prematurely to a single basin (genetic drift), missing other low-energy minima. GAtor counters this by clustering the live population by structural similarity (using machine learning) and applying a cluster-based fitness function, so subpopulations evolve in parallel within their own niches. The abstract claims this allows discovery of structures "located in disconnected, hard to reach regions of the potential energy landscape." 3. First-principles re-ranking hierarchy. After GA convergence, the best candidates are re-relaxed and re-ranked using progressively more accurate DFT functionals and dispersion methods — keeping the GA itself cheap while pushing final accuracy. The validation set is four targets from previous CCDC blind tests, chosen to span different interaction types (hydrogen bonds, weak van der Waals, etc.). The abstract reports success on all four: GAtor finds the experimentally observed structure for each, *plus* additional low-energy candidates. The flagged result is Target II (5-cyano-3-hydroxythiophene), where GAtor's top-ranked prediction is a *Z′* = 2 (two molecules in the asymmetric unit), *P*1̅ scaffold-packing structure — not previously reported in the published experimental literature. The implication, though the abstract doesn't say it explicitly, is a putative new polymorph predicted by the method. The paper is from the [[entities/noa-marom|Marom]] group at CMU (Materials Science / Physics / Chemistry) and was published in J. Chem. Theory Comput. in February 2018. As of the clip, it has 114 citing publications, with the first citation being Yang/Tom/Wui/Moussa/Marom 2025 — i.e. [[sources/2026-05-02_genarris-3|Genarris 3.0]], the companion paper Jade ingested at the same time. GAtor and [[entities/genarris|Genarris]] are companion tools in the same group's CSP toolkit: Genarris generates initial structures (random + Rigid Press), GAtor evolves them. The Genarris 3.0 paper makes this relationship explicit.
