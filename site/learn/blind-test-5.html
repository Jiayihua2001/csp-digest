Towards crystal structure prediction of complex organic compounds – a report on the fifth blind test

David A. Bardwell, Claire S. Adjiman, Yelena A. Arnautova, Ekaterina Bartashevich, S. X. M. Boerrigter, Doris E. Braun · Acta Crystallographica Section B Structural Science (2011) · 433 citations · doi:10.1107/s0108768111042868 confidence: low ⚠ verification flagged claims
> This is the report on the fifth crystal structure prediction blind test (CSP2010), organized at the Cambridge Crystallographic Data Centre following CSP1999, 2001, 2004, and 2007. Participating groups used a range of computational methodologies to predict the crystal structures of six organic target molecules. The first four targets (two rigid molecules, one semi-flexible molecule, and a 1:1 salt) matched CSP2007 criteria, while two new challenging targets were added: a larger, much more flexible molecule and a hydrate with more than one polymorph. Each group submitted three predictions per attempted target. Corpus: tier A - `blind-test-5` - source mode: metadata - confidence: low - FLAGGED - see below

Contribution

Provides a community-wide benchmark of CSP methods circa 2010, notably showing that dispersion-corrected DFT (DFT-D) can reliably predict smaller organic crystal structures, and extending the blind test to more complex targets (large flexible molecule, polymorphic hydrate).

Method

Collaborative blind test: CCDC selected six organic target molecules; participating groups predicted structures blind and submitted three ranked predictions per target; predictions compared against experimentally determined structures. (Specific per-group methods not available from abstract.)

Key results

- At least one successful prediction was achieved for each of the six targets - Two groups successfully predicted the large flexible molecule as their first-place submission - Fewer groups successfully predicted the three smallest molecules than in CSP2007 - Evidence that dispersion-corrected DFT (DFT-D) can reliably predict the smallest structures - Six targets: two rigid, one semi-flexible, one 1:1 salt, one large flexible molecule, one hydrate with more than one polymorph

Limitations

- Digest based on abstract/metadata only; full methodology, ranking statistics, and per-target success rates unavailable - Complex systems (large flexible molecule, polymorphic hydrate) still posed many challenges and unresolved issues - Success on the three smallest molecules declined relative to CSP2007

Takeaways

- CSP2010 marks the point where DFT-D became recognized as a reliable ranking method for small-molecule organic crystals - Molecular flexibility, salts, and hydrates/polymorphism remain the hard frontier for CSP - Historical benchmark context useful for positioning modern GA-CSP and MLIP-based approaches against 2010-era capabilities

Relevance to GAtor 2.0

Directly relevant as historical context for lattice-energy ranking and the CSP blind test series that GAtor and Genarris are benchmarked against. Establishes the DFT-D reliability baseline and the difficulty tiers (flexibility, salts, hydrates, multi-component) that GAtor 2.0 aims to address; however, no GA-specific or MBD/PBE0 details are extractable from the abstract alone.
