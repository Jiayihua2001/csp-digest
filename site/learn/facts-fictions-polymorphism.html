Facts and fictions about polymorphism

Aurora J. Cruz‐Cabeza, Susan M. Reutzel‐Edens, Joel Bernstein · Chemical Society Reviews (2015) · 682 citations · doi:10.1039/c5cs00227c confidence: low ⚠ verification flagged claims
> A review analyzing polymorphism using three data sources: crystallographic data from the Cambridge Structural Database (CSD), 229 industrial solid form screens from Hoffmann-La Roche and Eli Lilly, and a computed dataset of 446 polymorphic crystals with DFT-d energies and properties. The authors present statistics on how often compounds are polymorphic and which molecular properties (flexibility, size, chirality, hydrogen bonding) correlate with polymorphism, and characterize the energy differences between polymorphs. Corpus: tier C - `facts-fictions-polymorphism` - source mode: metadata - confidence: low - FLAGGED - see below

Contribution

Provides data-driven 'facts' about polymorphism that debunk common assumptions, quantifying polymorphism frequencies and property correlations from large crystallographic and industrial screening datasets plus a curated DFT-d energy dataset.

Method

Statistical analysis of (i) CSD crystallographic data, (ii) 229 industrial solid form screens at Roche (8+ years) and Lilly (15+ years), and (iii) a dataset of 446 polymorphic crystals with energies and properties computed via modern DFT-d methods. Details of the DFT-d functional/dispersion scheme are not available from the abstract.

Key results

- Molecular flexibility or size has no correlation with the ability of a compound to be polymorphic - Chiral molecules are less prone to polymorphism than achiral counterparts - Hydrogen-bonding compounds show only a slightly higher propensity to polymorphism than non-hydrogen-bonding ones - Energy difference between polymorphs is usually less than 1 kcal/mol - Conformational polymorphs can differ by larger values, up to 2.5 kcal/mol in their dataset - One in three compounds in the CSD are polymorphic - At least one in two compounds in the Roche and Lilly set display polymorphism, rising up to three in four under intensive screening

Limitations

- Digest based on abstract/metadata only; full methodology and DFT-d details not available - CSD-based statistics are subject to reporting/deposition biases (polymorphs may be under-reported) - Industrial screening frequencies depend on screening intensity, giving a range rather than a fixed value - Predicting and realizing targeted polymorphism remains unsolved ('holy grail')

Takeaways

- Polymorphism is common: roughly 1/3 of CSD compounds, up to 3/4 with intensive screening, relevant framing for CSP energy-window justification - The small typical polymorph energy gap (<1 kcal/mol, up to ~2.5 for conformational) sets a demanding accuracy target for lattice-energy ranking in CSP - Molecular flexibility/size do not predict polymorphism, so structure generation should not deprioritize based on these features - Provides empirical priors useful for evaluating CSP predictions against realistic polymorph counts and energy spreads

Relevance to GAtor 2.0

Highly relevant for calibrating CSP energy windows and ranking accuracy: the reported <1 kcal/mol typical polymorph gap (up to 2.5 kcal/mol for conformational polymorphs) directly informs how tight lattice-energy ranking (e.g. PBE0+MBD, MLIPs) must be to resolve real polymorphs. The polymorphism-frequency statistics and property correlations give useful priors when judging GAtor outputs.
