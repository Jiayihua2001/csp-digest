Modelling temperature-dependent properties of polymorphic organic molecular crystals

Jonas Nyman, Graeme M. Day · Physical Chemistry Chemical Physics (2016) · 103 citations · doi:10.1039/c6cp05447a confidence: low ⚠ verification flagged claims
> Nyman and Day report a large-scale computational study of the temperature dependence of structures, free energy differences, and properties across 475 pairs of polymorphs (864 crystal structures) of organic molecular crystals. Using an anisotropic multipole-based force field, they compute lattice-vibrational Gibbs free energy differences at 0 K and at each system's melting point, incorporating thermal expansion via a negative thermal pressure. They assess how often thermal effects re-rank polymorph stability (indicating possible enantiotropic phase transitions), and characterize thermal expansion coefficients and temperature-dependent mechanical properties. Contribution

Provides statistics, from a large polymorph dataset, on how vibrational free energy and thermal expansion change relative polymorph stability with temperature, quantifying how frequently finite-temperature effects can invert 0 K stability rankings and thus matter for CSP.

Method

Anisotropic atomic multipole-based force field; lattice-dynamics (harmonic/quasi-harmonic) calculation of vibrational Gibbs free energies at 0 K and at melting points; thermal expansion modeled through an applied negative thermal pressure rather than full quasi-harmonic phonon optimization; computed over 475 polymorph pairs / 864 structures. Exact method details unavailable (abstract only).

Key results

- 475 polymorph pairs (864 crystal structures) studied - Enantiotropic re-ranking of relative thermodynamic stability occurs in 21% of studied systems between 0 K and the melting point - Vibrational contributions to free energies can significantly affect thermodynamic stability - Impact of thermal expansion on polymorph free energy differences is generally very small - Thermal expansion coefficients and temperature-dependent mechanical properties were computed for the structures (specific numerical values not available from abstract)

Limitations

- Digest based on abstract/metadata only; no full text reviewed - Uses an empirical anisotropic multipole force field rather than dispersion-inclusive DFT (e.g. PBE0+MBD), so accuracy relative to first-principles ranking is unstated here - Thermal expansion approximated via a negative thermal pressure rather than explicit free-energy minimization vs volume - Numerical distributions of free energy differences, expansion coefficients, and mechanical properties not extractable from abstract

Takeaways

- Finite-temperature free energy corrections matter: about 1 in 5 polymorph pairs can re-rank between 0 K and melting, relevant to lattice-energy ranking in CSP - Thermal expansion has a small net effect on polymorph free energy differences, so vibrational (zero-point plus thermal) contributions dominate temperature-driven re-ranking - A negative-thermal-pressure trick is a cheap way to approximate thermal expansion effects at scale - Supports incorporating vibrational free energy into GAtor ranking workflows for temperature-relevant predictions

Relevance to GAtor 2.0

Directly relevant to finite-temperature stability and lattice-energy ranking in molecular CSP: quantifies how often 0 K rankings (as produced by GAtor's energy evaluation) are overturned by vibrational free energy at finite temperature, motivating free-energy re-ranking of GA-generated polymorphs. The 21% re-ranking statistic is a useful benchmark for whether static ranking suffices.
