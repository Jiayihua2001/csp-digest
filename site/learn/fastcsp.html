FastCSP: Accelerated Molecular Crystal Structure Prediction with Universal Model for Atoms

Vahe Gharakhanyan, Yi Yang, Luis Barroso-Luque, Daniel S. Levine, Sahoo, Sushree Jagriti, Brandon M. Wood · arXiv (Cornell University) (2025) · 1 citations · doi:10.48550/arxiv.2508.02641 confidence: high ⚠ verification flagged claims
> FastCSP is an open-source, end-to-end molecular crystal structure prediction workflow driven entirely by a single pretrained universal MLIP (UMA), eliminating both classical force field pre-screening and DFT-based re-ranking. It integrates RDKit/UMA-OMol conformer generation, Genarris 3 random structure generation with Rigid Press, UMA-OMC geometry relaxation and lattice-energy ranking, and optional UMA free-energy (QHA) and OMC->OMol conformer-energy corrections. Benchmarked on 28 semi-rigid and 10 flexible molecules spanning 74 experimental polymorphs, it recovers all known structures within 9 kJ/mol of the predicted global minimum and reproduces PBE-D3 with high fidelity at a fraction of the cost. The workflow exploits the complementarity of UMA's OMC task (periodic crystals at PBE-D3 level) and OMol task (finite molecules at wB97M-V level), with the conformer correction being especially beneficial for conformational polymorphs such as ROY. Contribution

Demonstrates that a single pretrained universal MLIP (UMA) can support a complete molecular CSP workflow with DFT-level accuracy and no system-specific fine-tuning or DFT re-ranking, and releases the entire pipeline (FastCSP) open-source. Introduces an OMC->OMol conformer-energy correction scheme that combines OMC crystal-packing accuracy with OMol monomer-conformation accuracy to fix GGA delocalization errors.

Method

Pipeline: (1) RDKit multi-sampler conformer generation (4 ETKDGv3/MMFF passes to 500 conformers, Butina clustering, UMA-OMol relaxation, energy gating to 40 kJ/mol window); (2) Genarris 3 random crystal generation across compatible space groups (Z in 1,2,3,4,6,8; Z'<=1) with PyMoVE volume estimation and Rigid Press compression, StructureMatcher deduplication; (3) UMA-Small v1.1 OMC-task BFGS relaxation of positions+cell (0.01 eV/A, up to 1000 steps) with second deduplication; (4) optional harmonic/quasi-harmonic free energies via Phonopy supercells fit to Vinet EOS; (5) optional OMC->OMol conformer correction on Z fragments. Validation against PBE-D3 (VASP, OMC25 settings) via RMSD30 (COMPACK) and lattice-energy parity; glycine free energies benchmarked against FHI-aims PBE-D3, PBE+MBD, and PBE0+MBD.

Key results

- Semi-rigid subset: >94% recall within top 10 predictions and 100% within 5 kJ/mol of the 0 K lattice-energy global minimum; flexible subset: >81% recall within top 50 and 100% within 9 kJ/mol. - Single-polymorph systems (26 targets): experimental structure ranked as global minimum in 14/26 cases and within top 5 in 9 additional cases; all within 5 kJ/mol of predicted minimum. - Multi-polymorph systems (12 targets): one experimental polymorph ranked as global minimum in 9/12 cases and within top 5 for the remaining 3; all experimental polymorphs within 9 kJ/mol. - UMA vs PBE-D3 relaxed structures: 99% average match rate at RMSD30 < 1 A and 91% at stricter RMSD30 < 0.5 A; mean RMSD30 of 0.19 A across the benchmark. - UMA vs PBE-D3 relative lattice energies: MAE 0.84 kJ/mol (semi-rigid) and 0.90 kJ/mol (flexible), Spearman 0.76 and 0.85; worst cases Target XVI (MAE 2.32 kJ/mol, Spearman 0.51) and Target XXXI (MAE 1.96 kJ/mol), whose chemistry is underrepresented in OMC25. - ROY: UMA-OMC lattice energy ranks the experimentally most stable Y form at #336 (7.14 kJ/mol above ORP); OMC->OMol conformer correction promotes Y to rank #1 and recovers experimental order Y > YT04 > R, matching B86bPBE-XDM+SCS-MP2D results. - Glycine: UMA-OMC and PBE-D3 both give alpha > gamma > beta (0 K), disagreeing with experimental gamma > alpha > beta; conformer correction brings gamma to only 0.11 kJ/mol above alpha but does not fully reverse order (hybrid+MBD-level intermolecular treatment still needed). - Cost: each MLIP relaxation step averages ~5,600x cheaper (CPU-core-hour per GPU-hour) than a PBE-D3 ionic step; initial DFT single-point ~22,000x more expensive; full campaign ~10^5 GPU-hours vs estimated 1.6x10^9 CPU-core-hours for DFT equivalent. - QHA free-energy corrections at 300 K worsened recall for semi-rigid molecules but improved it for flexible molecules; effect on polymorph ranking was non-uniform, consistent with seventh blind test findings, attributed to PBE-D3/QHA limitations. - Conformer correction fixed Target X and Target XXII rankings to #1 (GGA conjugation/delocalization errors) but worsened Target XVIII from #1 to #22.

Limitations

- Free-energy (QHA) corrections are not universally beneficial and did not systematically improve rankings; anharmonic effects are neglected and become significant at higher temperature (deviations from experiment near 200 K for glycine heat capacity). - UMA accuracy degrades for chemistry underrepresented in OMC25 (diazo groups <0.1% of training set for Target XVI; the aryl-CF2-SO2 linker of Target XXXI entirely absent), causing higher lattice-energy MAEs. - Conformer correction assumes the DFT functional describes intermolecular interactions well; for glycine (hydrogen-bonded zwitterions) it does not fully recover the correct order, requiring hybrid functionals with many-body dispersion. - Olanzapine Forms I and IV were not generated with default settings (1500 structures/space group); required increasing to 3000 structures/space group and 5 conformers. - Benchmark restricted to Z'<=1 structures; Z'>1 and multi-component crystals are outside the demonstrated scope, and ROY Form C (Z=18) and Target XXXI Form C were excluded. - Absolute polymorph ordering still fails in several flexible cases (e.g., Target XXXI Form B, olanzapine at 0 K, ROY without correction), inheriting PBE-D3/wB97M-V functional limitations rather than MLIP error. - DFT validation only used PBE-D3 as reference (matching OMC25 training level); higher-level PBE0+MBD comparison performed only for glycine.

Takeaways

- A single pretrained universal MLIP (UMA) can replace both classical force fields and DFT re-ranking in molecular CSP with PBE-D3-level fidelity, directly relevant to modernizing the ranking stage of GA-based workflows like GAtor. - The OMC->OMol conformer-energy correction is a cheap, training-free remedy for GGA delocalization error in conformational polymorphs (ROY, Target X, Target XXII), addressing a long-standing DFT limitation in CSP ranking. - Genarris 3 with Rigid Press remains the structure generator, and heavy deduplication (67% semi-rigid, 43% flexible removed) plus UMA relaxation make exhaustive sampling tractable; conformer pools of ~20 (often <10 sufficient) cover experimentally relevant conformations. - MLIP phonon spectra from UMA match PBE-D3 closely (low-frequency modes, no imaginary modes for glycine), but QHA free-energy corrections remain unreliable for ranking small energy differences due to neglected anharmonicity. - For very small polymorph energy gaps (glycine), even wB97M-V-level monomer corrections are insufficient; accurate intermolecular electrostatics and many-body dispersion (hybrid+MBD) are still required.

Relevance to GAtor 2.0

Highly relevant: this is a direct product of the Marom group (Yang, Gharakhanyan, Marom co-authors) and uses Genarris 3, the same structure generator paired with GAtor. It demonstrates a fully MLIP-driven ranking alternative to dispersion-inclusive DFT and PBE0+MBD, showing UMA reproduces PBE-D3 lattice energies (MAE ~0.9 kJ/mol) and phonon spectra, which could replace or accelerate the fitness evaluation in GAtor 2.0. The OMC->OMol conformer correction offers a concrete, cheap way to handle conformational polymorphism and GGA delocalization error that GA-based CSP of flexible molecules must contend with. It also provides an updated benchmark set (74 polymorphs, blind-test targets) and cost analysis directly comparable to GA+DFT workflows. Caveats: it uses random structure generation rather than GA operators/niching, and is limited to Z'<=1 single-component crystals.
