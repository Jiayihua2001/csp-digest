Genarris 2.0: A random structure generator for molecular crystals

Rithwik Tom, Timothy Rose, Imanuel Bier, Harriet O’Brien, Álvaro Vázquez‐Mayagoitia, Noa Marom · Computer Physics Communications (2020) · 39 citations · doi:10.1016/j.cpc.2020.107170 our group confidence: high ⚠ verification flagged claims
> Genarris 2.0 is a new version of the open-source Python package for random molecular crystal structure generation of semi-rigid homomolecular crystals over all 230 space groups, on both general and special Wyckoff positions. Major improvements include an MPI-based (mpi4py) parallelization scheme with user-defined sequential workflows, a machine-learned unit cell volume estimation model trained on CSD experimental structures using a topological molecular fragment fingerprint, a new algorithm for generating structures with molecules on special Wyckoff positions, a hierarchical three-stage structure check for detecting unphysical close contacts (including rigorous treatment of non-orthogonal cells via a sphere decoder), and new intermolecular cutoff distance settings for strong hydrogen bonds. The features are demonstrated on benzene (symmetric molecule occupying special positions) and glycine (strong hydrogen bonds) using a new 'Robust' clustering/down-selection workflow interfacing with FHI-aims. Contribution

Delivers a faster, more portable, feature-rich random structure generator for seeding CSP algorithms (e.g. GAtor genetic algorithm) and building ML datasets, notably adding special Wyckoff position generation, an ML-based solid-form volume predictor within polymorph-density-difference accuracy, hierarchical structure checks, and hydrogen-bond-aware cutoff distances.

Method

Python 3 package (structure generation core Pygenarris in C), parallelized with mpi4py; interfaces with FHI-aims for energy evaluations and geometry relaxations. Volume estimation: Monte Carlo vdW-sphere volume plus a topological fragment fingerprint (atomic-neighborhood strings), with a Bayesian ridge regression linear model (scikit-learn), regularized via grid search and five-fold cross validation. Structure generation samples space groups compatible with molecular symmetry, draws volumes from a Gaussian, and aligns molecular symmetry axes to crystal viewing directions for special positions. Three-stage hierarchical structure checks: (I) non-PBC screening, (II) approximate minimum-image with lattice reduction, (III) sphere-decoder (Finck-Pohst) exact closest-vector solution for non-orthogonal cells. Down-selection via 'Robust' workflow: RCD or RDF descriptors, affinity propagation clustering with parallel binary search on preference, PBE+TS single-point energies then relaxations. DFT: PBE + Tkatchenko-Scheffler dispersion, light/tier1 settings; SPE 1x1x1 k-grid, relaxations 3x3x3, unconstrained lattice; benzene Z=2 high-pressure polymorph at 25 kbar.

Key results

- Polymorph dataset: 3,768 CSD entries and 2,173 unique homomolecular polymorph pairs; distribution of percent polymorph density (=solid-form volume) differences has standard deviation 2.95%, taken as a lower bound on achievable volume accuracy. - Monte Carlo volume systematically underestimates true solid-form volume; ratio of experimental to MC volume = 1.47; linear MC-only model gives 4.72% standard-deviation error. - Topological fragment model (64 unique fragments retained, threshold 30 occurrences) reduces error to 2.93% standard deviation, comparable to polymorph density differences. - Benzene raw pools ~6000 structures each; predicted volumes 243 A^3 (Z=2) and 487 A^3 (Z=4) with std 18 and 37 A^3; experimental volumes 206 A^3 (Z=2, high pressure) and 471 A^3 (Z=4); Z=4 prediction closer since model trained at ambient pressure. - New hydrogen-bond cutoff distances (H...Y): OH...O 1.5 A (sr 0.55), OH...N 1.6 A (sr 0.58), NH...O 1.6 A (sr 0.59), NH...N 1.7 A (sr 0.62); default sr=0.85 retained for vdW and for halogen or C-donor H-bonds. - Glycine predicted volume 79 A^3 per molecule, close to experiment for all polymorphs; ~5000 structures per Z; mean/std volumes (159,238,318) A^3 and (12,18,24) A^3 for Z=(2,3,4). - Final relaxed pools contained experimental structures for both benzene polymorphs (Z=2 in both RCD and RDF; Z=4 only in RDF run), beta-glycine (Z=2, both runs) and gamma-glycine (Z=3, both runs); alpha-glycine not found but similar space group/lattice/packing structures were present. - Space group distributions in raw pools were approximately uniform; mirror-plane space groups (e.g. P2/m (10), Pmm2 (25), Pmmm (47)) are hard to generate, while glide/screw-axis groups are easier.

Limitations

- Restricted to semi-rigid molecules with no bond rotational (rotatable) degrees of freedom, and homomolecular crystals only (no Z'>1 flexibility or multi-component systems). - Volume model trained on ambient-pressure CSD structures, giving larger error for high-pressure polymorphs (e.g. benzene Z=2 at 25 kbar). - Robust workflow is designed to seed structure search (e.g. GA), not as a standalone predictor; alpha-glycine experimental structure was not recovered in the final pools. - DFT ranking used PBE+TS at light/tier1 settings, not higher-accuracy dispersion methods (e.g. PBE0+MBD); accuracy of final energies is limited accordingly. - Only two case-study molecules (benzene, glycine) demonstrated; broad chemical generality is shown only for the volume model, not the full CSP workflow.

Takeaways

- Genarris 2.0 is the front-end structure generator that seeds GAtor's initial population; understanding its space-group sampling, volume model, and cutoff settings is directly relevant to GA-CSP setup. - The topological-fragment volume model achieves ~2.93% error, on par with intrinsic polymorph density spread, giving reasonable target unit cell volumes for generation. - Special Wyckoff position generation is essential for symmetric molecules like benzene (inversion center); prior generators relied on serendipitous special-position occupation. - Hydrogen-bond-specific cutoffs (much shorter than 0.85*sum vdW) are necessary to avoid discarding physically valid dense H-bonded crystals such as glycine. - The RCD vs RDF descriptor choice can affect which experimental structures survive down-selection (benzene Z=4 found only with RDF), relevant when choosing descriptors for GA niching/clustering.

Relevance to GAtor 2.0

Directly relevant: Genarris is the standard random-structure generator used to seed GAtor's initial GA population, from the same group. The paper details structure generation across 230 space groups, special Wyckoff positions, the ML volume estimator, hierarchical structure checks, and the RCD/RDF descriptors plus affinity propagation clustering that also inform GA niching and down-selection. The FHI-aims PBE+TS ranking pipeline and hydrogen-bond cutoff handling are practical dependencies for a GAtor 2.0 workflow, though it does not address Z'>1, multi-component crystals, MLIPs, or higher-level dispersion ranking.
