envxgen

· () · 0 citations
> Tool paper presenting a CSP method for the case where part of a unit cell is already known from experiment (heavy atoms from XRD; host molecule of a cocrystal) and only the missing positions need to be predicted. Validated on superconducting hydrides (LaH₁₀, UH₈, H₃S) and CL-20 cocrystals; applied to the experimentally-unsolved Y₂H₉. Authors are at XPANCEO (Dubai), not the established CSP groups.

Takeaways

- Problem framing is the contribution, not the algorithm. EnvXGen targets *partial-information CSP* — where XRD gives you the lattice + heavy-atom (or host-molecule) positions but light atoms / guest molecules remain hidden. The authors package this as a named tool. Conceptually it's the inorganic/organic crystallographic analog of molecular replacement. - Algorithm is engineering, not innovation. Three steps: (1) lay a 3D grid over the unit cell; remove sites that violate min-interatomic-distance constraints with the known atoms; (2) randomly place missing atoms or molecules at valid sites, with multi-stage distance checks (and rotation trials for molecules); (3) relax with VASP+PBE (atomic) or LAMMPS+ReaxFF (molecular), then rank by *both* lattice energy *and* cosine similarity to the input structure's ALIGNN fingerprint. - ALIGNN's role is narrow. Pretrained `mp_e_form_alignn` (formation-energy model from JARVIS) is used for fingerprinting only — penultimate-layer atom-wise embeddings averaged into a fixed-length vector. Cosine similarity drives the selection score; agglomerative clustering + UMAP/PCA are decorative (visualization only). - Validation papers: experimental phases recovered for LaH₁₀ (Fm-3m at 150 GPa, similarity 0.993, ΔH 17 meV/atom over the next phase), UH₈ (Fm-3m at 49 GPa), H₃S (Im-3m at 200 GPa), CL-20·CO₂ (Pbca), CL-20·N₂O (Pbca). All "fixed environment" inputs are partial structures, not raw diffraction. - Y₂H₉ at 120 GPa is the only blind prediction, and it's hedged: P1 lowest by 12–17 meV/atom over 3–4 alternative space groups (Amm2, Pmm2, Cmm2). Within DFT noise; experimental confirmation is left for later work. - Limitations the paper does not discuss: no comparison to GAtor / USPEX with fixed-atom constraints (both support that natively); no foundation-MLIP attempted (MACE-OFF, AIMNet2 would have been the interesting test on the cocrystals); no diffraction-residual signal in the loop (this is *downstream* of XRD, not coupled to it). - Verdict for the wiki: low-priority *tool* paper. Useful to know exists if Jade ever needs the fixed-environment niche, but not a methodological advance to chase.

Summary

The paper introduces EnvXGen, a Python-based CSP code aimed at the case where the lattice parameters and the positions of *some* structural units (heavy atoms or host molecules) are already known. The motivating cases are: 1. Superconducting hydrides at high pressure, where XRD locates La/U/Y/S atoms but H is invisible. 2. Energetic-molecule cocrystals (CL-20 with small guests like CO₂, N₂O), where the rigid host can be located but disordered guests cannot. The workflow lays a 3D grid over the unit cell; for each grid node, the algorithm checks whether placing a new structural element there would violate user-tunable minimum interatomic distances. Valid sites form a candidate pool. Hundreds of candidate full-cell structures are generated by randomly placing the missing elements at sampled grid nodes (with multi-stage distance checks and, for molecules, random rotations). Relaxation runs in VASP (PBE+PAW, atomic crystals) or LAMMPS (ReaxFF, molecular crystals). For ranking, the authors compute crystal fingerprints from the penultimate layer of the pretrained `mp_e_form_alignn` model (JARVIS, trained on Materials Project formation energies), averaged atom-wise into a fixed-length vector. Each candidate is scored by both relaxed energy and cosine similarity to the input "environment" fingerprint. Survivors are clustered (agglomerative, with the cluster count chosen by silhouette score) and visualized in 2D via UMAP or PCA. Verification on LaH₁₀ at 150 GPa, UH₈ at 49 GPa, and H₃S at 200 GPa successfully recovers the experimental cubic phases. CL-20·CO₂ and CL-20·N₂O recover the Pbca cocrystal phases (CCDC 771863 and 1585914 as ground truth). The blind application to Y₂H₉ at 120 GPa identifies P1-Y₂H₉ as the lowest-energy candidate but with three additional space groups (Amm2, Pmm2, Cmm2) within ~17 meV/atom — all proposed as candidates pending experimental confirmation.

Notable quotes

> "EnvXGen takes the crystal structure parameters of a studied material. Also, before generation it takes the types, number, and positions of atoms or molecules that must be placed in the considered cell as a known atomic environment." — Section 2 > "Similarity is calculated as a cosine distance between the vector representation of the initial structure and any newly generated structure. As a vector representation or descriptor, we used embeddings from the ALIGNN graph neural network pretrained on the Materials Project data set." — Section 2.1.3

Implications for the wiki

- Touches concepts: concepts/crystal-structure-prediction (adds the *fixed-environment* sub-problem), concepts/energetic-materials (CL-20 cocrystals as benchmark targets). - New concept page warranted: concepts/fixed-environment-csp — the problem class is real and worth filing as a distinct CSP sub-problem, even if EnvXGen itself is incremental. - Touches entities: none of the existing entity pages need updates. VASP, LAMMPS, ReaxFF, ALIGNN, CL-20 are mentioned in passing but don't yet warrant their own pages until a *second, better* source touches them — promote later if needed. - No author pages for Propad / Kruglov: single-paper signal, XPANCEO is a private outfit, not academic CSP community.

Open questions

- Would GAtor or USPEX with positional constraints have produced the same result with less engineering? Authors don't compare. - Would foundation MLIPs (MACE-OFF, AIMNet2, UMA) have outperformed ReaxFF on the CL-20 cocrystals? - Is there an experimental confirmation of the proposed Y₂H₉ structure (P1 vs. the three alternatives within DFT noise)? - Is the ALIGNN-as-fingerprint trick more accurate than simpler alternatives (heavy-atom RMSD, COMPACK, Steinhardt-parameter cosine) for this task? The paper does not test that.
