Predicting crystal structures of organic compounds

Sarah L. Price · Chemical Society Reviews (2013) · 527 citations · doi:10.1039/c3cs60279f confidence: low ⚠ verification flagged claims
> A review by Sarah Price (Chem Soc Rev, 2013) on the state of organic crystal structure prediction (CSP). It frames CSP as a search for the most thermodynamically stable crystal structure using approximate crystal energy evaluations, where the global minimum provides a candidate experimental structure. The review emphasizes that many other structures may lie very close in energy, and these near-degenerate structures on the crystal energy landscape can correspond to polymorphs, static or dynamic disorder, or thermodynamically feasible packings that are never nucleated. A key motivation stated is CSP as a complement to experimental solid form screening. Corpus: tier C - `price-review-2014` - source mode: metadata - confidence: low - FLAGGED - see below

Limitations

- Digest based on abstract/metadata only; full text not available. - 2013 review predates modern MLIPs (MACE-OFF, AIMNet2, UMA) and much of the recent dispersion-inclusive DFT and blind-test progress. - Focused on thermodynamic (lattice-energy) ranking; kinetics of nucleation and finite-temperature effects noted only qualitatively. - No specific numerical benchmarks or method comparisons captured in the abstract.

Takeaways

- The energy landscape, not just the global minimum, is the meaningful CSP output - relevant to GA niching and retaining diverse low-energy candidates. - Near-degenerate structures motivate careful, accurate ranking (dispersion-inclusive DFT) since polymorphs differ by small energies. - CSP is best used as a complement to experimental solid-form screening, framing the practical value of GAtor-style predictions. - The distinction between thermodynamically feasible and nucleable structures highlights the gap between lattice-energy ranking and observed polymorphism.

Relevance to GAtor 2.0

Foundational conceptual background for GAtor 2.0: it justifies preserving structural diversity across the energy landscape (relevant to GA operators and niching) and stresses accurate energy ranking because polymorphs are near-degenerate (motivating PBE0+MBD). It also frames finite-temperature stability and nucleation as open issues beyond pure lattice-energy minimization. As a 2013 review it gives historical grounding but no modern MLIP or blind-test specifics.
