Predicting crystal form stability under real-world conditions

Dzmitry S. Firaha, Yifei Michelle Liu, Jacco van de Streek, Kiran Sasikumar, H. Dietrich, Julian Helfferich · Nature (2023) · 91 citations · doi:10.1038/s41586-023-06587-3 confidence: low ⚠ verification flagged claims
> This Nature (2023) paper reports advances in computing solid-solid free-energy differences for molecular crystals under realistic conditions of temperature and relative humidity. The authors claim to improve free-energy calculation accuracy, build an experimental benchmark for solid-solid free-energy differences, quantify statistical errors on computed free energies, and place anhydrate and hydrate crystal structures of different stoichiometries on a common energy landscape with defined error bars. The stated goal is to make crystal structure prediction more actionable for crystal form selection in industrial (pharmaceutical) settings. NOTE: digest based on abstract/metadata only. Contribution

Redefining the state of the art in in silico crystal form selection by improving free-energy accuracy, constructing a reliable experimental benchmark for solid-solid free-energy differences, quantifying statistical errors, and unifying hydrate structures of different stoichiometries with anhydrates on a single temperature- and humidity-dependent energy landscape with error bars.

Method

Not described in the abstract beyond references to accurate and affordable free-energy calculations. The abstract mentions constructing an experimental benchmark, quantifying statistical errors, and a method to place different hydrate stoichiometries on the same energy landscape as a function of temperature and relative humidity. Specific computational engine, sampling scheme, and level of theory are not stated in the available material.

Key results

- Calculated free energies reported to have standard errors of 1-2 kJ mol-1 for industrially relevant compounds - Method places anhydrate and hydrate crystal structures of different stoichiometries on the same energy landscape as a function of temperature and relative humidity with defined error bars - Approach stated to be extensible to other multi-component systems including solvates
