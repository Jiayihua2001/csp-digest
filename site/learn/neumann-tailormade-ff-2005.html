Energy Ranking of Molecular Crystals Using Density Functional Theory Calculations and an Empirical van der Waals Correction

Marcus A. Neumann, Marc‐Antoine Perrin · The Journal of Physical Chemistry B (2005) · 295 citations · doi:10.1021/jp050121r confidence: low ⚠ verification flagged claims
> This 2005 paper introduces a hybrid method combining plane-wave DFT (using VASP) with an empirical pairwise van der Waals correction for optimizing and energy-ranking molecular crystal structures. The vdW correction is a sum of atom-atom C6/r^6 pair potentials multiplied by damping functions that ensure crossover from asymptotic dispersion behavior at long range to a finite interaction at short range. C6 coefficients were fit to molecular C6 values from dipole oscillator strength distributions (following Wu and Yang, using data from Meath and co-workers), with parameters for H, C, N, O, F, Cl, and S. Damping functions were calibrated to reproduce experimental low-temperature unit cells, achieving about 1% average deviation in cell lengths. The method was tested on candidate polymorphs of six small molecules generated with Accelrys Polymorph Predictor. Contribution

An early, carefully parametrized dispersion-corrected DFT (DFT+vdW) scheme specifically designed and validated for molecular crystal structure optimization and lattice-energy ranking, emphasizing the importance of damping-function parametrization to balance the DFT and empirical dispersion contributions.

Method

DFT calculations performed in VASP; empirical van der Waals correction added as a sum over atom-atom pair potentials, each an asymptotic C(6,A,B)/r^6 term times a damping function d(A,B)(r). C6 coefficients determined by least-squares fitting to molecular C6 coefficients (Meath dipole oscillator strength distributions, method of Wu and Yang). Damping functions parametrized to best match experimental low-temperature crystal unit cells after lattice-energy optimization. Validation: candidate polymorphs of ethane, ethylene, acetylene, methanol, acetic acid, and urea generated with Accelrys Polymorph Predictor, then optimized/ranked with the hybrid method.

Key results

- Average deviation between experimental and calculated unit cell lengths is 1%. - In 5 out of 6 test cases the experimentally observed low-temperature structure is the most stable calculated structure. - Empirical vdW parameters provided for elements H, C, N, O, F, Cl, and S.

Limitations

- Digest based on abstract/metadata only; full-text details not available. - Only six small-molecule test systems used for ranking validation. - One of six cases failed to rank the experimental structure as most stable (specific case not stated in abstract). - Empirical, element-limited parametrization (7 elements); transferability beyond the fitted set not established from the abstract. - Pairwise C6/r^6 dispersion model lacks many-body/beyond-pairwise effects (predates MBD-type corrections).

Takeaways

- This is a foundational reference for the Neumann DFT+vdW (DFT-d) lattice-energy ranking approach later prominent in CSP blind tests. - Correct balancing of the empirical dispersion damping against the DFT energy is highlighted as crucial for ranking accuracy - relevant when comparing against modern PBE0+MBD. - Serves as a historical baseline for how dispersion corrections improved molecular crystal energy ranking over plain DFT.

Relevance to GAtor 2.0

Directly relevant as a precursor to dispersion-inclusive DFT lattice-energy ranking used in GAtor-style CSP workflows. The Neumann method (this paper and its successors) became a benchmark in CSP blind tests and motivates why GAtor uses dispersion-inclusive functionals such as PBE0+MBD for final ranking. Useful context for descriptor-independent, energy-based ranking of GA-generated polymorphs.
