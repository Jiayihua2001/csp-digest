Accurate and Efficient Method for Many-Body van der Waals Interactions

Alexandre Tkatchenko, Robert A. DiStasio, Roberto Car, Matthias Scheffler · Physical Review Letters (2012) · 1548 citations · doi:10.1103/physrevlett.108.236402 confidence: low ⚠ verification flagged claims
> This PRL introduces the many-body dispersion (MBD) method for van der Waals interactions, combining the Tkatchenko-Scheffler (TS) vdW method with a self-consistent screening (SCS) equation from classical electrodynamics to describe the frequency-dependent polarizability of finite-gap molecules and solids. The screened long-range many-body vdW energy is obtained by solving the Schrodinger equation for a system of coupled quantum harmonic oscillators. The abstract reports that screening and many-body effects matter even for small molecules and are crucial for conformational energies of biomolecules and binding of molecular crystals, at negligible computational cost relative to the underlying electronic structure calculation. Contribution

Establishes the MBD (many-body dispersion) approach that later became the standard dispersion-inclusive correction (e.g. PBE0+MBD) used for accurate lattice-energy ranking of molecular crystals, by unifying atom-resolved TS polarizabilities with self-consistent screening and a coupled-oscillator many-body treatment.

Method

Combine the TS vdW method (Phys. Rev. Lett. 102, 073005) with the self-consistent screening equation of classical electrodynamics to get seamless polarization/depolarization of the polarizability tensor. Model the system as coupled quantum harmonic oscillators and solve the Schrodinger equation to obtain the screened long-range many-body vdW energy. Takeaways

Relevance to GAtor 2.0

Directly relevant: MBD (and its successor PBE0+MBD) is a core dispersion-inclusive energy method for accurate lattice-energy ranking of molecular crystals in CSP, exactly the ranking stage in GAtor 2.0. Understanding MBD's coupled-oscillator screening formulation informs why it outperforms pairwise corrections for polarizable molecular crystals.
