Efficient Molecular Crystal Structure Prediction and Stability Assessment with AIMNet2 Neural Network Potentials

Kamal Singh Nayal, Dana O’Connor, R.I. Zubatyuk, Dylan M. Anstine, Yi Yang, Rithwik Tom · Crystal Growth & Design (2025) · 6 citations · doi:10.1021/acs.cgd.5c01001 our group confidence: low ⚠ verification flagged claims
> This paper describes a crystal structure prediction (CSP) methodology used in the seventh CCDC blind test that accelerates lattice-energy ranking by training target-specific AIMNet2 machine-learned interatomic potentials (MLIPs). The key idea is that AIMNet2 potentials trained on gas-phase, dispersion-corrected DFT reference data of molecular clusters (n-mers) extend successfully to crystalline environments, reproducing the CSP energy landscape and ranking candidate structures by relative stability without requiring expensive periodic DFT. The approach is demonstrated across pharmaceutical, optoelectronic, and agrochemical target systems. NOTE: digest is from abstract/metadata only. Contribution

A target-specific MLIP training strategy in which AIMNet2 potentials are fit to dispersion-corrected DFT energies of molecular clusters (n-mers) and shown to transfer to periodic crystal environments, enabling efficient CSP lattice-energy ranking without periodic self-consistent DFT. Applied and analyzed in the context of the CCDC seventh blind test.

Method

Train target-specific AIMNet2 neural network potentials on DFT calculations of molecular clusters (n-mers) computed in the gas phase with dispersion corrections. Use these MLIPs to evaluate energies of candidate crystal structures and rank them by relative (lattice) stability within a CSP workflow. Evaluated on the CCDC seventh blind test target systems. Specific structure-generation, sampling, and DFT functional/dispersion details are not available from the abstract.

Key results

- No quantitative results (energy errors, ranking accuracy, RMSDs, speedups, or per-target statistics) are reported in the available abstract/metadata. - Qualitative claim: potentials trained only on gas-phase dispersion-corrected n-mer DFT data reportedly extend to crystalline environments and correctly rank structures by relative stability. - Qualitative claim: the method is demonstrated across pharmaceutical, optoelectronic, and agrochemical systems from the seventh blind test.

Limitations

- Full text not available; all specifics (accuracy numbers, cost/speedup, systems, DFT reference level) are unknown from the abstract. - Approach is target-specific, requiring retraining of an MLIP per system, which may limit general transferability. - Training uses gas-phase cluster (n-mer) data; whether many-body and long-range crystalline interactions are fully captured is asserted but not quantified in the abstract. - Finite-temperature stability assessment is mentioned in the title but the extent covered in the abstract is unclear.

Takeaways

- n-mer / molecular cluster training is a route to build MLIPs that transfer to periodic molecular crystals, potentially cheaper than periodic DFT training sets - relevant to accelerating GAtor lattice-energy ranking. - AIMNet2 (relevant MLIP the group tracks) was used competitively in the CCDC seventh blind test with a target-specific training protocol. - Read the full paper to extract the DFT reference level (dispersion scheme), n-mer construction, ranking accuracy vs DFT, and any finite-temperature results before citing numbers.
