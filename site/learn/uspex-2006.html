Crystal structure prediction using ab initio evolutionary techniques: Principles and applications

Artem R. Oganov, Colin W. Glass · The Journal of Chemical Physics (2006) · 2546 citations · doi:10.1063/1.2210932 confidence: low ⚠ verification flagged claims
> This 2006 paper introduces USPEX, an evolutionary-algorithm approach to crystal structure prediction that couples a specifically devised evolutionary algorithm with ab initio total-energy calculations. The method predicts the most stable and low-energy metastable structures for a given compound at arbitrary P-T conditions without experimental input, and reports a very high success rate across tens of test cases spanning ionic, covalent, metallic, and molecular systems with up to 40 atoms per cell. Contribution

Establishes an evolutionary-algorithm framework (later known as USPEX) for ab initio crystal structure prediction, demonstrating near-100 percent success across diverse bonding types and applying it to resolve high-pressure crystallography problems and discover new phases.

Method

Merges a purpose-built evolutionary algorithm with first-principles total-energy calculations. Structures are evolved via genetic operators and ranked by ab initio energy; exact operators, generation, and selection details are not available from the abstract alone.

Key results

- Reported nearly 100 percent success rate across a few tens of tests - Handled ionic, covalent, metallic, and molecular structures with up to 40 atoms in the unit cell - Predicted stable phases including epsilon-oxygen and a new phase of sulphur - Predicted new metastable phases of carbon, sulphur, and nitrogen - Reported stable and metastable phases of CaCO3

Limitations

- Digest based on abstract/metadata only; algorithmic details not verified - System size limited to about 40 atoms per unit cell in these tests - Molecular crystals mentioned only generically; no molecular-CSP-specific benchmarks quoted in the abstract - Quantitative success metrics beyond 'nearly 100 percent' not available from abstract

Takeaways

- Foundational evolutionary-algorithm CSP paper (USPEX) relevant as prior art and comparison baseline for GA-based CSP - Demonstrates that GA plus ab initio energy ranking generalizes across bonding types, supporting the GAtor design philosophy - High-pressure phase discovery shows the value of finding low-energy metastable structures, not just the global minimum

Relevance to GAtor 2.0

Directly relevant as a seminal genetic/evolutionary-algorithm CSP method predating and paralleling GAtor. Useful for situating GAtor 2.0's operators, selection, and energy-ranking choices against the USPEX lineage, though this paper targets general inorganic/high-pressure crystals rather than molecular crystals specifically.
