Modelling organic crystal structures using distributed multipole and polarizability-based model intermolecular potentials

Sarah L. Price, Maurice Leslie, Gareth W. A. Welch, Matthew Habgood, Louise S. Price, P.G. Karamertzanis · Physical Chemistry Chemical Physics (2010) · 327 citations · doi:10.1039/c004164e confidence: low ⚠ verification flagged claims
> This paper describes DMACRYS, a crystal lattice simulation program designed for organic crystal structure prediction (CSP), particularly for pharmaceutical molecules. The program uses anisotropic atom-atom model intermolecular potentials grounded in the theory of intermolecular forces, including distributed multipole electrostatics, anisotropic repulsion, damped dispersion, and a newly implemented induction (polarizability-based) energy contribution. It can optimize lattice energies, compute second-derivative properties (elastic tensor, terahertz k=0 rigid-body lattice modes for free-energy estimation), and reduce spacegroup symmetry to escape transition states. The authors demonstrate the method on the four polymorphs of carbamazepine, showing that while reproducing structures is relatively easy, reaching the few kJ/mol accuracy needed for polymorph energy ranking is very demanding. Contribution

Presents the DMACRYS lattice simulation program and, as a new feature, an accurate induction-energy model using atomic anisotropic dipole polarizabilities to capture crystal-field-induced changes in molecular charge density, enabling more accurate anisotropic atom-atom lattice energy calculations for CSP.

Method

Anisotropic atom-atom model intermolecular potentials based on intermolecular force theory: distributed multipole electrostatic model (multipoles Q up to rank 4, t=00...44s), anisotropic atom-atom repulsion, damped isotropic dispersion up to R^-10, empirically fitted isotropic exp-6 atom-atom models, and a new induction term via atomic anisotropic dipole polarizability models. Lattice energy optimization, second-derivative properties (elastic tensor, terahertz rigid-body lattice modes for free energies), and symmetry reduction to move off transition states. Validated on carbamazepine (C15H12N2O) polymorphs.

Key results

- Demonstrated on the four polymorphs of carbamazepine (C15H12N2O) - Reproducing crystal structures is described as relatively easy, but computing polymorphic energy differences to the required accuracy of a few kJ/mol is very demanding of the modelling assumptions - No specific numerical energy or RMSD values are provided in the abstract

Limitations

- Achieving the few kJ/mol accuracy needed for pharmaceutical applications is highly sensitive to modelling assumptions - Digest based on abstract only - full quantitative results, computational cost, and comparisons not available - Approach relies on rigid-body treatment for lattice modes and free energies - Method predates and does not use dispersion-inclusive DFT (e.g. PBE0+MBD) or MLIPs

Takeaways

- DMACRYS provides an anisotropic atom-atom force-field alternative for the lattice-energy ranking stage of CSP, relevant as a classical baseline versus modern DFT/MLIP ranking in GAtor 2.0 - The induction/polarizability contribution is highlighted as important for accurate lattice energies - Terahertz k=0 lattice modes and elastic tensor enable free-energy estimates, relevant to finite-temperature stability work - Carbamazepine polymorphs serve as a benchmark showing energy ranking to few kJ/mol is the hard part, not structure reproduction

Relevance to GAtor 2.0

Relevant as a historical/classical lattice-energy ranking tool for organic CSP that GAtor's pipeline could contrast against. The emphasis that structure generation/reproduction is easy while few-kJ/mol energy ranking is the bottleneck directly motivates high-accuracy ranking (PBE0+MBD, MLIPs) in GAtor 2.0. The rigid-body terahertz lattice modes for free-energy estimation connect to finite-temperature stability interests. Distributed multipoles and polarizability models are descriptor/potential ingredients of possible interest.
