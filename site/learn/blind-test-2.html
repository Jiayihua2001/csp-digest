Crystal structure prediction of small organic molecules: a second blind test

W.D.S. Motherwell, Herman L. Ammon, Jack D. Dunitz, Alexander Dzyabchenko, Peter Erk, A. Gavezzotti · Acta Crystallographica Section B Structural Science (2002) · 398 citations · doi:10.1107/s0108768102005669 confidence: low ⚠ verification flagged claims
> This paper reports the second blind test of organic crystal structure prediction (CSP2001), organized at the Cambridge Crystallographic Data Centre following the first (CSP1999) workshop. Seventeen participants were given only the 2D chemical diagram of three organic molecules and asked to predict crystal structures within a set of named common space groups, using various programs that build theoretical crystal structures and rank them primarily by minimum calculated lattice energy. Each participant could submit up to three predictions per molecule. Corpus: tier A - `blind-test-2` - source mode: metadata - confidence: low - FLAGGED - see below

Contribution

Provides an objective, community-wide benchmark of the state of CSP methodology circa 2001, documenting successes and failures across multiple prediction programs and highlighting persistent challenges such as molecular flexibility and energy ranking.

Method

Blind prediction exercise: participants generated candidate crystal structures within given space groups from molecular models and ranked candidates by calculated lattice energy; up to three submissions per molecule. A secondary test explored the use of non-indexed powder diffraction data after ab initio submissions were completed.

Key results

- Two correct predictions were obtained for the first molecule - Four correct predictions were obtained for the second molecule - No correct predictions were obtained for the third molecule, which had torsional flexibility - The correct structure was often present in participants' sorted low-energy lists but at a ranking position greater than three

Limitations

- No single method was found to be completely reliable - Torsionally flexible molecules (molecule 3) were not predicted correctly by any participant - Lattice-energy ranking frequently failed to place the correct structure within the top three even when it was generated - Digest based only on the abstract/metadata; full text not available, so details on programs, molecules, and methods are unknown

Takeaways

- As early as 2001, structure generation could often capture the experimental structure, but energy ranking was the bottleneck - the correct answer frequently ranked worse than third - Molecular flexibility (conformational/torsional degrees of freedom) was a major unsolved challenge, motivating later focus on flexible-molecule CSP - Blind tests provide an objective yardstick for comparing CSP methodologies, a tradition GAtor and later methods are benchmarked against

Relevance to GAtor 2.0

This is the second in the series of CCDC CSP blind tests that frame the entire field GAtor operates in. It documents the enduring core problems the group cares about: reliable low-energy structure generation (relevant to Genarris) and accurate lattice-energy ranking (relevant to dispersion-inclusive DFT and MLIPs). The ranking failures and the flexible-molecule difficulty foreshadow motivations for GA search, better energy methods, and Z'>1 / flexible-molecule handling.
