Genarris 3.0: Generating Close-Packed Molecular Crystal Structures with Rigid Press

Yi Yang, Rithwik Tom, Jose A. G. L. Wui, Jonathan E. Moussa, Noa Marom · Journal of Chemical Theory and Computation (2025) · 7 citations · doi:10.1021/acs.jctc.5c01080 our group
> 2025 J. Chem. Theory Comput. paper from [[entities/noa-marom|Marom]]'s group introducing [[entities/genarris|Genarris 3.0]], an open-source Python tool for molecular [[concepts/crystal-structure-prediction|CSP]]. Two main contributions: the [[concepts/rigid-press-algorithm|Rigid Press]] algorithm (regularized hard-sphere objective compresses random unit cells to close-packing without energy evaluations), and a clustering / down-selection workflow that uses [[entities/mace-off|MACE-OFF23(L)]] [[concepts/machine-learned-interatomic-potentials|MLIPs]] in early stages with [[entities/fhi-aims|FHI-aims]] DFT for final ranking. Validated on six diverse targets — aspirin, two CCDC blind-test targets, and three energetic materials (HMX, CL-20, DNI) — recovering the experimental polymorph as global or near-global minimum in all cases. Worth flagging: MACE-OFF23(L) is shown to be unreliable for chemistries underrepresented in its SPICE training set, especially nitrogen-rich energetics.

Takeaways

- Rigid Press algorithm: a regularized hard-sphere potential that compresses an inflated, randomly-generated unit cell to maximally close-packed geometry, without any energy evaluation. ~2 orders of magnitude cheaper than MLIP relaxation and a strict prerequisite for some targets — for ε-CL-20, the experimental structure is recovered *only* with Rigid Press in the workflow ([[sources/2026-05-02_genarris-3|Yang et al. 2025]]). - Clustering and down-selection workflow: alternating affinity-propagation clustering and energy-window selection at successively more accurate levels (MACE-OFF23(L) SPE → MACE-OFF23(L) relax → PBE+MBD SPE → PBE+MBD relax). Crucially, the workflow keeps up to 5 structures per cluster within a 10 kJ/mol window rather than just the most stable one — this is what rescues experimental structures that the MLIP misranks ([[sources/2026-05-02_genarris-3|Yang et al. 2025]]). - Six benchmark targets: aspirin (Forms I & II), Target I from the 1st CCDC blind test, Target XXII from the 6th, and three [[concepts/energetic-materials|energetic materials]] — δ-HMX, ε-CL-20, DNI. Experimental structure is the PBE+MBD global minimum for five of six (Target XXII ranks #2; previously reported global only with PBE0+MBD). - MACE-OFF23(L) reliability is chemistry-dependent: ~87–91% relaxation match rate on aspirin / Target I (drug-like, in-distribution), drops to 57–74% for the energetic materials (out-of-distribution). Energy-ranking errors balloon from <5 kJ/mol (aspirin, Target I) to >20 kJ/mol MAE/RMSE for δ-HMX. Experimental structure ranking falls to #19 for DNI under MACE-OFF23(L). The clustering workflow mitigates this but does not fix it ([[sources/2026-05-02_genarris-3|Yang et al. 2025]]). - System-specific MLIPs as fallback: when general-purpose MACE-OFF fails for a specific compound, the authors recommend training a system-specific [[entities/aimnet2|AIMNet2]] potential. Demonstrated for CL-20 — improved both relaxation match rate and ranking (experimental structure → #4 at +2.50 kJ/mol vs. #8 with MACE-OFF23(L)). - Tool integration: Genarris 3.0 talks to relaxation/energy backends via [[entities/ase|ASE]], so any MLIP with a Python calculator interface plugs in. Demonstrated with MACE-OFF, AIMNet2, and Meta's UMA model. Modular Python (abc-based), supports MLIP GPU acceleration, checkpoint/restart. - Direct lineage to GAtor: this paper is citation #1 of [[sources/2026-05-02_gator|GAtor (2018)]]'s cited-by list — same group, same CSP toolkit. Genarris generates, GAtor evolves. The 7th CCDC blind test team (Group 16) used a preliminary Rigid Press inside Genarris with system-specific AIMNet2 — claimed by the authors as the earliest use of MLIPs for molecular CSP (2020–2021), with a 67% target-success rate (highest among academic teams, third overall). - Headline caution from the discussion: "Although general-purpose MLIPs … can considerably accelerate early stage CSP workflows, dispersion-inclusive DFT remains indispensable for accurate final ranking." Validate per-compound before trusting an out-of-the-box MLIP for CSP.

Summary

The paper's thesis is that CSP at scale needs a hierarchy of methods of increasing cost and accuracy, and that achieving close-packing efficiently at the bottom of that hierarchy is the limiting bottleneck. Genarris 3.0 makes two contributions to that hierarchy: a cheap geometric step (Rigid Press) at the bottom, and a clustering/down-selection workflow that preserves diversity through the levels. Background. Molecular crystals exhibit [[concepts/polymorphism|polymorphism]] driven by weak van-der-Waals interactions, with energy differences of only a few kJ/mol between polymorphs. Reliable CSP therefore requires both (a) generating millions of putative structures across all symmetry-compatible space groups, and (b) ranking them at near-DFT accuracy. The CCDC's [[concepts/csp-blind-tests|blind tests]] (1st through 7th) have benchmarked progress and exposed three persistent challenges: finite-temperature stability, the "overprediction" problem where distinct 0 K minima collapse into the same finite-temperature structure, and crystallographic disorder. Out of scope here, but framing the cost trade-off [[sources/2026-05-02_genarris-3|Yang et al. 2025]]. Generation phase. Genarris generates structures at random across all space groups compatible with the requested *Z* (number of molecules per unit cell) and the molecular point-group symmetry, including molecules occupying special Wyckoff positions. The unit-cell volume is sampled around a target inflated by 1.5× to make initial molecule placement easy; molecules are placed by symmetry; intermolecular distances are checked against a fraction of summed van-der-Waals radii (default *s_r* = 0.95 at this stage). Genarris 3.0 currently generates only *Z′* = 1 (one molecule in the asymmetric unit) — flexible molecules are handled by running with an ensemble of conformers ([[sources/2026-05-02_genarris-3|Yang et al. 2025]]). Rigid Press. The new bottom-of-stack algorithm. Each generated structure is compressed using a regularized hard-sphere potential — concretely, an objective `F(s) = V(s) + P_contact(s)` minimized over a state vector `s` of (lattice vectors, asymmetric-unit COG position, asymmetric-unit Euler angles). The pairwise contact penalty is piecewise-defined to be smooth and differentiable: infinity below the hard-sphere diameter `σ_ij = s_r × (r_i^vdW + r_j^vdW)` (default `s_r = 0.85`), a `w·(D−d)/(d−σ)` term in the soft-contact range, zero beyond cutoff `D`. The penalty is normalized by `1/N_atoms²` so molecule size doesn't dominate. Optimization is BFGS via SciPy; space-group symmetry is enforced by reconstructing the full crystal from the optimized asymmetric unit each step. Convergence: gradient-norm tolerance 0.01 or 5000 iterations. A faster C implementation without symmetry constraints is also available, interfaced via SWIG ([[sources/2026-05-02_genarris-3|Yang et al. 2025]]). The trajectories show rapid initial volume reduction (compaction from the inflated initial volume) and a tight final pack. Reported cost: up to 2 orders of magnitude cheaper than MLIP relaxation, and starting MLIP relaxation from a Rigid-Press-preoptimized structure cuts MLIP relaxation steps significantly. The benefit is concentrated where it matters most — for very dense systems like the energetic materials, Rigid Press is the difference between finding the experimental structure and not finding it (demonstrated explicitly for ε-CL-20 in the SI). Down-selection workflow. Stages are: (1) generate ~10⁵ structures per target, (2) Rigid Press, (3) duplicate removal via Pymatgen's `StructureMatcher` (loose tolerances: 0.5 fractional length, 0.5 site, 10° angle), (4) MACE-OFF23(L) SPE + AP clustering to ~10% of pool with lowest-energy-per-cluster, (5) MACE-OFF23(L) full relax + dedup, (6) AP cluster to 100 clusters keeping up to 5 per cluster within a 10 kJ/mol window, (7) PBE+MBD SPE + AP cluster keeping all within 10 kJ/mol per cluster, (8) full PBE+MBD unit-cell relax + final dedup. The "keep multiple per cluster" rule at step 6 is the workflow's load-bearing feature: it preserves the experimental DNI structure even though MACE-OFF23(L) misranks it as #79, +19.3 kJ/mol above the MACE-OFF minimum ([[sources/2026-05-02_genarris-3|Yang et al. 2025]]). Computational details. All DFT in [[entities/fhi-aims|FHI-aims]] v240507, single-molecule geometries relaxed with PBE0+MBD, crystal SPEs with PBE+MBD (Tier 1, *light* settings), final unit-cell relaxations with PBE+MBD (Tier 2, *tight*), 3×3×3 *k*-grid. MLIP relaxations and DFT relaxations both use BFGS in [[entities/ase|ASE]] with 0.01 eV/Å force convergence, ASE `FrechetCellFilter` for cell+atoms, and `FixSymmetry` to preserve space group. Structure similarity assessed with COMPACK molecular-overlay (CSD Python API) using cluster size 30 (RMSD₃₀), 35% distance / 35° angle tolerances, hydrogens excluded — same metric as the 7th CCDC blind test ([[sources/2026-05-02_genarris-3|Yang et al. 2025]]). Results. All six experimental structures are recovered. PBE+MBD ranks the experimental structure as global minimum for aspirin, Target I, δ-HMX, ε-CL-20, and DNI; for Target XXII it ranks #2 (consistent with prior literature that shows PBE0+MBD is needed for #1 there). For aspirin, PBE+MBD predicts Form II below Form I by 0.69 kJ/mol; experiments and prior dispersion-corrected DFT show the two are essentially degenerate, and quasi-harmonic-approximation free energy at 300 K (PBE+MBD+QHA) flips the ordering to Form I below Form II by 1.10 kJ/mol — a clean demonstration that 0 K lattice energy alone is not enough for finely-balanced polymorphs. MACE-OFF23(L) failure modes. The detailed MACE-OFF analysis is the paper's most actionable warning. Relaxation match rate to PBE+MBD-relaxed structures: aspirin 86.6%, Target I 91.0%, Target XXII 80.2%, ε-CL-20 73.7%, δ-HMX 61.8%, DNI 56.9%. Energy ranking MAE/RMSE: <5 kJ/mol for aspirin and Target I (Kendall > 0.7), 9–13 kJ/mol for Target XXII / ε-CL-20 / DNI (Kendall 0.5–0.6), >20 kJ/mol for δ-HMX (Kendall < 0.4). Experimental structure ranks under MACE-OFF23(L) alone: aspirin #1, Target I #1, Target XXII #2, ε-CL-20 #8, δ-HMX #12, DNI #19. The cause is training-set transferability: SPICE 1.0 is mostly drug-like organics, so nitrogen-rich energetics and unusual heterocycles (Target XXII has C/S/N motifs) are out-of-distribution. The fallback recommended by the authors: train a system-specific [[entities/aimnet2|AIMNet2]] potential, demonstrated for CL-20 ([[sources/2026-05-02_genarris-3|Yang et al. 2025]]). Conclusion / framing. Genarris 3.0 is positioned as a versatile, open-source CSP generator that's plug-and-play with whatever relaxation backend you trust for your system. The headline guidance: general-purpose MLIPs accelerate early-stage CSP, but dispersion-inclusive DFT remains indispensable for final ranking, and out-of-the-box MLIP performance for CSP must be validated per-compound. Future work flagged: extending generation to *Z′* > 1 ([[sources/2026-05-02_genarris-3|Yang et al. 2025]]).

Open questions

To deepen entities/genarris, either ingest the 7th-blind-test paper (Tom et al. cited as ref 89) or the Genarris 2.0 paper (refs 87, 88). - Aspirin Form I/II ordering depends on QHA free-energy correction, not 0 K lattice energy. This is a more general lesson about polymorph ranking — worth a dedicated [[concepts/polymorph-ranking|polymorph ranking]] page if more sources discuss it. - Universal models for atoms (UMA) MLIPs are mentioned as another supported backend (ref 116). UMA is a Meta/FAIR-style universal MLIP — worth a future ingest if Jade cares about the MLIP-for-CSP frontier. - The 7th CCDC blind test result paper (ref 89) is cited as containing system-specific AIMNet2 details and the team's full result set. That is a logical next-source candidate.
