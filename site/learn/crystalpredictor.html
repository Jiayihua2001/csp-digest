Ab initio crystal structure prediction—I. Rigid molecules

P.G. Karamertzanis, Constantinos C. Pantelides · Journal of Computational Chemistry (2004) · 140 citations · doi:10.1002/jcc.20165 confidence: low ⚠ verification flagged claims
> This 2004 paper presents an ab initio crystal structure prediction methodology for rigid molecules that requires only the molecular atomic connectivity as input. Crystal structures are predicted by globally minimizing the lattice enthalpy. Electrostatics are modeled via a set of distributed charges that are automatically selected and positioned to reproduce quantum mechanical results. A four-step, parallelized global optimization algorithm searches the lattice enthalpy surface, enabling exploration of less common space groups and structures with Z' > 1. The method was applied to four molecular systems, with the experimental structure appearing among the most stable predicted structures in all cases (though not always as the global minimum). Contribution

A rigid-molecule CSP methodology combining automatically fitted distributed-charge electrostatics with a parallelized four-step global lattice enthalpy minimization that can access low-frequency space groups and Z' > 1 crystals.

Method

Global minimization of lattice enthalpy using a four-step global optimization algorithm with a parallelized implementation. Molecules treated as rigid. Electrostatic interactions represented by distributed charges optimally and automatically selected/positioned from quantum mechanical calculations. Input limited to atomic connectivity.

Key results

- Experimentally known structure found among the most stable predicted structures for all four test systems, but not necessarily the global minimum - 3-aza-bicyclo(3.3.1)nonane-2,4-dione predicted in P2(1)/a, Z' = 1 - Allopurinol predicted in P2(1)/c, Z' = 1 - 1,3,4,6,7,9-hexa-azacycl(3.3.3)azine predicted in Pbca, Z' = 2 - Triethylenediamine predicted in P6(3)/m, Z' = 1

Limitations

- Molecules treated as rigid, so conformational flexibility is not handled (this is Part I of a series) - Experimental structure is not always the global minimum, indicating force field / ranking accuracy limits - Details of the four-step algorithm, energy model, and success metrics unavailable from abstract only - No dispersion-inclusive DFT ranking; relies on distributed-charge classical model as described

Takeaways

- Early demonstration that connectivity-only CSP can reach Z' > 1 and less common space groups via parallelized global search - Automatic quantum-derived distributed-charge fitting is used to capture electrostatics accurately - The recurring finding that experimental structures rank near but not at the global minimum motivates later high-accuracy re-ranking approaches (e.g. dispersion-inclusive DFT, MBD)

Relevance to GAtor 2.0

Relevant as foundational CSP prior art: it targets lattice-energy (enthalpy) global minimization, Z' > 1 crystals, and space-group sampling, all central to GAtor. The observation that the experimental structure is stable but not the global minimum underscores the importance of accurate lattice-energy ranking (PBE0+MBD) that GAtor and Genarris pursue. It is a non-GA global optimization method, offering a contrast to genetic-algorithm search operators and niching.
