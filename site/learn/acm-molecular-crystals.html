Takeaways

- Methodological advance, not a tool paper. This extends a quantum-chemistry framework (ACM/MPAC, lineage Levy / Liu / Pernal / Gori-Giorgi / Fabiano) from *molecules* to *periodic solids*. A real first. - HFAC24 is the headline. Parameter-free post-HF correlation expression. Recovers two limits exactly: the uniform electron gas limit *and* the strong-interaction limit (the latter is what makes ACMs distinct from standard DFT functionals). The only one of the tested ACMs accurate on *both* lattice/binding energies and total energies — most fitted methods sacrifice one for the other. - Benchmark scope: 19 representative molecular crystals. Compared against periodic MP2, high-level reference data, and experimental lattice energies. Achieves accuracy *comparable to dispersion-corrected hybrid DFT* and *approaches correlated wave-function methods*. - Why this matters for MCSP: lattice-energy ranking with ~0.5 kJ/mol accuracy is the unsolved problem in CSP. Current best practice is PBE+MBD or PBE+TS — empirical-dispersion-corrected GGAs/hybrids. ACMs offer a *non-empirical* alternative with comparable accuracy and a clear path to systematic improvement. If HFAC24 holds up on the CCDC blind-test targets, it's a real upgrade for the ranking step. - Authors not in clipping — the ACS landing-page clip didn't capture the author block. From the framework lineage and venue (JCTC), this is almost certainly the Della Sala / Fabiano / Constantin / Daas axis. Worth confirming via the DOI; flagged below. - The "systematically improvable" claim is the structural promise of MPAC-based methods: unlike empirical dispersion corrections, the framework allows you to add higher-order terms *without re-fitting parameters*. If true at the periodic limit, this is a methodologically clean route forward.

Summary

The paper takes the adiabatic connection (AC) formalism — the integral that connects the non-interacting Kohn–Sham reference state to the fully-interacting electronic system as a function of a coupling-strength parameter λ — and constructs interpolation-based models (ACMs) in the Møller–Plesset variant (MPAC) for periodic molecular crystals. ACMs use information from both the small-λ (perturbative MP2) regime and the large-λ (strong-interaction, Wₐ-style) limit to construct a closed-form expression for the correlation energy. Until now this had been demonstrated only for finite molecules. Lattice energies are computed for 19 molecular crystals and compared against: - periodic MP2 (the second-order Møller–Plesset benchmark); - high-level reference data (likely CCSD(T) / dispersion-corrected wave-function methods); - experimental lattice energies. Several ACMs are tested. The standout is HFAC24, a parameter-free post-Hartree–Fock correlation expression that: 1. correctly recovers the uniform electron gas (UEG) limit; 2. correctly recovers the strong-interaction limit (where electrons are localized); 3. is the *only* one of the tested ACMs accurate for *both* binding (lattice) energies and total energies — most ACMs sacrifice one for the other. Authors conclude that MPAC-based ACMs "provide an accurate and transferable framework for modeling molecular-crystal energetics" and represent a "robust, systematically improvable route" for correlation models in extended systems.

Notable quotes

> "We extend adiabatic connection models (ACMs) derived from the Møller–Plesset adiabatic connection (MPAC) formalism, previously applied only to finite systems, to periodic molecular crystals." — Abstract > "Among them, the HFAC24 model, which is a post-Hartree–Fock parameter-free correlation expression that correctly recovers both the uniform electron gas and strong-interaction limits, is the only one with accurate binding energies and accurate total energies." — Abstract

Open questions

- Authors and affiliations — the clipping lost them. Confirm by looking at the DOI page; likely the Della Sala / Fabiano / Constantin / Daas axis. - Which 19 molecular crystals? The benchmark set composition matters — a set heavy on rigid aromatics (e.g. X23) will say something different than one with flexible drug-like molecules. - Cost vs. PBE+MBD? The paper doesn't compare wall-clock cost. ACMs require both an MP2 calculation *and* a strong-interaction-limit estimate; even if accuracy matches MBD, runtime may not. - Has anyone tried HFAC24 on a CCDC blind-test target yet? Not yet (paper is fresh). - Implementation availability — TURBOMOLE V7.8 was used. Is this a publicly-available implementation or custom code?
