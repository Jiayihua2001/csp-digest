Is zeroth order crystal structure prediction (CSP_0) coming to maturity? What should we aim for in an ideal crystal structure prediction code?

Sarah L. Price · Faraday Discussions (2018) · 105 citations · doi:10.1039/c8fd00121a confidence: low ⚠ verification flagged claims
> A perspective/framing paper by Sarah Price (Faraday Discussions 2018) that assesses the maturity of lattice-energy-based crystal structure prediction, which she terms CSP_0 (zeroth-order CSP: searching for the global minimum in the static lattice energy). She proposes a conceptual hierarchy: CSP_0 (lattice-energy global minimum search), CSP_thd (predicting relative thermodynamic stability as a function of pressure, temperature, size, solvent, templates and impurities), and CSP_aim (what an ideal CSP code should ultimately deliver, including kinetically accessible and metastable forms). The paper argues the basic thermodynamic hypothesis behind CSP needs scrutiny because many important materials persist or form when they are not the most thermodynamically stable structure. Introduces a terminology/framework (CSP_0, CSP_thd, CSP_aim) for describing the current state and future goals of crystal structure prediction, and articulates the gap between global lattice-energy minimization and what would be needed for a truly predictive, application-relevant CSP code.

Key results

- No quantitative results are reported in the available abstract/metadata. - Framework defined qualitatively: CSP_0 = global minimum of lattice energy; CSP_thd = relative thermodynamic stability vs pressure, temperature, size, solvent, templates, impurities; CSP_aim = ideal code capturing outcomes even when the observed form is not the thermodynamically most stable.

Limitations

- Digest is based on abstract/metadata only; no full text was available. - As a perspective piece it presents conceptual framing rather than benchmarked methods or numerical results. - Predates modern MLIP-based CSP workflows (MACE-OFF, AIMNet2, UMA) so does not address them.

Takeaways

- Useful vocabulary for GAtor discussions: distinguish pure lattice-energy ranking (CSP_0) from finite-temperature/thermodynamic ranking (CSP_thd) and from the aspirational ideal (CSP_aim). - Reinforces that global lattice-energy minimum is not always the observed polymorph, motivating finite-temperature free-energy and kinetic considerations in ranking. - Frames why dispersion-inclusive energies alone are insufficient and why solvent, size, and templating effects matter for real crystallization outcomes.

Relevance to GAtor 2.0

Directly relevant conceptually: it names the exact assumption underlying GA-based lattice-energy CSP (that GAtor performs CSP_0) and argues for moving toward finite-temperature/thermodynamic ranking (CSP_thd), which connects to the group's interest in finite-temperature stability and dispersion-inclusive DFT ranking. It provides framing language rather than methods or numbers.
