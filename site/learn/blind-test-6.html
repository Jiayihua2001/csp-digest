Report on the sixth blind test of organic crystal structure prediction methods

Anthony M. Reilly, Richard I. Cooper, Claire S. Adjiman, Saswata Bhattacharya, A. Daniel Boese, Jan Gerit Brandenburg · Acta Crystallographica Section B Structural Science Crystal Engineering and Materials (2016) · 606 citations · doi:10.1107/s2052520616007447 confidence: high ⚠ verification flagged claims
> This paper reports the sixth CCDC blind test of organic crystal-structure prediction (CSP), the largest to date with 25 submissions (7 full, 14 partial, 4 re-ranking). Five target systems were used: XXII (a nearly rigid, hinged molecule), XXIII (a polymorphic former drug candidate with 5 known forms including two Z'=2), XXIV (a three-component chloride salt hydrate), XXV (a Troeger's base + 3,5-dinitrobenzoic acid co-crystal later found to have proton disorder), and XXVI (a bulky flexible molecule, the largest attempted in a blind test). The format changed: participants could submit up to 100 ranked structures per list plus a second re-ranked list of 100, reflecting a shift from predicting 'the' structure toward exploring solid-form landscapes. All targets except the potentially disordered Z'=2 form E of XXIII were generated by at least one submission, and one method (Neumann, Kendrick, Leusen; Monte Carlo parallel tempering + DFT) generated all of them except XXIII-E. The report documents growing use of dispersion-inclusive DFT for ranking, hierarchical workflows, free-energy corrections, and larger compute budgets. Contribution

Provides a community benchmark of the state of the art in organic CSP circa 2015-2016, documenting methodological trends (rise of vdW-inclusive DFT, hierarchical ranking, free-energy corrections, larger/more flexible and multi-component targets) and analyzing per-target successes and failures across a broad set of methods, including for the first time salt-hydrate and large flexible-molecule targets.

Method

Blind test: participants received 2-D chemical diagrams and crystallisation conditions for five unpublished target structures (chosen by an external referee so the CCDC could participate). Predictions were compared to experiment with the Crystal Packing Similarity Tool (Chisholm & Motherwell) via Mercury 3.6 / CSD Python API: heavy-atom clusters of 20 molecules, matched if distances agree within 25% and triangle angles within 25 degrees; a match required 20 of 20 molecules with RMSD typically below ~0.8 Angstrom. Special handling was used for XXIII (H-atom placement on carboxylic acid), XXIV (three components, N=20 then N=60), and XXV (analysed as both co-crystal and salt after post-test discovery of proton disorder). CSP methods spanned random/quasi-random (Sobol') search, Monte Carlo simulated annealing, parallel tempering, grid searches, and genetic/evolutionary algorithms, with ranking by empirical/tailor-made potentials, distributed multipoles+exp-6, SAPT(DFT)-fitted potentials, and vdW-inclusive DFAs (PBE, TPSS, BLYP with D2/D3/TS/XDM/MBD/Neumann-Perrin, plus vdW-DF and optB86b-vdW); some added harmonic/anharmonic lattice-dynamics free energies, MD, or kinetic Monte Carlo nucleation.

Key results

- 25 total submissions (7 full, 14 partial, 4 re-ranking), versus 15 in the fifth blind test. - XXII: experimental structure generated and ranked in top 100 by 12 of 21 submissions; all but one ranked it within the top 8, and four ranked it number 1. Successful predictions ranged from <200 CPU hours (simple potentials) to 10,000-30,000 CPU hours; some full DFT/ab initio submissions failed despite far more resources. - XXIII (Z'=1 forms): 14 attempted predictions. Form A generated 4 times (best rank 23, Day et al.), form B 10 times (best rank 1, Price et al.), form D 3 times (best rank 2). Two methods generated all three Z'=1 forms. Form C (Z'=2) predicted once (rank 6, Neumann/Kendrick/Leusen); form E (Z'=2) predicted by no submission. - XXIV (salt hydrate): of 8 full submissions, only Neumann/Kendrick/Leusen generated the experimental structure, ranking it 2nd (PBE + Neumann-Perrin). Some post-test methods ranked it more than 20 kJ/mol above the global minimum. - XXV (co-crystal): 14 attempted; 5 groups generated it plus 2 re-ranking submissions found it; all predicted a co-crystal (no isostructural salt), worst rank 6, with three predictions ranking it number 1. Post-test 100 K data showed proton disorder (0.58 occupancy on carboxylic acid O, 0.42 on N). - XXVI (largest, flexible): 12 attempted, 3 methods generated form 1 (Elking & Fusti-Molnar, Neumann/Kendrick/Leusen, Price et al.), all ranking it number 1 in at least one list; Elking & Fusti-Molnar improved rank from 8 (empirical) to 1 with PBE+XDM. - Aggregate: 36 of 70 predictions (for Z'=1 structures) generated the experimental structure, versus ~24 of 68 in the fifth blind test (noting different success criteria). - Free-energy and MD corrections mattered: for XXII, MD RMSD20 was 0.187 Angstrom at 300 K and 0.140 Angstrom at the true 150 K experimental temperature, smaller than submitted 0 K structures; Helmholtz free energies (300 K) improved rankings for several submissions and reordered XXIII B vs D. - Compute: 13 submissions used more than 100,000 CPU hours (versus 4 in the fifth test); several full submissions exceeded 500,000 CPU hours. Solid-state DFT was used by 12 submissions, up from 2 in the fifth blind test. - Reported DFT/vdW lattice-energy accuracy cited as 3-4 kJ/mol for absolute lattice energies, with best quantum-chemical benzene lattice energy accurate to ~1 kJ/mol, both potentially insufficient to resolve close polymorphs.

Limitations

- Ranking remains the central unsolved problem: no single method consistently ranked all experimental structures; lattice-energy differences may rely on fortuitous error cancellation given 3-4 kJ/mol accuracies. - Most methods rank on static 0 K lattice energies; anharmonic vibrations, thermal expansion, configurational disorder, and pressure effects are largely neglected and their role in polymorph free-energy differences is poorly understood. - Kinetics of nucleation/growth were essentially ignored (only one submission used kinetic Monte Carlo), and no submission used the supplied crystallisation conditions. - Structure generation failed in cases due to rigid-conformation searches or insufficiently exhaustive flexible searches (notably XXVI) and assumptions limiting the search space. - The proton disorder in XXV was unknown at submission, so protonation state was fixed and salt/co-crystal ambiguity was not explored by most methods. - Direct comparison of computational cost is confounded by heterogeneous hardware/architectures (data not normalised); volume of data precludes exhaustive per-submission analysis. - Some higher-ranked predicted structures may correspond to real but not-yet-screened polymorphs, complicating what counts as a ranking 'failure'.

Takeaways

- Genetic/evolutionary algorithms were used by three submissions (8, 12, 21); GAtor-relevant lesson is that dispersion-inclusive DFT ranking (PBE+TS, PBE+MBD, PBE-D2, vdW-DF) can dramatically reorder GA output, so the final ranking functional matters as much as the search. - The single method that generated nearly every target combined Monte Carlo parallel tempering search with DFT ranking (Neumann/Kendrick/Leusen), underscoring value of tailor-made DFT-fitted potentials plus a strong ranking stage. - Multi-component and Z'>1 targets (XXIV salt hydrate, XXIII/C,E co-crystal and Z'=2) are the hardest: only one method predicted XXIV, and Z'=2 forms were rarely or never generated, directly relevant to Z'>1 goals in GAtor 2.0. - Finite-temperature effects are quantitatively significant: MD at the correct temperature reduced XXII RMSD to 0.140 Angstrom and free energies changed polymorph rank order, motivating entropy/anharmonic and MLIP-based finite-T workflows. - PBE+MBD and TPSS-D3 were highlighted (including in re-ranking by Tkatchenko et al. and post-test analysis) as likely to improve rankings, consistent with using PBE0+MBD in a Marom-group pipeline. - Cheap potentials sometimes beat expensive DFT for generation (XXII predicted with <200 CPU hours), suggesting hierarchical screening with a cheap surrogate (e.g. MLIP) before DFT is a sensible cost strategy.

Relevance to GAtor 2.0

Directly central: this is a CSP blind-test report that benchmarks the exact task GAtor performs. Submissions 8, 12, and 21 used genetic/evolutionary algorithms, and Marom's group (Curtis, Li, Marom et al., Submission 12) participated with a GA plus PBE+TS/PBE+MBD ranking and a modified Harris-approximation coarse DFT step. The paper's emphasis on dispersion-inclusive DFT ranking (PBE+MBD, TPSS-D3), multi-component and Z'>1 targets, finite-temperature/free-energy corrections, and hierarchical workflows maps onto GAtor 2.0 priorities around ranking accuracy, niching/diversity in generation, and extension to salts/hydrates and larger flexible molecules.
