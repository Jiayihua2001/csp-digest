Current approaches to predicting molecular organic crystal structures

> A 2011 review by Graeme M. Day surveying computational methods for predicting molecular organic crystal structures, ideally from just the molecular structural formula. The article focuses on the dominant global lattice energy minimization paradigm, discussing progress, current limitations, and results from the CCDC blind tests. Contribution

Provides a review and synthesis of the state of molecular organic crystal structure prediction circa 2011, emphasizing global lattice energy minimization and using the community blind tests as an objective benchmark of field progress.

Method

Literature review and synthesis; no new experiments or calculations reported in the abstract. Discusses methods centered on global lattice energy minimization and references blind test results.

Limitations

- Digest based on abstract/metadata only; full text not available. - As a 2011 review, it predates modern MLIPs (MACE-OFF, AIMNet2, UMA) and later blind tests and DFT dispersion methods now central to the field. - Specific methods, energy rankings, and blind test statistics discussed cannot be confirmed from the abstract alone.

Takeaways

- Global lattice energy minimization was the dominant CSP approach as of 2011, motivating the search and ranking pipeline that GA-based methods aim to improve. - The abstract flags the central tension of CSP: reliable ranking of nearly isoenergetic candidate structures remains a limiting factor. - Blind tests are framed as the objective yardstick for monitoring progress, relevant context for benchmarking GAtor. - Useful as historical/background reference on lattice energy ranking; not a source of current numerical benchmarks.

Relevance to GAtor 2.0

Relevant as background on the lattice-energy-minimization framework that GAtor's genetic algorithm operates within, and on the CSP blind tests used to evaluate methods. However, being a 2011 review, it predates GAtor, Genarris, dispersion-inclusive DFT workflows (PBE0+MBD), and MLIPs, so it is primarily contextual/historical rather than methodologically current.
