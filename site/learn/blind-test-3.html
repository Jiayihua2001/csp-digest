A third blind test of crystal structure prediction

Graeme M. Day, W.D.S. Motherwell, Herman L. Ammon, S. X. M. Boerrigter, Raffaele Guido Della Valle, Elisabetta Venuti · Acta Crystallographica Section B Structural Science (2005) · 435 citations · doi:10.1107/s0108768105016563 confidence: low ⚠ verification flagged claims
> This paper reports the third CSP blind test (CSP2004) organized by the Cambridge Crystallographic Data Centre, with 18 participating groups predicting crystal structures for three molecules from molecular diagrams only. Unlike prior tests, no space-group restriction was imposed and Z'=2 structures were allowed. Participants submitted three predictions per molecule; success rates were lower than in CSP1999 and CSP2001, with only one successful prediction across the three blind molecules. The authors conclude that better energy models describing conformational and packing energies simultaneously, and better search procedures for Z'>1 and flexible molecules, are needed. Contribution

Provides the third community-wide blind assessment of CSP methods, expanding scope to unrestricted space groups and Z'=2 structures, and documenting the low success rates that motivate improvements in energy ranking and search for multi-molecule/flexible cases.

Method

Collaborative blind test: participants received molecular diagrams for three molecules (small rigid with common atom types; plus a flexible molecule) and submitted up to three ranked predictions each. Most methods were based on global minimization of the lattice energy. Details of individual methods not available from abstract. One target had to be replaced part-way through because a partial structure report was discovered, breaking its blind status.

Key results

- 18 research groups participated - Only one successful prediction was made across all three blind molecules (within the three submissions allowed) - No success in predicting the structure of the flexible molecule, as in CSP2001 - Success rates were lower than in the two previous blind tests (CSP1999, CSP2001) - The 'simplest' rigid molecule crystallized with Z'=2 (two molecules in the asymmetric unit), partly explaining the lack of success - One original target molecule had to be replaced because a partial structure report was found, disqualifying it as blind

Limitations

- Digest based on abstract/metadata only; no full text reviewed - Individual group methods, molecule identities, and quantitative per-method statistics not available from abstract - Energy models of the era (pre dispersion-inclusive DFT / MBD) not described; abstract only notes the general need for better energy models - Realized polymorphs also governed by nucleation and crystal growth processes noted as insufficiently understood

Takeaways

- Z'>1 (multiple independent molecules) was a major failure mode even for the 'simplest' rigid molecule - directly relevant to GAtor Z'>1 work - Conformational flexibility remained unsolved, underscoring the need for energy models capturing conformational and packing energies simultaneously - Unrestricted space-group search plus Z'=2 dramatically increased difficulty relative to earlier restricted tests - Establishes historical baseline (CSP2004) against which modern MLIP + PBE0+MBD ranking pipelines can be contrasted

Relevance to GAtor 2.0

Directly relevant historical context: this early blind test exposed the two problems central to GAtor 2.0 development - Z'>1 structure generation/search and simultaneous conformational+packing energy accuracy. It motivates modern dispersion-inclusive DFT ranking and MLIP-based sampling. Useful as a baseline citation on CSP blind test evolution and why niching/search for multi-molecule crystals matters.
