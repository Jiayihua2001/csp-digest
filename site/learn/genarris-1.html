Genarris: Random generation of molecular crystal structures and fast screening with a Harris approximation

Xiayue Li, Farren S. Curtis, Timothy Rose, Christoph Schober, Alvaro Vazquez-Mayagoitia, Karsten Reuter · The Journal of Chemical Physics (2018) · 27 citations · doi:10.1063/1.5014038 our group confidence: high ⚠ verification flagged claims
> Genarris is a Python package for configuration-space screening of molecular crystals of rigid molecules. It generates random structures with physical constraints (symmetry, unit cell parameters, intermolecular close contacts) and screens them rapidly using a Harris approximation (HA), where the total crystal density is built by superposition of self-consistent single-molecule densities and DFT total energy is evaluated without a self-consistency cycle. The HA is implemented in FHI-aims using modified Wigner matrices to rotate NAO basis functions. Genarris introduces a relative coordinate descriptor (RCD) based on relative positions and orientations of neighboring molecules, and uses affinity propagation (AP) clustering for structure selection. Three workflows (Rigorous, Energy, Diverse) apply successive clustering/selection steps. The Diverse workflow is recommended for generating GA initial populations. Demonstrated on three past CSP blind test targets (II, XIII, XXII). Corpus: tier B - `genarris-1` - source mode: pdf - confidence: high - FLAGGED - see below

Contribution

First use of the Harris approximation for molecular crystal configuration-space screening (fragments = whole molecules), a new relative coordinate descriptor (RCD) tailored to capture molecular crystal packing motifs, and three standardized clustering/selection workflows packaged for generating diverse or low-energy structure pools (notably as GA seed populations for GAtor).

Method

Single molecule 3D coords optimized with PBE+TS (tight/tier 2 in FHI-aims). Unit cell volume estimated from 10 trial relaxations; random structures generated with user-defined space groups (Bilbao definitions), half-normal volume sampling, fuzzy sr close-contact criterion (fraction of sum of vdW radii). Molecule placed with uniform random orientation (unit-sphere axis) and random COM; closeness checks reject unphysical contacts. Fast energy via PBE+TS@Harris (Gamma-point, rigid molecules). Clustering via RCD (N=16 neighbors, orientation/position weighting k=1, M=8 matched entries) fed as distance matrix to affinity propagation (scikit-learn), tuned by preference to hit target silhouette scores. Validated HA on dimer binding-energy curves and on ranking of raw pools; compared RCD vs RDF and AP vs k-means. Final pools used as GAtor GA initial populations.

Key results

- HA dimer binding-energy error at equilibrium |dBE(xeq)| = 0.0740, 0.0049, 0.0288 eV for Targets II, XIII, XXII respectively; HA is non-variational so dBE(x) always negative and grows with decreasing distance. - For Target XXII raw pools (2000 P21/n structures), HA vs self-consistent PBE+TS binding-energy r^2 = 0.946, 0.960, 0.994 for sr=0.500, 0.625, 0.750; ranking r^2 = 0.976, 0.989, 0.979. Optimal at sr=0.625; sr=0.500 has unphysical N...N contacts (1.64, 1.80, 1.56 A). - RCD-based AP clustering outperformed RDF: grouped nearly all 69 duplicate pairs (Target II) up to 7 clusters, stronger correlation with space group, weaker with unit cell volume, and consistently higher silhouette scores that increase with cluster number. - AP clustering resolves isolated/under-sampled satellite clusters that k-means merges; identified a distinct low-a-parameter cluster (within 0.2 eV of global minimum) for Target XXII. - Rigorous workflow found the experimental structure for all three targets; Energy workflow yielded more duplicates (26, 4, 28 dup pairs for II, XIII, XXII) vs Rigorous (1, 7, 0); Diverse gave most uniform sampling. Table II reports unique structures and average RCD differences per workflow. - GA seeded from Diverse pool gave best performance, consistently finding experimental structures (#7 for II, #1 for XIII and XXII); Energy pool inconsistent; Random pool worst. - Raw pools: 5000 structures for Targets II and XIII, 10000 for Target XXII (conformational flexibility); all constrained to non-chiral space groups, Z=4, Z'=1; final pools 1% of raw (50, 50, 100).

Limitations

- Restricted to rigid molecules and Gamma-point calculations only; flexible molecules with bond-rotational degrees of freedom listed as future work. - HA fails when molecules are unphysically close (strong density overlap/repulsion not captured), requiring careful sr choice; HA accuracy also degrades when structures are very close in energy (e.g. sr=0.750). - HA does not capture hydrogen bonding, pi-pi, halogen dipole, or dipole-dipole density changes at close contact. - RCD clustering breaks down when the pool becomes too diverse (below ~5% of raw pool), forcing a purely energy-based final selection in the Rigorous workflow. - RCD requires user-defined reference axes (two atoms per molecule), introducing manual input; Z'=1 and Z=4 used throughout demonstrations. - GA runs were not exhaustive searches, limiting strength of GA-performance conclusions; Target XIII was an exception to duplicate trends (attributed to halogen bonds). - PBE+TS used for screening/ranking (not the more accurate PBE0+MBD, which is only used post-hoc via Ref 48 for representative-structure indexing).

Takeaways

- For GA seeding use the Genarris Diverse workflow (HA + RCD-AP clustering, exemplar selection) - it gives the most uniform coverage and best GA convergence to diverse low-energy motifs. - RCD is a packing-motif-sensitive descriptor (sensitive to space group, insensitive to volume) that beats RDF for de-duplication and cluster resolution; consider it or its successors for GAtor 2.0 niching/similarity. - Affinity propagation is preferable to k-means for preserving under-sampled/isolated clusters in configuration space - directly relevant to maintaining diversity/niching in GA populations. - The Harris approximation is a parameter-free, force-field-free fast pre-screen that ranks structures accurately (ranking r^2 ~0.98) as long as close contacts are excluded via a sensible sr; the sr sweet spot (~0.625) balances accuracy and diversity. - Enforce a minimum inter-atomic distance during generation to keep HA reliable; unphysical close contacts (e.g. N...N ~1.6 A) produce large negative HA energy errors and outliers.

Relevance to GAtor 2.0

This is the foundational Genarris (v1) paper from the Marom group and is the structure-generation front end for GAtor. It directly informs GAtor 2.0 on initial population generation (Diverse workflow recommended for GA seeding), the RCD descriptor and AP-clustering-based niching/diversity maintenance, fast HA-based pre-ranking to reduce DFT cost, and physical-constraint sampling with space groups/Wyckoff positions. Limitations (rigid molecules, Z'=1, Gamma-point, PBE+TS) mark exactly what later work and MLIPs must extend for flexible, Z'>1, multi-component crystals and finite-temperature stability.
