Ab initio random structure searching

Chris J. Pickard, R. J. Needs · Journal of Physics Condensed Matter (2011) · 1253 citations · doi:10.1088/0953-8984/23/5/053201 confidence: low ⚠ verification flagged claims
> This review paper by Pickard and Needs describes ab initio random structure searching (AIRSS), a method for predicting stable atomic arrangements of materials using first-principles electronic structure methods such as DFT. The central idea is to generate many random initial structures, relax them to local energy minima with DFT, and identify low-energy structures. The paper reviews applications across solids, point defects, surfaces, and clusters, and presents new results for iron clusters on graphene, silicon clusters, polymeric nitrogen, hydrogen-rich lithium hydrides, and boron. Contribution

Establishes and reviews AIRSS as a simple, general, and powerful DFT-based structure prediction approach, demonstrating its breadth across bulk solids, defects, surfaces, and clusters.

Method

Ab initio random structure searching: generate random initial structures (with sensible constraints such as reasonable volumes and minimum interatomic distances, per the general AIRSS methodology) and relax each to a local minimum using DFT, then rank by energy. Takeaways

- AIRSS is a foundational, widely cited structure-search paradigm that contrasts with genetic-algorithm CSP by using random generation plus local relaxation rather than evolutionary operators. - The random-generation-with-constraints philosophy is directly relevant to structure generation strategies (e.g., Genarris-style random initial pools). - Demonstrated generality across solids, defects, surfaces, and clusters shows the approach transfers across problem classes.

Relevance to GAtor 2.0

AIRSS is a canonical baseline and philosophical counterpoint to genetic-algorithm CSP as used in GAtor.
