MACE-OFF: Short-Range Transferable Machine Learning Force Fields for Organic Molecules

Dávid Péter Kovács, J. Harry Moore, Nicholas J. Browning, Ilyes Batatia, Joshua T. Horton, Yixuan Pu · Journal of the American Chemical Society (2025) · 188 citations · doi:10.1021/jacs.4c07099 confidence: low ⚠ verification flagged claims
> MACE-OFF is a series of short-range transferable machine learning force fields for organic molecules, built on the MACE equivariant message-passing architecture and trained on first-principles reference data computed at a high level of quantum mechanical theory. The abstract reports that the models accurately predict a range of gas- and condensed-phase properties, including dihedral torsion scans of unseen molecules, molecular crystals, liquids (with quantum nuclear effects), solvation free energy surfaces, peptide folding, and nanosecond simulations of a solvated protein. No numerical results are available from the metadata/abstract alone. Corpus: tier G - `mace-off` - source mode: metadata - confidence: low - FLAGGED - see below

Contribution

Introduces MACE-OFF, a transferable short-range MLIP for organic molecules trained on high-level QM reference data, aiming to replace classical empirical force fields with first-principles accuracy at relatively low cost across gas- and condensed-phase applications.

Method

MACE equivariant message-passing neural network potential (based on abstract description as short-range ML force field); trained on first-principles reference data at a high level of quantum theory. Specific architecture details, training set composition, DFT/QM level, and dispersion treatment are not available from the abstract.

Key results

- Abstract only - no quantitative results reported in the provided material - Claims accurate dihedral torsion scans of unseen molecules (no numbers) - Claims reliable description of molecular crystals and liquids including quantum nuclear effects (no numbers) - Demonstrates free energy surfaces in explicit solvent, peptide folding dynamics, and nanosecond simulations of a fully solvated protein (no numbers)

Limitations

- Digest based on abstract/metadata only; no quantitative benchmarks, error metrics, or training details available - Model is explicitly short-range, so long-range electrostatics and dispersion handling from the abstract is unclear - Transferability limits, element coverage, and applicability to Z'>1 or multi-component crystals not stated in abstract

Takeaways

- A transferable organic-molecule MLIP that claims molecular-crystal accuracy is directly relevant as a candidate ranking/relaxation engine for GA CSP - Short-range design means dispersion/long-range treatment needs verification before use in lattice-energy ranking against PBE0+MBD - Full text required to assess accuracy on molecular crystals and whether it complements MACE-OFF vs AIMNet2/UMA for CSP workflows

Relevance to GAtor 2.0

MACE-OFF is a transferable MLIP in the same family the group tracks (MACE-OFF, AIMNet2, UMA) and explicitly claims molecular-crystal capability, making it a potential fast relaxation and pre-ranking tool in the GAtor 2.0 pipeline before dispersion-inclusive DFT. Relevance to lattice-energy ranking accuracy cannot be confirmed from the abstract, and its short-range nature warrants checking against MBD-level references.
